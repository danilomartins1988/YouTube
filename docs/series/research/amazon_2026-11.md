**Reverse-DCF illustration (our calculation; not a valuation).**
Formula: Market cap = Σ_{t=1..10} F·(1+g)^t/(1+r)^t + [F·(1+g)^10·(1+g_T)/(r−g_T)]/(1+r)^10. We solve for g. Inputs: market cap $2.929T (2026-11-10), r = 8% or 10%, g_T = 3%, 10 years. Equity value is used as is, with no net-debt or investment adjustment.

| Base cash flow F | r = 8% | r = 10% |
|---|---|---|
| TTM FCF −$42.4B | **cannot be solved**: a negative base has no implied growth rate | n/a |
| TTM operating cash flow $168.3B (**used because capex has pushed FCF below zero**) | **g ≈ 0.9%/yr** (year 10 ≈ $183B) | **g ≈ 5.3%/yr** (year 10 ≈ $281B) |

How to say it: the OCF version assumes Amazon could stop all capex, including maintenance, which it cannot. The low 1–5% therefore understates what the price really requires. The honest framing: "Before investment, today's cash flow would support the price with modest growth. After investment, there is no free cash yet. The price is a bet that the $200B-plus a year of capex turns into free cash flow."

## 6. What has to happen (tied to filing numbers)
1. **AWS backlog converts.** Commitments "approximately **$578 billion** as of September 30, 2026", with a "weighted-average remaining life of … 6.1 years" [F]. That is up from about **$244B** (4.1 years) at 2025-12-31 [F, 10-K], or +137% [C]. AWS TTM sales are $161.4B. CEO target: over 40% growth in 2027 and 2028 [F, claim].
2. **AWS margins hold while depreciation ramps.** AWS margin was 38.7% in Q3 and 37.8% TTM [F]. TTM depreciation and amortization was $79.4B, vs $61.9B a year earlier [F]. Property and equipment, net, rose from $357.0B to $508.9B in nine months [F].
3. **Retail margins keep expanding.** North America TTM margin rose from 6.6% to 8.2% and International from 3.2% to 3.3% [F]. Q4 OI guide of $28.5–33.5B vs $25.0B a year ago [F].
4. **Capex peaks and FCF turns positive.** About $220B of cash capex in 2026, "meaningfully" more in 2027 [F]. FCF was −$42.4B TTM, with long-term debt up $112B in nine months [C].

## 7. What could go wrong
- **Data-center capacity risk (10-Q risk factor):** "Failures to adequately predict customer demand … or otherwise optimize and operate our fulfillment network and data centers successfully from time to time result in excess or insufficient fulfillment or data center capacity, service interruptions, increased costs, and impairment charges" [F].
- **Return on new technology (10-Q):** "profitability or other intended benefits, if any, in our newer activities (including development and adoption of automation, artificial intelligence, and machine learning technologies …), may not meet our expectations, and we may not be successful enough in these newer activities to recoup our investments in them" [F].
- **Chip supply (10-Q):** "we rely on a limited group of suppliers for semiconductor products, including products related to artificial intelligence infrastructure such as graphics processing units" [F]. The guidance cites "resource and supply volatility, including for memory chips" [F].
- **Concentration and circularity in AI deals.** Amazon is both investor and supplier:
  - its OpenAI preferred stock is carried at $50.0B
  - its Anthropic preferred stock is carried at $92.5B, and its Anthropic convertible notes at a $146.2B fair value
  - a further $20.0B Anthropic financing facility is unlocked by compute-delivery milestones [F, 10-Q Note 2]
  - total equity, warrant and convertible investments: "$298.8 billion" [F]
  - 10-Q: "Valuations of private companies are inherently more complex … we believe that market sensitivities are not practicable." [F]
- **Commitments:** total contractual commitments of **$883.6B**, including $164.9B of leases not yet commenced and $159.6B of property and equipment [F, 10-Q Note 4].
- **Regulation and litigation, with $ where disclosed [F]:**
  - **FTC Prime settlement:** a "$2.5 billion" charge in Q3 2025 [10-K].
  - **FTC + 22 state AGs, August 2026:** a new suit over "the use of reserve prices in Amazon's sponsored ad auctions", seeking "unspecified amounts of civil penalties, restitution, forfeiture, disgorgement". Texas filed a parallel suit. Advertising is $78.0B TTM [C].
  - **Antitrust cases since March 2020** (FTC, state AGs, private class actions; US, Canada, UK): they "seek billions of dollars of alleged damages, treble damages … structural relief". UK: "three class actions have been certified"; US: "one class action has been certified" [10-Q]. The filing gives no trial date and no loss estimate.
  - **Xockets ITC patent case** against AWS GB200 and GB300 servers, seeking injunctive relief [10-Q].
  - **Unrecognized tax contingencies:** about $7.3B [10-Q].
- **Earnings volatility:** Q2 2026 net income of $62.6B vs Q3 of $20.7B shows how much Anthropic marks swing GAAP EPS [F].

## 10. Flags
- **The capex arithmetic looks odd.** "Approximately $220 billion" for 2026 less 9M cash capex of $172.2B implies only about $48B in Q4, after $75.9B in Q3. Either the guide is conservative or rounded, or a definitional difference applies (cash capex net of incentives). **Check the call transcript before quoting an implied Q4 figure.**
- **The GAAP P/E is flattered by the Anthropic mark-ups.** Show the ex-non-operating ~37x only as our illustration with its assumed 21% tax rate.
- **Reverse DCF on FCF is impossible** (negative). The OCF version ignores capex and understates what the price assumes. Say both on screen.
- **Two AWS backlog expansions are named** (OpenAI +$100B, Anthropic >$100B), but their share of the $578B is **not disclosed**. Don't claim a percentage.
