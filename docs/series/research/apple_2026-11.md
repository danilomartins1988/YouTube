## 5. Guidance

- **Filed documents:** the 8-K release contains **no outlook**. Apple gives guidance only on the call.
- **Call guidance for the December quarter (Q1 FY27)**, as reported by MacRumors (Fetched, secondary; not verified against an official transcript):
  - "Apple expects revenue to grow 7 to 10 percent year over year, leading to the best quarter for revenue in Apple's history." Applied to Q1 FY26 revenue of $143,756M, that is roughly **$153.8B–$158.1B** (Computed).
  - "Services revenue is expected to grow between 8 and 9 percent."
  - "Gross margin is expected to be between 45 and 46 percent." The article attributes the pressure to memory costs and the Siri AI ramp.
  - The article says Apple "expects constraints on the Mac and iPad in the December quarter, primarily driven by memory."
  - CFO Kevan Parekh, quoted by MacRumors on Siri AI cloud cost: "The mix of [on-device and cloud Siri] is very early days, and so it's hard to predict exactly what" the costs will look like.
- Search snippets (not opened) also attribute 7–10% / 8–9% / 45–46% to Parekh and say the revenue range "reflects ... constrained supply" (Snippet).
- **The 10-K itself** says: "the Company believes, in general, gross margins will be subject to volatility and downward pressure." It also says the Company "expects these trends to intensify for memory and storage components."
- **Analyst consensus: not available** from any fetched page. One snippet says "analysts had expected about 47%" gross margin, unattributed. **Do not use it.**

## 6. What has to happen (tied to filed numbers)

1. **iPhone keeps compounding off a record base.** iPhone was $254.1B (+21%), 53% of sales. The Q4 FY26 comparison was +17.5%. Supply is the stated constraint: CEO John Ternus told Reuters Apple can't meet iPhone Duo demand (via MacRumors, secondary).
2. **Services growth re-accelerates or at least holds near 10%+.** It is 40% of gross profit at a 76.2% margin. Q4 slowed to +9.8% and the guide is 8–9%. The 10-K says FY26 growth came "primarily due to higher net sales from advertising and cloud services."
3. **Gross margin absorbs memory costs and AI inference.** It went from 48.8% in FY26 (helped by tariff refunds) to a 45–46% guide. R&D was already +32% to $45.8B, "primarily due to higher infrastructure-related costs, including investments in artificial intelligence".
4. **Greater China keeps its rebound.** It was +28% to $82.4B in FY26 (−4% in FY25). The 10-K credits iPhone and Mac plus a "favorable" renminbi.

## 7. What could go wrong (10-K quotes, Fetched)

- **Google search licensing:** "the Company earns revenue from licensing arrangements with Google LLC ... A reversal of the order on appeal could result in the imposition of certain remedies initially proposed by the DOJ, such as those prohibiting Google from offering the Company commercial terms for search distribution. If implemented, these remedies could materially adversely affect the Company's ability to earn revenue from such licensing arrangements." **No dollar amount is disclosed.**
- **App Store, U.S. (Epic):** "currently subject to a court-ordered injunction in the U.S. preventing it from imposing any commission or fee on purchases ... completed using external links". The Ninth Circuit (2025-12-11) said Apple "can charge some commission on link-out purchases". The Supreme Court granted review on 2026-06-30 "to review the applicable legal standard for civil contempt."
- **EU DMA:** fined **€500 million** (2025-04-23, under appeal). In the Article 6(4) investigation the Commission "may impose fines up to 10% of the Company's annual worldwide net sales". That is a statutory ceiling, not an estimate; 10% of FY26 sales is $47.9B (Computed). DMA interoperability "may in the future, cause the Company to not launch or maintain products, services and features, such as Siri AI, in certain jurisdictions."
- **DOJ antitrust suit** (filed 2024-03-21, D.N.J.) alleging monopolization of "performance smartphones" and "smartphones". It seeks equitable relief. Follow-on civil suits seek monetary damages. No amount is disclosed.
- **Tariffs:** "Tariffs and other measures that are applied to the Company's products or their components can have a material adverse impact". It also notes Section 301 tariffs and the pending Section 232 semiconductor measures. "If tariffs are subsequently rescinded or revoked, the Company may not be able to successfully claim and collect tariff refunds." FY26 margin included refunds after the Supreme Court IEEPA ruling of 2026-02-20, so that benefit may not repeat.
- **Memory and components:** "The Company is experiencing supply constraints and rising costs ... including advanced semiconductors, memory and storage. The Company expects these trends to intensify for memory and storage components."
- **AI competitiveness:** "Failure to successfully develop, deploy or differentiate artificial intelligence capabilities, or to do so as quickly or cost-effectively as competitors, could result in loss of market share". Compute dependency: "the Company relies on third-party cloud service providers ... There are limited third-party providers that can reliably meet such demand".
- **Concentration:** "the Company generates a significant portion of its net sales from a single product category". Manufacturing is concentrated in China mainland and Asia (quoted in §2).
- **Tax:** gross unrecognized tax benefits were $22.5B, of which $10.6B would affect the effective tax rate (auditor's critical audit matter). The 2024 EU State Aid charge was $10.2B net (history).
- Other: the 10-K says Q4 settlements "did not individually or in the aggregate have a material impact". Contingencies note: "there was not at least a reasonable possibility the Company may have incurred a material loss".

## 8. Upside the price may not include (company-attributed)

- **Siri AI and cloud services:** the 10-K says cloud services "will provide expanded access to Apple Intelligence™ and Siri® AI features." Per MacRumors, Apple plans usage limits on server-side AI with expanded access for users who need more, and "pricing and plan details have not been announced" (secondary). This is a possible new paid tier, but it is **unsized**. Do not size it.
- **New product categories in the FY26 lineup:** iPhone Duo (Ternus calls it "the most transformational iPhone since the original"), Apple HomeView ("smart home display"), MacBook Neo, and MacBook Pro with Touch. Revenue for each is not disclosed.
- **Installed base:** CFO in the release: "our installed base of active devices reached a new all-time high across all major product categories and geographic segments." No number is given.
- **Advertising:** named as a growth driver of Services; "advertising services include third-party licensing arrangements and the Company's own advertising platforms."
- **Supply easing:** per MacRumors (secondary), Apple said advanced-node constraints "are easing as more of the lineup moves to 2nm chips."

## 10. Flags

- **Guidance source:** the December-quarter guide is from a secondary article, not an Apple document. Verify against the official call audio or transcript before putting exact wording on screen.
- **Tariff refunds flatter FY26:** they inflated FY26 gross margin and Q4 EPS ($0.05). Say "including tariff refunds" whenever 48.8% or 47.9% is used.
- **Capex fell while AI spending rose.** Capex dropped from $12.7B to $9.0B while R&D "infrastructure-related costs" rose, and the 10-K says Apple relies on third-party cloud. FCF looks high partly because AI compute runs through opex, not capex. Do not frame Apple's low capex as "free AI".
- **OCF jump (+30%) is partly working capital** (liabilities +$13.7B; inventory −$8.9B). The FY25 base may be affected by the State Aid escrow; that is unverified, so do not explain the jump on air without checking.
- **Google licensing:** never give a dollar figure; it is not disclosed in the filings.
- **"Up to 10% of sales" DMA fine** is a legal maximum, not an expected cost.
- **"Europe" includes India**, so do not call it "European sales".
- Reverse DCF is an **illustration**: it ignores net cash and uses one year of FCF as the base. Say "our calculation, assumptions on screen".
- **What not to say:** no "fair value", "cheap/expensive", "buy/sell", or targets. Say "the price assumes ..." and never "Apple will ...".
