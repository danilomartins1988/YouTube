# Visual direction: How Google Actually Makes Money

Generated by `tools/visual_direction.py` from the script, then reviewed. The YAML twin (`projects/how-google-actually-makes-money/visual_direction.yaml`) is applied to the render: palette, scene tones, tone shifts, presenter demeanour and the music bed all follow it.

1. **Primary emotional tone:** strategy
2. **Secondary tones:** bright, trade, crisis, reflective
3. **Colour palette:** cool greys and blues, one accent per rival (episode palette: bg #E9EEF3, accent #E08A00, risk #D93A3A)
4. **Lighting and contrast:** clean, even, slight vignette
5. **Animation intensity:** measured, diagrammatic
6. **Character behaviour:** neutral: draws maps of rivals, decisions and consequences (demeanour `neutral`, at least 4 presenter sequences, only where they explain)
7. **Background design:** boardrooms, maps, company towers
8. **Charts and diagrams:** side-by-side comparisons, networks
9. **Transitions:** clean slides
10. **Music and sound:** C-major pad, light pulse; the bed changes with each chapter's tone and crossfades at turning points
11. **Key emotional turning points:**
    - chapter "The auction": strategy → bright
    - chapter "The toll booths": bright → strategy
    - chapter "The second screen": strategy → bright
    - chapter "The cloud turns": bright → trade
    - chapter "Where the cash goes": trade → strategy
    - chapter "The threat": strategy → tech
    - chapter "The lesson": tech → reflective
    - inside `m5_2026` at "flipped the waterfall": → crisis (capex overtakes operating cash; buybacks stop)
12. **Scene-specific requirements:**
    - No company logos, brand colours as logo, or trademarked art: generic search box, phone, TV and data centres
    - Sundar Pichai only as a respectful caricature where the narration names him; no other real person drawn (judge and executives unnamed or in text)
    - Apple figure attributed to the court as an estimate; illustrative auction bids labelled ILLUSTRATIVE; our calculations labelled
