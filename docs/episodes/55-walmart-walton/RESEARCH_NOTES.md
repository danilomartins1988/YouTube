# Ep 55 research notes: How One Family Still Controls Walmart

All pages read on 2026-11-13. Primary sources first; every number in the film maps to a claim id in

## The "largest family business" claim (how it is worded and why)

- **2025 EY and University of St. Gallen Global 500 Family Business Index** (familybusinessindex.com; EY page dated
  March 12, 2025), ranked by revenue: 1 Walmart (Walton) $648.13bn, 2 Volkswagen (Porsche/Piëch) $356.71bn,
  3 Schwarz Group $179.09bn, 4 Cargill $177.00bn, 5 Ford $176.19bn. Inclusion for listed companies: family voting
  control of at least 32%. Walmart's index revenue is fiscal 2024 (the latest at compilation).
- **Fortune** (June 3, 2026): "Amazon has dethroned Walmart as No. 1 on the Fortune 500, ending the retailer's
  13-year reign atop the list." Fortune Global 500 (July 28, 2026): Amazon No. 1, Walmart No. 2 ("ending Walmart's
  12-year run"). Amazon 2025 net sales $716,924M (10-K XBRL); Walmart fiscal 2026 revenue $713,163M (10-K). Gap ≈ $3.8bn
  (our calculation; different fiscal periods).
- **Wording used:** "the latest global ranking of family-controlled companies, compiled by EY and the University of St.
  Gallen, Walmart is number one" and, in the payoff, "the world's largest family business". This is supported by the
  index (ranked by revenue) and by Walmart's lead of ≈ 1.8× over No. 2 (our calculation). We do not claim Walmart is the
  world's biggest company (it is now No. 2 to Amazon by revenue, which the hook says).

## History (Walmart, Bloomberg)

- Walmart, "Sam Walton" page: 1950 the Waltons moved from Newport to Bentonville, where Sam opened Walton's 5&10 on the
  downtown square; first Walmart 1962 in Rogers, Arkansas, at age 44; went public 1970, proceeds financed expansion;
  died 1992. History timeline: 1970 "The first stock is sold at $16.50 per share"; 1972 NYSE; 1983 first Sam's Club;
  1992 Rob Walton chairman; 2015 Greg Penner succeeds him. 10-K Item 1: business began 1945 (Ben Franklin store, Newport).
- **1953 partnership.** Bloomberg (Sept 13, 2013, republished by Accounting Today), citing *Made in America*: Sam began
  arranging his affairs to avoid a potential estate tax bill in 1953, when his oldest child was 9; each child got a 20%
  stake and Sam and his wife kept 20%; quote: "The best way to reduce paying estate taxes is to give your assets away
  before they appreciate." The Waltons have held their stake in a family partnership or similar structure since 1953.
  Secondary source quoting the autobiography: attributed on screen and in narration. The name "Walton Enterprises" for
  the 1953 entity is NOT asserted for 1953 (only for today, per the proxy).
- Not used: the "300,000 shares" IPO size (only secondary sources); "$9 billion in tax savings" (Substack, unsourced).

## Today's stake (2026 proxy, Schedule 13D, Forms 4)

- DEF 14A (April 23, 2026), holders > 5% as of April 10, 2026 (7,970,990,515 shares outstanding): Walton Enterprises,
  LLC 3,516,197,849 shares = 44.11% (3,002,673,393 held directly + 513,524,456 held by the Walton Family Holdings Trust,
  voted by Walton Enterprises under an irrevocable proxy); the trust alone 6.44%. "Approximately 44% of our company's
  Shares are held by entities related to the family of Sam Walton." Managing members: WELLCO Mgmt Trusts #1-#4,
  majority vote.
- Schedule 13D (Dec 19, 2024): on Dec 18, 2024 Rob, Jim and Alice Walton's managing interests moved, for no
  consideration, into WELLCO Mgmt Trusts #1-#3 (the John T. Walton Estate Trust became #4); eight family members became
  additional trustees of the holdings trust; the trust granted the irrevocable proxy. Holdings then: 3,002,673,393 and
  603,989,702. Purpose: distributions to beneficiaries and sales "to meet investment, personal, and charitable
  objectives". Relationships: Rob is the father of Carrie Walton Penner; Jim is the father of Steuart Walton.
- 13D/A (Mar 3, 2026), Ex. 6: Walton Enterprises 3,523,409,231 (44.21%) incl. trust 520,735,838 (6.53%); individual
  holdings (Alice 20.2M, Jim 31.5M, Rob 7.0M...) not used in the film.
- **Forms 4, Walton Family Holdings Trust (36 filings, transactions Dec 20, 2024 - Jun 24, 2026):** our tally of all
  non-derivative lines: open-market sales 69,040,950 shares, Σ shares × reported weighted-average price ≈ $7.145bn;
  distributions to beneficiaries (code J, "for no consideration") 35,113,000 shares; holding after June 24, 2026:
  499,835,752. No form checks the Rule 10b5-1 box (so the film does NOT say the family sells under 10b5-1 plans;
  Walmart's 10b5-1 8-Ks in 2024-2026 concern executives such as McMillon, Furner, Rainey).
- 3-for-1 split effective Feb 23, 2024 (8-K).

## Our calculations (labelled on screen)

- Two family entities after June 24, 2026: 3,002,673,393 + 499,835,752 = 3,502,509,145 shares.
- Value at the Nov 12, 2026 close of $126.19 (market data): ≈ $442.0bn. Walmart market value: 7,933,746,241 shares
  (10-Q cover, Aug 26, 2026) × $126.19 ≈ $1.00 trillion.
- Dividend: fiscal 2027 annual dividend $0.99 (10-Q; $0.2475 quarterly). 3,502,509,145 × $0.99 ≈ $3.47bn a year
  (≈ $289M a month), gross, before taxes. On the proxy count it is ≈ $3.48bn.
- Vote weight: 3,516,197,849 / 7,164,547,083 shares represented at the June 4, 2026 meeting (8-K) ≈ 49.1%.
- Walmart vs Volkswagen in the index: 648.13 / 356.71 ≈ 1.82×.

## The business (10-K fiscal 2026, Q4 FY26 and Q2 FY27 releases)

- Total revenues $713,163M; net sales $706,413M; operating income $29,825M (4.2% of net sales); net income attributable

  carriers; "everyday low prices, supported by everyday low costs"; pricing strategies include absorbing cost increases.

- Q4 FY26 release: global advertising grew 46% to nearly $6.4bn, including VIZIO. Q2 FY27 (quarter ended Jul 31, 2026):
  revenue $187.9bn (+5.9%); eCommerce +23%; advertising +38%; membership fee revenue +17%; results include tariff refunds.

## Governance

- 2026 proxy: 8 of 11 nominees independent; "only two members of the Walton family currently serve as non-management
  Board members" (Greg Penner, Steuart Walton); Penner's spouse is Steuart's cousin. 2024 proxy (Penner's letter): "Rob
  Walton, my father-in-law" will not seek re-election; Rob did "most of the work to take us public" in 1970 (as Sam
  remembered); on the board since 1978; chairman 1992-2015, sales ≈ $44bn → $482bn.
- 8-K Nov 14, 2025: John Furner CEO from Feb 1, 2026; "joined the Company as an hourly associate in 1993". Doug
  McMillon retired as CEO Jan 31, 2026 (not named in the film).
- 2026 meeting (8-K): cumulative-voting shareholder proposal 122,306,285 for, 6,390,550,159 against. The proposal text
  (proxy) says concentrated ownership "limits the ability of non-controlling shareholders to influence board
  composition"; its "roughly half" claim is the proponent's and is not used.

## Estate-planning tools (only what is documented)

- **Walton v. Commissioner, 115 T.C. 589 (Dec 22, 2000)**, Docket 3824-99: petitioner Audrey J. Walton; two GRATs created
  in 1993; deficiency $4,532,776.82 in 1993 gift tax; the court ruled for the taxpayer on the section 2702 valuation.
  Bloomberg (2013): she put $200 million of Wal-Mart stock into the GRATs, a member of the family of Sam's brother Bud.
  Audrey Walton is never drawn (documents only).
- Form 144 (June 3, 2026): a trust named "CRW 2024 WMT GRAT" gave notice of a proposed sale of 86,000 shares. Initials
  not decoded on air.
- Charitable lead annuity trusts: Bloomberg (2013) — Helen Walton set up four in 2003, her estate twelve more; Alice
  Walton's mother and brother put more than $9bn into such trusts since 2003. "JCLAT" as a term is not used on air.
- All tools are lawful; nothing in the film implies wrongdoing.
