# Visual direction: Why the World Still Depends on Chinese Factories

Generated by `tools/visual_direction.py` from the script, then reviewed. The YAML twin (`projects/why-the-world-still-depends-on-chinese-factories/visual_direction.yaml`) is applied to the render: palette, scene tones, tone shifts, presenter demeanour and the music bed all follow it.

1. **Primary emotional tone:** sitcom
2. **Secondary tones:** trade, history
3. **Colour palette:** saturated flat primaries, bold outlines (episode palette: bg #F3F7F4, accent #FF9F1C, risk #E5484D)
4. **Lighting and contrast:** bright daylight, punchy
5. **Animation intensity:** constant motion, squash and stretch, whip pans
6. **Character behaviour:** warm: explains between gags (demeanour `warm`, at least 4 presenter sequences, only where they explain)
7. **Background design:** bespoke retro-futurist sets
8. **Charts and diagrams:** bold bars, big numbers
9. **Transitions:** act bumpers, title card
10. **Music and sound:** major turnaround, 3 s chords, bouncy pulse; the bed changes with each chapter's tone and crossfades at turning points
11. **Key emotional turning points:**
    - chapter "Everything is down the street": sitcom → trade
    - chapter "The tariff wall": trade → sitcom
    - chapter "China plus one": sitcom → trade
    - chapter "Why it's hard to leave": trade → sitcom
12. **Scene-specific requirements:**
    - No company logos or brand names on products, signs or ships; generic products only
    - No real flag designs; countries are named in text only
    - No national-stereotype caricature, accent jokes, or mocking of any people; Chinese workers and engineers shown as skilled professionals
    - No character, design, name, catchphrase, location, title text or music borrowed from any existing show
    - Jokes never change a number; our calculations and the 2022 IEA solar figures labelled on screen; tariff rates dated
    - Day scenes, many generic human characters; no real person drawn
