## 3. Human question
"After all the tariffs, why does almost everything I buy still say Made in China?"

## 4. First 30 seconds
1. **Recognizable subject (0–15 s):** Mel's toaster dies. She finds "Made in China" on it, then on the kettle, the lamp and the
   charger in her pocket.
2. **Contradiction (15–28 s):** since 2018 Washington has raised tariffs again and again; US imports from China fell to about
   $309B in 2025 from $440B; yet China still makes about 27% of everything the world manufactures.
3. **Reason to stay (28–35 s):** "So why is it so hard to leave?" The film then promises the tour: the factory floor, the street
   of suppliers, the port, the refinery, and a factory that tried to move.

The channel intro follows this promise. There is no history lecture before the hook.

## 6. Animated explanatory sequences (≥3, presenter interacts)
1. **p08: the world's factory bar.** The presenter draws world manufacturing in 2004 and 2025 and fills China's slice, from about
   1 dollar in 12 to more than 1 in 4. He points at the 2025 slice.
2. **p14: the supplier cluster.** He draws the final assembler surrounded by screw, cable, mold, glass, motor and board
   suppliers, with short arrows marked MINUTES. He then drags the assembler far away and every arrow becomes a container, a
   customs form and weeks at sea. Paired with s13: the parts street lights up shop by shop while scooters carry parts.
3. **p25: who pays a tariff.** Factory, then the US importer (who pays at the border), then shoppers (at least 30% in retail
   prices, Fed estimate). He points at the border.
4. **p30: where the orders went.** Paired bars for 2017 and 2026 for China, Mexico, Vietnam, Taiwan and India. He points at
   Vietnam's jump.
5. **Signature non-presenter sequences:**
   - s18: the rare-earth refinery runs from mine to tank cascade to magnets.
   - s22–s24: the tariff wall grows with dated rows, and some rows topple.
   - s27/s32: the boxes that walk back home.

## 7. Payoff
The world depends on Chinese factories not because of one cheap wage but because of a **neighborhood**: suppliers minutes
apart, refiners for the hidden materials (85% of magnet rare-earth refining), the busiest port (55 million containers), and
engineers who have launched products many times. Tariffs moved the **last step**: China's share of US imports fell from about a
fifth to under 8%, while Vietnam, Taiwan and Mexico rose. The **parts list** moved far less (China's exports to Southeast Asia
rose 13.4%, partly parts and materials). That is why, after eight years of trade war, China still makes more than a quarter of
the world's manufactured value. The broader insight is that a factory can be relocated in a few years, but a supply
neighborhood takes far longer to rebuild.
