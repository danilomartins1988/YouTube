## 2. The $166 billion figure

- **[ESTIMATE] $166bn is a CBP estimate made on March 6, 2026, cited by the trade court.** "On March 4, 2026, when the initial order was issued, it was estimated that unlawfully collected duties amounting to $166 billion had been assessed on 53 million entries, involving roughly 330,000 importers of record. See Decl. Brandon Lord (Mar. 6, 2026) ¶ 12, *Atmus Filtration, Inc. v. United States*, No. 26-01259, ECF No. 31." — U.S. Court of International Trade (CIT), *Freestyle World, Inc. v. CBP*, No. 26-01088, Opinion and Order, ECF 61 — https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.cit.19175/gov.uscourts.cit.19175.61.0.pdf (2026-10-08)
- **[ESTIMATE] Interest:** It was also estimated that interest was "accumulating every day, with approximately $650 million accruing per month" (based on a roughly 4.5% rate), and "If the entries are not liquidated before the end of the year, it is further estimated that $10 billion of interest will have accrued." — same CIT opinion, 2026-10-08
- **[FACT] The interest rate CBP pays on corporate refunds for Q4 2026 is 6%.** "For the calendar quarter beginning October 1, 2026 … The interest rate for overpayments will be 7 percent for non-corporations and 6 percent for corporations." — Federal Register 2026-19292, 91 FR 60141 — https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/09/22/2026-19292/quarterly-irs-interest-rates-used-in-calculating-interest-on-overdue-accounts-and-refunds-of-customs (2026-09-22)

## 4. How the refund works (CAPE, liquidation, protests, the CIT)

- **[FACT] What CAPE is:** "CBP has developed a new capability within its system of record for imported merchandise — the Automated Commercial Environment (ACE) — to calculate and provide valid refunds of additional ad valorem duties imposed under IEEPA. This new ACE functionality is called the Consolidated Administration and Processing of Entries (CAPE)." Phase 1 went live "at 7:11am eastern time on Monday, April 20, 2026." — Lord Decl., ECF 50, 2026-09-15 (URL above)
- **[FACT] Definitions (liquidation / finally liquidated):** "Liquidation is the 'final computation or ascertainment of duties on entries for consumption or drawback entries.' 19 C.F.R. § 159.1." "An entry is considered 'finally liquidated' when it has been liquidated for more than 90 days and is thus beyond the period for voluntary reliquidation by U.S. Customs and Border Protection that is set out in 19 U.S.C. § 1501." — CIT ECF 61, 2026-10-08
- **[FACT] Phases:** Phase 1 = unliquidated entries and entries liquidated within the preceding 80 days (CBP page). Phase 2 (June 29, 2026) = reconciliation-flagged entries with no Type 09 recon entry filed. Phase 3 (Oct. 6, 2026) "covers finally liquidated entries filed by plaintiffs for which the Court has ordered reliquidation, including those entries subject to a protest." — Lord Decl., ECF 65, 2026-10-26
- **[FACT] Phase 3 is for litigants only.** CIT order: plaintiffs in individual cases can process finally liquidated entries "that are the subject of a protest, in CAPE Phase 3, (1) even if the protest has not been withdrawn, and (2) no matter if the protest relates exclusively to IEEPA duties or includes other issues as well." — CIT ECF 66 — https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.cit.19175/gov.uscourts.cit.19175.66.0.pdf (2026-10-29)
- **[FACT] Protests:** "Entries on an open or suspended protest cannot be included on a CAPE Declaration"; to contest a refund amount, file a protest within 180 days of liquidation or reliquidation. — CBP, IEEPA Duty Refunds page — https://www.cbp.gov/trade/programs-administration/trade-remedies/ieepa-duty-refunds ("Last Modified: Nov 02, 2026"; paraphrase of FAQ, not verbatim)
- **[FACT] Timing:** refunds "generally" issued within 60–90 days of CAPE Declaration acceptance; paid by ACH; interest is included ("You will not receive a separate interest refund"). — CBP page above, last modified 2026-11-02 (paraphrase except the quoted sentence)
- **[FACT] Litigation volume:** "As of October 7, 2026, more than 4,000 nearly identical cases have been filed involving around 7,000 plaintiffs." — CIT ECF 61, 2026-10-08
- **[FACT] Appeal still pending:** the CIT's reliquidation order in *V.O.S. Selections* is on appeal, "appeal docketed, No. 26-1895 (Fed. Cir. June 3, 2026) … the CAFC's decision will be rendered in due course." — CIT ECF 61, 2026-10-08
- **[FACT] Importer class action certified (importers, NOT consumers).** Class: "All importers of record who paid duties imposed by the Challenged Tariff Orders between February 1, 2025, and February 19, 2026, excluding any entity that has separately filed an action seeking to recover IEEPA duties payments." Class counsel: Barnes, Richardson & Colburn and Lieff Cabraser. — CIT ECF 61, 2026-10-08. Complaint filed Feb. 19, 2026 (same opinion).
- **[FACT] Next steps in the importer class action:** plaintiff to file motion for an order directing CBP to liquidate/reliquidate class members' entries "by November 11, 2026"; government answer due "November 13, 2026"; government response "by December 9, 2026." — CIT ECF 68 — https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.cit.19175/gov.uscourts.cit.19175.68.0.pdf (2026-11-09)
- **[FACT] CBP is formalizing court-ordered refunds:** Federal Register notice, "Agency Information Collection Activities; Court-Ordered Refunds Under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act Worksheet," 91 FR 63299 — https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/05/2026-20228/agency-information-collection-activities-court-ordered-refunds-under-the-international-emergency (2026-10-05)

## 5. Who gets the money: importer of record, not consumers

- **[FACT] CBP, verbatim:** Q: "Is CBP sending refunds directly to consumers who may have paid additional money because of an IEEPA duty?" A: "CBP is only able to refund IEEPA duties to the Importer of Record or the Notify Party (designated on CBP Form 4811) who have their U.S. bank account information in their ACE Portal account." — https://www.cbp.gov/trade/programs-administration/trade-remedies/ieepa-duty-refunds (last modified 2026-11-02)
- **[FACT] CBP, verbatim:** "The IOR or the party designated by the IOR (the 4811 notify party) on either the CBP Form 4811 (Special Address Notification) or on the ACE Portal account, and on the entry summary will be eligible to receive refunds." — same page
- **[FACT] The CIT on who is left out:** "Some importers of record have not taken advantage of CAPE. It is believed that these are overwhelmingly small importers who, because of the cost or effort involved in filing a CAPE declaration, or because they are unaware of the program, will never take advantage of CAPE." — CIT ECF 61, 2026-10-08
- **[FACT] No government consumer refund program exists.** None of the CBP page, the CIT orders, or the Federal Register notices reviewed describes any refund to consumers. The only money flowing to end customers is voluntary, by companies (see FedEx and Costco below).

## 8. What replaced the IEEPA tariffs

- **[FACT] IEEPA duties formally ended Feb. 20.** Executive Order 14389, "Ending Certain Tariff Actions": the IEEPA ad valorem duties "shall no longer be in effect and, as soon as practicable, shall no longer be collected." — 91 FR 9437 — https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/02/25/2026-03832/ending-certain-tariff-actions (signed 2026-02-20, published 2026-02-25)
- **[FACT] Section 122 (10%, 150 days).** Proclamation 11012: "I impose, for a period of 150 days, a temporary import surcharge of 10 percent ad valorem … on articles imported into the United States, effective February 24, 2026." — 91 FR 9339 — https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/02/25/2026-03824/imposing-a-temporary-import-surcharge-to-address-fundamental-international-payments-problems (signed 2026-02-20, published 2026-02-25; text read via govinfo https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-02-25/html/2026-03824.htm)
- **[FACT] Section 122 expired July 24, 2026; Section 301 and 232 took over.** "With the expiration of the Section 122 tariffs on July 24, 2026, the President imposed additional duties using Section 301 of the Trade Act of 1974 and continued to use Section 232 of the Trade Expansion Act of 1962 to impose duties throughout 2026." "On July 23, 2026, President Trump imposed tariff rates of 10% or 12.5% on 60 economies resulting from USTR's final determination of its Section 301 investigation into whether the relevant countries had banned—and effectively enforced those bans—on imports made with forced labor." Since Feb. 20 the President has cited four laws, including Section 338 of the Tariff Act of 1930. — CRS R49481, "Active Presidential Tariff Actions in 2026: Fact Sheet" — https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/PDF/R49481/R49481.1.pdf (2026-10-08)
- **[FACT] Section 301 memorandum text:** "The Trade Representative shall impose a tariff of 10 percent on goods of Argentina, Bangladesh, Cambodia, Canada, Ecuador, El Salvador, Guatemala, Honduras, India, Indonesia, Jordan, Malaysia, Mexico, Pakistan, Sri Lanka, the United Kingdom, and Trinidad and Tobago"; EU/Taiwan capped at 10% and Japan/Korea/Switzerland at 12.5% including MFN duty. — Presidential Memorandum of July 23, 2026, 91 FR 47717 — https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/07/28/2026-15274/actions-by-the-united-states-in-the-investigations-under-section-301-of-the-trade-act-of-1974-of-the (2026-07-28)
- **[FACT] Section 232 actions continued**, e.g. "Strengthening Actions Taken To Adjust Imports of Aluminum, Steel, and Copper" (FR 2026-06960, 2026-04-09), pharmaceuticals (2026-06956, 2026-04-09), commercial aircraft (2026-14334, 2026-07-15), robotics/industrial machinery (2026-22385, 2026-11-02). — Federal Register API listing, https://www.federalregister.gov/api/v1/documents.json (titles only; texts not read)
- **[COMPANY STATEMENT] Section 122 challenged in court:** "in May 2026, the CIT issued a ruling that the Section 122 tariffs are unauthorized by the statute, although this ruling is currently stayed pending appeal." — Mattel 10-Q, filed 2026-11-06 (CIT ruling itself not read — see Flags)

## 9. Who actually paid the tariffs (pass-through) — ESTIMATES, attribute by name

- **[ESTIMATE] Yale Budget Lab:** "Implied passthrough of tariffs to imported consumer goods prices ranges from roughly 46–86% for core goods and 51–115% for durables, depending on methodology." Also: "In its modeling, TBL assumes that passthrough of tariffs to consumer prices will ultimately reach 100%." It notes other researchers find less, e.g. "Cavallo, Llamas, and Vazquez (2026) estimate that the passthrough to retail prices was 24 percent in October 2025." — Yale Budget Lab, "Tracking the Economic Effects of Tariffs" — https://budgetlab.yale.edu/sites/default/files/page_to_pdf/1349/publication_1349.pdf (published 2026-04-01)
- **[ESTIMATE] Yale Budget Lab on the effective rate:** "Before the IEEPA tariffs were struck down, consumers faced an overall average effective tariff rate of 16%, the highest since 1936. Immediately following the IEEPA ruling, the rate fell to 9.1%." — "State of U.S. Tariffs: February 21, 2026" — https://budgetlab.yale.edu/sites/default/files/page_to_pdf/1371/publication_1371.pdf (2026-02-21). (This report modeled Section 122 at 15%, which is not what the proclamation imposed — see Flags.)
- **[ESTIMATE] New York Fed (Amiti, Heise, Weinstein):** "nearly 90 percent of the 2025 tariffs were passed through to U.S. import prices"; consumer goods prices rise "about a quarter of a percent after one year" per percentage point of tariff; for a hypothetical 10% tariff on all imports, consumer goods prices "2.6 percent higher after twelve months." — Liberty Street Economics, "How Fast Do Tariffs Pass Through into Consumer Prices?" — https://libertystreeteconomics.newyorkfed.org/2026/10/how-fast-do-tariffs-pass-through-into-consumer-prices/ (2026-10-06)
- **[ESTIMATE] Federal Reserve Board (Hacıoğlu-Hoke, Malladi, Feler):** pass-through "has been at least 30% for goods imported from China" (April–Dec. 2025), a conservative estimate; "many producers and retailers first worked through excess inventories accumulated before tariffs took effect." — FEDS Notes, "The Slow Climb: How Tariffs Gradually Raised Retail Prices in 2025" — https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/the-slow-climb-how-tariffs-gradually-raised-retail-prices-in-2025-20260305.html (2026-03-05)
- **[ESTIMATE] Tax Foundation:** average tariff tax increase of about $820 per US household in 2026 (down from roughly $1,000 in 2025); Section 122 "took effect at 10 percent, not the threatened 15 percent"; 2025 customs collections "rose to $264 billion." — Tax Foundation, "Trump Tariffs: Tracking the Economic Impact of the Trump Trade War" — https://taxfoundation.org/research/all/federal/trump-tariffs-trade-war/ (last updated 2026-09-10; read through WebFetch summary, quotes short)
- **Takeaway for script:** Studies agree foreign exporters absorbed little (import prices rose ~90% of the tariff, per NY Fed), but disagree widely on how much reached shelf prices (~24% to ~100%, depending on method and horizon). "Consumers paid all of it" and "companies paid all of it" are both unsupported.

## Flags / not verified

1. **"$166bn collected" is an estimate, not an audited total.** It is CBP's March 6, 2026 estimate (Lord Decl. ¶12, *Atmus Filtration*, CIT No. 26-01259, ECF 31) as quoted by the CIT on Oct. 8. I read the CIT's quotation, not the March 6 declaration itself. Say "an estimated $166 billion."
2. **"$100bn certified by July 31" is confirmed, but it is duties PLUS interest** and means "certified and sent to Treasury for disbursement," not necessarily money already in bank accounts. Same for $122bn (Sept. 11), $126bn (Oct. 2) and $127.7bn (Oct. 23).
3. **Apples-to-oranges math:** the CIT's "more than 75% of the $166 billion … plus interest" divides a duties+interest figure by a duties-only base. Don't compute "% refunded" on screen without saying so.
4. **"$137.9bn accepted" includes "potential" refunds** that CBP has not finished reviewing. Do not call it "paid."
5. **Section 122 rate conflict:** the proclamation (91 FR 9339) says **10%**. Yale Budget Lab's Feb. 21 report modeled "the new 15% Section 122 tariffs"; Tax Foundation says it "took effect at 10 percent, not the threatened 15 percent." I found no Federal Register document raising it to 15%. Use 10%.
6. **CIT ruling that Section 122 was unlawful (May 2026)** comes only from Mattel's 10-Q. The CIT opinion itself was not located or read.
7. **CIT March 4, 2026 order (*Atmus Filtration*)** was not read directly. Its content comes through the Oct. 8 CIT opinion and Mattel's 10-Q ("The CIT's refund order has been appealed").
8. **Consumer class-action dockets were not read directly.** CourtListener search was blocked (403). Case names and numbers come from the defendants' own SEC filings (Costco, Mattel, FedEx), which count as primary for "a suit exists," plus a Manatt alert for the other cases.
