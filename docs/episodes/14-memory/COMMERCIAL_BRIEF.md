## 4. First 30 seconds (as rendered)
What if the price of one tiny part inside your computer jumped more than five times in a single year, and almost nobody outside the industry noticed why? That part is a memory chip. In October twenty twenty-five, big buyers paid eight dollars and seventy cents for one standard sixteen gigabit memory chip. By September twenty twenty-six, the same chip cost forty-eight dollars. That is a rise of about four hundred and fifty percent. And behind that price are just three companies, making one quiet choice. So what exactly got so expensive, and how much of it is in the device on your desk?

## 7. Payoff (as rendered)
So who decides how much your memory costs? Not you, and not the shop. Three companies, each choosing the customer who pays the most for every wafer. Today that customer is artificial intelligence. Tomorrow, when the new factories open, it may be you again. So the next time a memory stick costs more than you expected, remember that its chips were promised to the highest bidder long before it reached the shelf. In memory, the price is never about the chip. It is about who is first in line.
