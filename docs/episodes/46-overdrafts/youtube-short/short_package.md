> Thirty-five dollars. That is the typical overdraft fee at America's largest banks, according to the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau. You can pay it for spending just a few dollars more than you have. Banks have cut these fees sharply in the last few years. Even so, in twenty twenty-three, banks with more than a billion dollars in assets still collected five point eight three billion dollars in overdraft and bounced-payment fees. So why is a fee that everyone seems to hate still worth billions? And who is really paying it? By the end of this video, you will know how an overdraft fee is made, why a small group of customers pays most of it, and how a federal cap on the fee was written and then erased.
