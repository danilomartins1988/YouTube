## 4. First 30 seconds (as rendered)
Thirty-five dollars. That is the typical overdraft fee at America's largest banks, according to the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau. You can pay it for spending just a few dollars more than you have. Banks have cut these fees sharply in the last few years. Even so, in twenty twenty-three, banks with more than a billion dollars in assets still collected five point eight three billion dollars in overdraft and bounced-payment fees. So why is a fee that everyone seems to hate still worth billions? And who is really paying it?

## 7. Payoff (as rendered)
So why do banks still make billions from overdrafts? Because the fee is small for most people and huge for a few. It is charged on tiny purchases, paid by customers who live close to zero, and repeated month after month. Banks cut it in half when the pressure was high. The cap that would have cut it again was erased. If you want to keep your money out of that machine, the simplest move is to check whether you opted in.
