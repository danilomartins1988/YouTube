## Short 1 — "Why Are Chinese Electric Cars So Cheap?" (manufacturing cost mystery)
- **Hook headline:** A $9,900 EV. / HOW?
- **Segments:** cold open (co_open, co_hook, co_contra, co_promise), ≈ 50 s.
- **Script (narration):** "Picture a brand-new electric car. A big touchscreen. A laser scanner on the roof for driver assistance.
  A battery its maker says charges from ten to seventy percent in five minutes. Now, the price. In China, the newest BYD Seagull
  starts at sixty-five thousand nine hundred yuan. That's about nine thousand nine hundred dollars. The average new electric car in
  America sold for fifty-six thousand two hundred dollars in October. Meanwhile, Ford's electric car unit lost four point eight
  billion dollars last year. Is China just better at building cars? Is the government paying? Are the books dressed up? Or is
  someone burning cash on every car? Four suspects…"
- **Standalone insight:** the price gap is real and big enough to be a mystery (with the market/tax context on screen).
- **Reason to watch the full film:** the four suspects are named; the verdict is in the film.
- **Output:** `output/shorts/61-chinese-cars_short.mp4`; package `docs/episodes/61-chinese-cars/youtube-short/`.

## Short 2 — "Are Chinese Car Companies Actually Making Money?" (prices vs profits vs cash burn)
- **Hook headline:** PROFITABLE. / AND BURNING CASH
- **Segments:** a4_byd + a4_unwind, ≈ 50 s.
- **Script (narration):** "So the cars are cheaper to build. Are the companies making money? BYD is. Last year it earned about four
  and a half billion dollars on revenue of about one hundred and twelve billion dollars, though profit fell nineteen percent as
  prices dropped. Its car business made about four billion dollars before tax. That's roughly eight hundred and sixty dollars a
  vehicle, by our rough math. Watch BYD's cash. What it owed suppliers fell by about four point eight billion dollars. Cash from
  operations dropped from about eighteen and a half billion dollars to about eight point two billion dollars. It spent about
  twenty-two billion dollars on factories and equipment, and borrowed more. Free cash flow, by our calculation: minus thirteen
  point six billion dollars. Profitable, and burning cash."
- **Standalone insight:** accounting profit ≠ free cash flow; paying suppliers faster and building factories drained cash.
- **Reason to watch:** why suppliers got paid faster (the 60-day pledge) and who else is losing money.
- **Output:** `output/shorts/61-chinese-cars-b_short.mp4`; package `youtube-short-b/`.

## Short 3 — "China's Biggest EV Advantage Isn't Just Subsidies" (a structural advantage: the battery chain)
- **Hook headline:** IT'S NOT JUST / SUBSIDIES
- **Segments:** a2_turn + a3_chain + a3_price, ≈ 50 s.
- **Script (narration):** "If subsidies were the whole story, those duties would have closed the gap. Instead, sales of
  Chinese-made electric cars in Europe grew almost fifty percent last year. Let me draw the battery's journey. Minerals are mined,
  refined, turned into powders, made into cells, packed, and put in a car. Now let me shade China's share: about eighty-five percent
  of the cathode powder, more than ninety percent of the anode powder, more than eighty percent of the cells. And two companies,
  CATL and BYD, supplied more than half of the world's car batteries. The result: in twenty twenty-five, battery packs in China
  cost thirty percent less than in North America, and thirty-five percent less than in Europe."
- **Standalone insight:** the whole battery chain in one country makes the most expensive part of an EV ~30–35% cheaper.
- **Reason to watch:** the full verdict on subsidies (grants ≈ 1.6% of BYD's revenue but ≈ two-fifths of its profit).
- **Output:** `output/shorts/61-chinese-cars-c_short.mp4`; package `youtube-short-c/`.
