## 3. Human question
"How can a Chinese company sell a high-tech electric car for less than ten thousand dollars when Ford is losing billions on EVs —
and is anybody actually making money?"

## 4. First 30 seconds
1. **Recognizable subject (0–10 s):** a gleaming new EV in a showroom: big screen, a laser scanner on the roof, five-minute
   charging (company claim). Then the price: about $9,900 (China list price, Oct 2026).
2. **Contradiction (10–22 s):** the average new EV in America sold for $56,220 last month; Ford's EV unit lost $4.8B last year.
3. **Reason to stay (22–35 s):** four suspects: efficiency, subsidies, financial engineering, cash burn — or all four? "We'll open the
   car, follow the battery, read the cash-flow statements, and watch a price war that's hurting the companies winning it."
Channel intro follows; then the show title card. No history lecture before the hook.

## 6. Animated explanatory sequences (≥3, presenter interacts)
1. **The $25,000 Car Mystery** — presenter draws a car outline and its cost slices; then a bespoke exploded car (`costcar`) splits
   into labelled slices (battery from BNEF; all other allocations labelled ILLUSTRATIVE).
2. **The Battery Supply Chain** — presenter draws mine → refine → powders → cells → pack → car → buyer and shades China's share;
   bespoke `chainvalley` set carries material along the chain.
3. **The Subsidy Experiment** — presenter builds bars: price, grant (≈1.6% of revenue), profit with and without grants (BYD 2025
   ratios, our calculation, labelled).
4. **The Cash Burn Machine** — bespoke `cashmachine` set: revenue pours through filters into gross, operating, operating cash,
   then the capex drain empties the free-cash-flow tank; presenter then compares two hypothetical makers' cash.
5. **The Global Price War** — bespoke `pricestreet` (price signs falling, margin gauge) + presenter drawing price and cost lines
   converging; `carport` export ship and `tariffgate` walls.

## 7. Payoff
The cheap Chinese car is real and mostly **structural**: LFP batteries made in-house or next door, a clustered supplier base,
development in about half the time, and enormous scale. **Subsidies were the accomplice** — about $231B of support from 2009 to
2023, mostly paid to buyers, built the industry; today BYD's grants equal ≈1.6% of revenue (too small to explain the price) but
≈ two-fifths of its profit. **Financial engineering** (paying suppliers late) was a real, cheap loan and is being unwound by the
60-day pledge. **Cash burn** depends on the company: BYD is profitable but spent ≈ $13.6B more cash than it generated in 2025;
NIO lost ≈ $2.1B; weaker brands went bankrupt. Broader insight: **the West can't copy the advantage with one factory or block it
forever with one tariff; the price war means the cars will stay cheap and there will be fewer companies making them.**
