# Owner production prompt (2026-11-17, PRIORITY): The Hidden Economics of Chinese Cars

Verbatim owner brief. Applies together with docs/COMMERCIAL_DIRECTIVE.md and docs/PRODUCTION_STANDARD.md.

## PROJECT OBJECTIVE
Produce a complete, original, commercially compelling, English-language YouTube documentary explaining why Chinese automakers have become so technologically advanced and cost-competitive.

Working title: "Why Are Chinese Cars So Cheap — And So Advanced?"
Alternative titles to evaluate:
- "How China Built Cars the West Struggles to Compete With"
- "The Hidden Economics Behind China's Cheap Electric Cars"
- "The Real Reason Chinese Cars Are Taking Over"
- "Are China's Cheap Electric Cars Too Good to Be True?"
Select the strongest title based on audience appeal, factual accuracy, search interest, and thumbnail potential.

Target audience: mainstream American viewers interested in business, technology, money, geopolitics, and the automotive industry. Target duration: 11–13 minutes. Format: premium animated economic documentary. Language: natural American English. Resolution 1920×1080. Final output: rendered MP4, thumbnail, optimized title, description, chapters, source references, Shorts concepts. Follow the channel's existing architecture, branding, recurring animated character, intro, voice and rendering workflow. Do not interrupt ongoing renders or overwrite completed projects.

## 1. CENTRAL DOCUMENTARY QUESTION
How can Chinese automakers offer advanced technology and competitive prices while many traditional Western automakers struggle to compete? And, more importantly: is China's advantage driven by genuine manufacturing efficiency, government subsidies, financial engineering, aggressive cash burn, or a combination of all four? Do not assume the answer before completing the research. Uncover the actual economic mechanisms. The audience should finish understanding not only why Chinese cars can be inexpensive, but whether the underlying business models are financially sustainable.

## 2. COMMERCIAL STORYTELLING — ENTERTAINMENT FIRST
Engaging business documentary, not an academic lecture: a surprising opening, a recognizable global conflict, strong financial stakes, genuine economic mysteries, unexpected findings, clear numerical explanations, a meaningful conclusion. Appeal to viewers who know very little about Chinese car companies. Do not begin with an extended history. Start with the contradiction: how can a Chinese EV offer impressive technology at a competitive price while established Western manufacturers struggle with costs and profitability? Use real, verifiable vehicle examples and comparable market prices. Avoid unsupported or misleading comparisons between vehicles with different specifications, taxes, or markets.

## 3. OPENING HOOK — FIRST 30 SECONDS
Concept: "Imagine walking into a dealership and finding an electric car with a giant touchscreen, advanced driver-assistance features, modern battery technology, and a price that makes traditional automakers uncomfortable. Now imagine that the company selling it may be making money, receiving government support, or losing money on every car. How do you tell the difference? And how did China become so good at building these machines?" Rewrite into a stronger, natural, evidence-supported opening that (1) establishes the mystery, (2) shows the technological appeal, (3) introduces the economic contradiction, (4) creates curiosity about the financial reality, (5) does not reveal the conclusion. Place the 5-second channel intro after the hook.

## 4. RESEARCH REQUIREMENTS
Most recent available information. Prioritize: BYD annual and interim reports; Geely financial statements; Xiaomi automotive segment disclosures; NIO financial statements; IEA EV reports; European Commission investigations and decisions; US government trade and industrial policy documents; Rhodium Group research; academic research on Chinese industrial policy; credible automotive industry analysis; Reuters, FT, Bloomberg and other reputable reporting. Verify all important claims. Separate: government subsidies; consumer purchase incentives; tax benefits; preferential financing; industrial policy support; operational efficiency; economies of scale; supplier financing; corporate cash burn; manufacturing margins. Never treat these as interchangeable. Do not invent statistics, prices, margins, financial statements, subsidies, or quotations. If current data cannot be verified, state the limitation and use the latest verified reporting period.

## 5. REQUIRED ECONOMIC INVESTIGATIONS
A. The price mystery: manufacturing costs, battery costs, labor productivity, supplier networks, automation, scale, development cycles, vertical integration, domestic competition, pricing strategies. Which advantages are structural vs temporary. Real vehicle comparisons only where reliable comparable data exists.
B. The subsidy question: direct subsidies, tax incentives, R&D support, government-backed financing, land and infrastructure, consumer incentives, battery industry policy, local-government competition. Subsidies can establish an industry without explaining every price difference. Where evidence permits, estimate their contribution. Distinguish company-level support from per-vehicle subsidies. Present counterarguments and methodological limitations.
C. The manufacturing advantage: integrated supply chains, supplier proximity, standardization, automation, engineering talent, scale, rapid iteration, domestic competition. Why an established automaker cannot replicate the economics simply by building a new factory.
D. The battery advantage: LFP vs NMC, manufacturing scale, material processing, cell-to-pack, BYD Blade Battery, CATL's role, supply-chain concentration, cost reductions. Effect on cost, design, performance, profitability. No chemistry is universally superior.
E. The technology question: software-defined vehicles, infotainment, connected features, driver assistance, rapid development, Chinese consumer expectations, competition among BYD, Geely, Xiaomi, NIO and others. Distinguish visible features from independently validated performance. A big touchscreen or long feature list is not superior safety, reliability or engineering.
F. Cash burn — who is actually making money? (one of the most important sections): revenue growth, gross margins, automotive segment margins, operating and net profit/loss, operating cash flow, capex, free cash flow, cash, debt, working capital, supplier payment terms, government support. Low prices ≠ losing money; accounting profit ≠ positive FCF; positive operating cash flow ≠ sustainable profitability. BYD, Geely, Xiaomi Auto, NIO as examples, subject to comparable disclosures. Not all Chinese makers share one business model.
G. The price war: price cuts, excess capacity, utilization, margin compression, supplier pressure, consolidation, export incentives, possible failures. Is it sustainable? Distinguish documented overcapacity from speculation about specific companies.
H. The global response: Tesla, Volkswagen, Toyota, GM, European automakers, US and EU trade barriers, tariffs and industrial policy, Chinese overseas manufacturing, consumer access and affordability, supply-chain security. The trade-off between protecting domestic manufacturing and consumer access to cheaper vehicles. Not a simplistic geopolitical victory for either side.

## 6. NARRATIVE STRUCTURE (adjust timing to material)
Act 1 The Impossible Car (0:00–2:00): paradox, why Western automakers are concerned, mystery.
Act 2 The Subsidy Explanation (2:00–3:45): evidence for support and the limits of a subsidy-only explanation.
Act 3 The Real Industrial Machine (3:45–6:15): supply chains, batteries, efficiency, scale.
Act 4 The Financial Reality (6:15–9:00): profitable vs loss-making makers; margins, working capital, supplier financing, cash burn.
Act 5 The Global Price War (9:00–10:45): overcapacity, tariffs, exports, Western responses, consequences.
Act 6 The Real Answer (10:45–12:30): which advantages are structural, which policy-dependent, which possibly unsustainable; memorable evidence-based conclusion.

## 7. ORIGINAL ANIMATIONS (at least five)
1. The $25,000 Car Mystery: a verified real vehicle or a clearly labeled hypothetical $25,000 car broken into battery, powertrain, electronics, body/materials, labor/manufacturing, distribution, taxes, margin. Never present illustrative allocations as real company data.
2. The Battery Supply Chain: animated map from mineral processing to components, cells, vehicle manufacturing, consumers; concentration and vertical integration.
3. The Subsidy Experiment: transparent hypothetical showing how subsidies, tax benefits, scale and efficiency affect unit economics. Label all assumptions. No invented per-vehicle subsidy amounts.
4. The Cash Burn Machine: revenue → gross profit → operating profit → operating cash flow → capex → free cash flow; why profit and cash diverge.
5. The Global Price War: capacity, competition, prices, margins, exports, tariffs; how a price war helps consumers and hurts weaker makers.

## 8. RECURRING CHARACTER
The established illustrated character must: draw a simplified car cost structure; build a battery supply-chain diagram; demonstrate how government support affects business economics; compare two hypothetical manufacturers' cash flows; illustrate how a price war compresses margins. Natural movement, real interaction; no static "pretend drawing".

No repetitive stock footage or generic AI images; no unlicensed footage. Original illustrations or properly licensed/permitted material. Consistent branding.

## 10. NARRATION AND AUDIO

Source dossier; key sources in the description. Never invent data for drama.
