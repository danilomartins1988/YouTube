## 6. Animated explanatory sequences (≥3, presenter interacts)
1. **$100 at the register** (p08): the presenter builds the bars; ≈ $89 goes back to suppliers, ≈ $9 to stores/staff/trucks, ≈ $2 left; he points at the $2. Preceded by the unrolling $100 receipt (s07).
2. **The fee ladder** (p14): the presenter draws the four fee steps and the renewal line; then the membership desk animates the 2024 raise and the "30% vs 70%" growth split (s15).
3. **The squeeze** (p25): supermarket long list vs Costco short list; arrows for bargaining power and the house-brand lever; the presenter points at the supplier link. Paired with the house-brand labeller factory (s23-s24).
4. **The cash clock** (p33) + loading-dock day strip (s31-s32): pallet in on day 0, sold ≈ day 26, bill due later; balance-sheet bars $19.3bn vs $22.6bn.
5. **The machine loop** (p38): five boxes chained by arrows; the presenter points at fees ≈ half of operating profit.

## 7. Payoff
Costco barely profits on the goods (≈ $2 per $100 before fees, our calculation from the FY2026 10-K); it sells access. The
low prices are the product, the $65 card is the business ($5.9bn, equal to about half of operating income), the short list
and house brand keep prices low, fast turnover lets it hold suppliers' money, and the $1.50 hot dog is the visible proof each
visit that the deal is still on. The broader insight: the store is the bait, the fee is the catch, and the whole machine
depends on renewals staying near nine in ten.
