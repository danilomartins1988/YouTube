# Ep 44 — "One Factory Makes 60% of America's IV Bags" — Research Notes

Fact-check compiled 2026-11-10. US dollars throughout. Every bullet below was checked against the primary page or PDF itself, not a search snippet, unless it is marked otherwise. Format: claim — exact quote or figure — URL — date published.

---

## 1. The plant's share of U.S. IV solution supply (the "60%")

- **The AHA is the source of the 60% figure, in its letter to the President.** "This facility is a critical supplier of intravenous (IV) and peritoneal dialysis solutions, producing approximately 60% — or 1.5 million bags — of the IV solutions used every day in the U.S." — https://www.aha.org/system/files/media/file/2024/10/aha-to-president-urging-the-administration-to-take-immediate-action-to-address-iv-solution-supply-shortage-as-a-result-of-helene-letter-10-7-2024.pdf (landing page: https://www.aha.org/2024-10-07-aha-president-urging-administration-take-immediate-action-address-iv-solution-supply-shortage-result-helene) — 2024-10-07
- **The AHA used different wording five days earlier.** "The plant manufactures 60% of the country's supply of IV solutions and produces 1.5 million bags per day." — https://www.aha.org/special-bulletin/supply-chain-updates-following-temporary-closure-baxters-plant-north-carolina-due-hurricane-helene — 2024-10-02
- **The AHA repeated the 60% on Oct 9.** "The plant manufactures approximately 60% — or 1.5 million bags — of the IV solutions used every day in the U.S." — https://www.aha.org/special-bulletin/2024-10-09-update-baxter-increase-allocations-iv-solution-supplies-hospitals-effective-today-oct-9 — 2024-10-09
- **Baxter's own wording gives no percentage.** North Cove "is Baxter's largest manufacturing facility and includes more than 2,500 employees. The site primarily manufactures intravenous and peritoneal dialysis solutions and is the largest manufacturer of these solutions in the United States." — https://www.baxter.com/news-insights/baxter-provides-update-north-cove-nc-facility-and-hurricane-helene — 2024-09-29
- **Baxter's fact sheet gives a lower daily output than the AHA.** It says "1M+ IV solutions produced at the site every day", "1.4M+ square foot facility", "LARGEST manufacturer of intravenous (IV) and peritoneal dialysis (PD) solutions in the U.S. and Baxter's largest manufacturing facility globally", and "$500M+ facility investments since 2017". — https://www.baxter.com/sites/baxtercorpna/files/2026-05/north-cove_fact-sheet-may2025-final.pdf — 2025-05-13
- **ASHP gives a qualitative description.** "The facility was responsible for manufacturing most of the large-volume sterile fluids used in American hospitals and healthcare settings." — https://news.ashp.org/-/media/assets/drug-shortages/docs/2024/ASHP-Sterile-Fluid-Shortage.pdf — survey fielded Oct 16–25, 2024

## 2. Shutdown, restart and recovery timeline (Baxter primary)

- **Storm date.** "Hurricane Helene on Sept. 27, 2024 ... resulted in water flooding the facility." — fact sheet, URL above — 2025-05-13
- **The plant was closed by Sept 29.** "Baxter's North Cove site was affected by flooding due to the storm and is currently closed for production." Also: "The heavy rain and storm surge triggered a levee breach, which led to water permeating the site. The bridges accessing the site have also been damaged." — https://www.baxter.com/news-insights/baxter-provides-update-north-cove-nc-facility-and-hurricane-helene — 2024-09-29
- **Oct 3: Baxter had no restart timeline.** "we do not yet have a timeline for when operations will be back up and running." — https://www.baxter.com/sites/baxtercorpna/files/2026-05/northcove_10-3.pdf — 2024-10-03
- **Oct 7: no structural damage, and the water source was intact.** "We have not identified any structural damage to the facility to date" and "the local aquifer, which is the main source of water used in the manufacturing process, is intact." — https://www.baxter.com/sites/baxtercorpna/files/2026-05/north-cove_10-7.pdf — 2024-10-07
- **Oct 9: Baxter set a goal of restarting by year-end.** "our goal is to restart North Cove production in phases and return to 90% to 100% allocation of certain IV solution product codes by the end of 2024." — https://www.baxter.com/sites/baxtercorpna/files/2026-05/update_10-9.pdf — 2024-10-09
- **Oct 31: the first line restarted, about a month after the storm.** "Baxter has restarted the highest-throughput IV solutions manufacturing line. At its peak operation (prior to Hurricane Helene), this line represented approximately 25% of the site's total production and approximately 50% of the site's production of one-liter IV solutions, the most commonly used size by hospitals and clinics." — https://www.baxter.com/sites/baxtercorpna/files/2026-05/update_10-31_0.pdf — 2024-10-31. The fact sheet dates this to the "Week of 10/28/24".
- **Nov 14: the second line restarted.** "Baxter restarted a second IV solutions manufacturing line this week. Together with the line restarted the week of Oct. 28, these two lines represent ... approximately 50% of the site's total production and approximately 85% of the site's production of one-liter IV solutions" — https://www.baxter.com/sites/baxtercorpna/files/2026-05/update_11-14.pdf — 2024-11-14
- **Nov 21: the first post-storm product was released.** "This week, Baxter released the first product – 1-liter IV solutions – that was manufactured post-hurricane. This is ahead of our original expectations" — https://www.baxter.com/sites/baxtercorpna/files/2026-05/update_11-21.pdf — 2024-11-21
- **Dec 5: the irrigation and PD lines restarted.** The restarted lines were "All 3L irrigation manufacturing lines" and "All peritoneal dialysis (PD) solutions manufacturing lines". — https://www.baxter.com/sites/baxtercorpna/files/2026-05/update-12-5.pdf — 2024-12-05
- **Dec 19: 8 of 10 lines were running.** Baxter had "restarted 8 of the 10 manufacturing lines at the site, which represent ~85% of the site's total pre-hurricane capacity." — https://www.baxter.com/sites/baxtercorpna/files/2026-05/update_12-19.pdf — 2024-12-19
- **Jan 28, 2025: all 10 lines had restarted.** "[We] have now restarted all of the site's 10 manufacturing lines. While some lines require additional time to ramp up production..." — https://www.baxter.com/sites/baxtercorpna/files/2026-05/update_1-28.pdf — 2025-01-28
- **Feb 17, 2025: production was back to pre-hurricane levels.** "Following our Jan. 28 announcement that all 10 of the site's manufacturing lines had restarted, we have now returned to pre-hurricane production levels." — https://www.baxter.com/sites/baxtercorpna/files/2026-05/2-17-25-website-update.pdf — 2025-02-17
- **May 13, 2025: allocations were removed (Baxter's final update).** "our inventory levels are restored and therefore allocations have been removed for virtually all IV Solutions product codes manufactured at North Cove. This update follows the February announcement that North Cove had returned to pre-hurricane production levels, less than five months after the storm." — https://www.baxter.com/baxter-newsroom/hurricane-helene-updates — 2025-05-13
- **Summary for script (our calculation).** Production was fully stopped for about 4.5 weeks (Sept 27 to the week of Oct 28). The first new product shipped about 8 weeks after the storm. All lines were running after 4 months, full output returned at about 4.7 months, and allocations ended at about 7.5 months.
- **Allocation levels (rationing by the supplier).** Baxter raised "U.S. allocation levels of our highest demand IV fluids for direct customers from 40% to 60%, and for distributors from 10% to 60%, effective Wednesday, Oct. 9" and raised children's hospitals to 100%. — update_10-9.pdf, URL above — 2024-10-09. The fact sheet adds: "100% allocation reached across critical IV product groups within approximately 3 months". **This 60% is an allocation level. It is NOT the plant's market share, so the script must not mix the two up.**

## 3. FDA actions

- **Oct 11: FDA shortage list and compounding guidance.** "The FDA also added three new drug products to the Drug Shortages Database as a result of Hurricane Helene. The agency now considers Dextrose 70% IV solution, Lactated Ringers IV Solution, and Peritoneal Dialysis Solution to not be commercially available in adequate supply to meet national demand." The same day FDA "issued an immediately-in-effect guidance, Temporary Policies for Compounding Certain Parenteral Drug Products ... to help fill the gaps from the impact of Hurricane Helene on Baxter International's North Cove facility." — https://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/fda-roundup-october-11-2024 — 2024-10-11
- **Oct 18: temporary imports.** "FDA acted quickly to conduct scientific and regulatory assessments to help facilitate the temporary importation of 23 different IV and PD fluids from five Baxter facilities around the world. These supply improvements, combined with increased output from other manufacturers, should help health care providers get more of the product they need over the coming weeks." — https://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/fda-roundup-october-18-2024 — 2024-10-18
- **Import sites as Baxter reported them.** "FDA announced Oct. 9 that it has authorized temporary importation for five Baxter manufacturing facilities in Canada, China (two sites), Ireland and the UK. Temporary importation authorization across these sites includes 19 IV solution and dialysis product codes." — https://www.baxter.com/sites/baxtercorpna/files/2026-05/update_10-14_0.pdf — 2024-10-14. Product also came from existing FDA-approved Baxter sites in "Mexico and Spain" (update_10-17.pdf).
- **Airlift scale (a Baxter projection, later reported as achieved).** On Oct 17 Baxter projected "approximately 200 747 airplanes full of product ... nearly 18,000 tons of product from Europe and Asia" by year-end (update_10-17.pdf, 2024-10-17). The May 2025 fact sheet reports "~200 airplanes delivered product to the U.S. by the end of 2024, representing nearly 18,000 tons" and "9 plants activated across our global network".
- **Extended use dates (as Baxter reported them).** "50+ IV and irrigation codes received FDA authorizations for extended use dates" — fact sheet, 2025-05-13.

## 5. Hospital impact (rationing and surgeries)

- **AHA, Oct 7: shortages and supplier rationing.** "Baxter and all other suppliers of these IV solutions have put their customers on strict ordering allocations and are not accepting new customers. As a result, our members are already reporting substantial shortages" — AHA letter PDF, URL above — 2024-10-07
- **ASHP survey (401 respondents, fielded Oct 16–25, 2024).** "84% of all respondents reported a moderate or critical impact of the fluid shortages. A critical impact was defined as requiring the delay or cancelation of treatments or procedures". Also: "Nearly half of respondents responsible for purchasing and inventory management (47%) reported their current inventory of large-volume fluids are down to two weeks' supply or less." In the strategy table, "Canceling or delaying voluntary surgeries and procedures" shows 17% of all respondents, 19% at Baxter-supplied facilities and 7% at non-Baxter facilities. The column mapping is taken from the PDF text layout and should be checked visually. — https://news.ashp.org/-/media/assets/drug-shortages/docs/2024/ASHP-Sterile-Fluid-Shortage.pdf — Oct 2024

## 6. Baxter financial impact (SEC filings)

- **Q3 2024 10-Q.** "In the third quarter of 2024, we recorded $25 million of pre-tax net charges related to damages caused by Hurricane Helene. This consisted of $44 million related to the write-off of damaged inventory and fixed assets ... offset by a receivable of $19 million". Also: "On November 1, 2024, we received $101 million of cash related to insurance proceeds". — https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/10456/000162828024047221/bax-20240930.htm — filed Nov 2024
- **Q3 2024 results release (8-K Ex. 99.1), which is guidance only.** "Baxter expects total company fourth-quarter sales to be negatively impacted by approximately $200 million", made up of $40–50M in Kidney Care and $150–160M in Medical Products & Therapies. Adjusted EPS was expected to fall "$0.15 to $0.20 per share". — https://investor.baxter.com/investors/events-and-news/news/press-release-details/2024/Baxter-Reports-Third-Quarter-2024-Results/default.aspx — 2024-11-08
- **FY2024 10-K (actual results).** "Hurricane Helene had an estimated $110 million adverse impact on sales" (MD&A, MP&T segment). "Our results in 2024 included pre-tax net charges of $110 million related damages caused by Hurricane Helene," made up of "$44 million related to the write-off of damaged inventory and fixed assets" and "$317 million of remediation, idle facility, air freight and other costs," "partially offset by $251 million of insurance recoveries." The facility "was not fully operational for most of the fourth quarter." Baxter expected "an estimated $50 million of charges in the first quarter of 2025." — https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/10456/000162828025007201/bax-20241231.htm — filed Feb 2025
- **10-K risk-factor language on single sites.** "Some of our products are manufactured at a single manufacturing facility or stored at a single storage site." and "Loss or damage to, or closure of, a manufacturing facility or storage site due to a natural disaster ... such as we experienced as a result of Hurricane Helene". — same 10-K
- **Two figures that are coincidentally the same.** The $110M sales hit and the $110M net pre-tax charge are different line items. Check: $44M + $317M − $251M = $110M. The Q4 sales hit came in at about $110M, against the roughly $200M guided in November.

## 7. The other suppliers: B. Braun and ICU Medical

- **ICU Medical's FY2024 10-K.** "Our IV Solutions products are sold in the U.S. and Canada and compete in the U.S. with Baxter and B. Braun." and "Our IV Solutions are manufactured at our manufacturing facility in Austin, Texas or our suppliers' facilities." Also: "a competitor's U.S. IV solutions manufacturing facility was damaged as a result of Hurricane Helene ... causing a national shortage of IV solutions" and "We experienced increased demand for our IV Solutions product lines in the fourth quarter of 2024". Vital Care revenue (the line that includes IV solutions) was $690.7M in 2024, up from $661.0M. — https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/883984/000088398425000007/icui-20241231.htm — filed Feb 2025. This supports the "three main U.S. makers" framing.
- **B. Braun's statement.** B. Braun's IV fluids plants are in "Irvine, CA and Daytona Beach, FL"; "In the coming weeks, production levels at those facilities are expected to increase by 20 percent." Its Daytona Beach site was "not seriously impacted by Hurricane Milton". — https://www.bbraunusa.com/en/about-us/media/company-news/2024/b--braun-takes-steps-to-secure-the-supply-of-critical-iv-fluids-.html — 2024-10-18

## 8. Why IV fluids have thin margins and few makers

- **FDA Drug Shortages Task Force report, "Drug Shortages: Root Causes and Potential Solutions".** It was published **October 2019 and updated 2/21/2020** (not 2022). The three root causes: (1) "Lack of incentives for manufacturers to produce less profitable drugs"; (2) "The market does not recognize and reward manufacturers for 'mature quality systems'"; (3) "Logistical and regulatory challenges make it difficult for the market to recover from a disruption." — https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-shortages/report-drug-shortages-root-causes-and-potential-solutions (PDF: https://www.fda.gov/media/131130/download)
  - **Shortage drugs are mostly cheap, old injectables.** "Of the 163 drugs in the sample, 63 percent (103) were drugs administered by injection ('sterile injectables') ... median time since first approval of almost 35 years ... In the year prior to going into shortage, the median per unit price was $8.73 for all the shortage drugs, $11.05 for injectables".
  - **Some generics sell below cost.** "some generic drugs have low profit margins or are even selling for less than they cost to produce. Their sponsors may not have resources to invest in manufacturing or redundant capacity".
  - **ASPR's section on 2017.** "disasters can exacerbate shortage issues, such as the shortage of intravenous (IV) saline solution, which became more acute during the 2017 hurricane season."
- **GAO-14-194, "Drug Shortages: Public Health Threat Continues, Despite Efforts To Help Ensure Product Availability".** "Many shortages are of generic sterile injectable drugs"; "low profit margins have limited infrastructure investments"; and other makers are limited "due to constrained manufacturing capacity." — https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-14-194 — 2014-02-10
- **ASPE issue brief, "Economic Analysis of the Causes of Drug Shortages".** "neither the quantity of these products needed by consumers nor the quantity produced by manufacturers is very responsive to short term changes in price"; "classwide shortages are affecting the sterile injectables segment". — https://aspe.hhs.gov/sites/default/files/migrated_legacy_files//57791/ib.pdf — October 2011

## 9. Precedent: Hurricane Maria, Puerto Rico, 2017

- **FDA Report on Drug Shortages for Calendar Year 2017.** "Sterile saline solution has been intermittently in shortage for several years as manufacturing capacity has worked to keep up with demand. Hurricane Maria disrupted the manufacturing of sterile saline solution at the Baxter Healthcare Corporation manufacturing facility located in Puerto Rico, resulting in a worsening of the availability of sterile saline solution in the United States." FDA "did not object to the temporary importation of sterile saline solutions ... manufactured at Baxter Healthcare Facilities located in Ireland, Australia, Mexico, Canada, and Brazil" or from "a B. Braun Medical, Inc. manufacturing facility located in Germany," and it approved saline from "Fresenius Kabi USA, LLC and Laboratorios Grifols, S.A." — https://www.fda.gov/files/drugs/published/Fifth-Annual-Report-on-Drug-Shortages-for-Calendar-Year-2017.pdf — published 2018 (no exact date on the PDF)
- **Script angle.** The same company, the same product and the same fix (temporary imports) happened seven years apart.

## Flags / not verified

1. **The "60%" is an AHA figure, and only the AHA uses it.** Baxter's releases, 10-Q and 10-K, and the FDA pages I could fetch never give a percentage. Baxter only says "largest manufacturer ... in the United States." On screen, attribute it as "according to the American Hospital Association." The AHA's wording also shifts: "60% of the country's supply" on Oct 2, and "approximately 60% ... of the IV solutions used every day" on Oct 7. Use "about 60%". No methodology is given, so treat it as an estimate.
2. **The 1.5 million bags/day figure (AHA) conflicts with Baxter's "1M+ IV solutions produced at the site every day"** in the May 2025 fact sheet. They could both be true, since 1.5M is more than 1M, but they are not the same claim. Prefer "more than a million bags a day" and attribute it to Baxter.
3. **Title wording.** "60% of America's IV bags" broadens the AHA claim, which is about IV solutions. The site also makes peritoneal dialysis and irrigation fluids. Consider "IV fluids".
4. **The "2022 FDA Root Causes report" does not exist under that date.** The report is from October 2019 and was updated February 2020. Correct the citation in the script.
5. **HHS/ASPR pages were not verified.** hhs.gov and aspe.hhs.gov returned 403 to both fetch tools. The Becerra letter and statements (Oct 9, 11, 18, 24, 31) are known only from search-index summaries, including the "50% more product" claim and the Defense Production Act orders. Do not quote them until someone opens the pages in a browser. The ASPE 2023 report "Policy Considerations to Prevent Drug Shortages" was not fetched either.
6. **FDA's dedicated page "Hurricane Helene: Baxter's manufacturing recovery in North Carolina" now returns 404**, and so does FDA's 2024 hurricane hub. Its content is known only from search snippets: a 2/21/2025 entry, a list of products in shortage (D5W, D10W, normal saline, sterile water and others), and extended use dates. The FDA Roundups of Oct 11 and Oct 18 were verified. The current FDA shortage-database status of these products was not checked.
7. **Hurricane Maria FDA statements.** The Gottlieb statements (Nov 17, 2017, Jan 2018), the Oct 24, 2017 testimony and the FDA "FDA works to help relieve IV fluid shortages" page all returned 404 or 403. Only the FDA annual shortage report for CY2017 was verified. Claims about Puerto Rico's grid power, hoarding, and how long the 2017–18 saline shortage lasted are unverified.
8. **B. Braun's ~23% market share** comes from news reports quoting a spokesperson, not a B. Braun document. It is unverified. No primary source gives a share breakdown across Baxter, B. Braun and ICU Medical.
9. **Named hospitals cancelling surgeries** (e.g., Mass General Brigham deferring elective procedures Oct 13–16, 2024) are known only from news reports. MGB's newsroom page could not be dated and did not show the quote. Use ASHP's 17% figure, or cite news outlets by name.
10. **Q4 guidance ($200M) vs. the actual sales hit ($110M).** Be clear which one the script uses. The $110M actual is the better number. The 10-K says the hurricane hit cost of sales and sales, and was largely offset by $251M of insurance recoveries.
11. **Allocations being "removed for virtually all" codes** is Baxter's own claim (May 13, 2025). Independent confirmation from FDA that the shortages were resolved was not found.
12. **Q1 2025 charges.** The 10-K expected ~$50M. A later 10-Q reportedly shows $115M for the first half of 2025 (search summary only; not verified).
13. **Do not run the 60% allocation level together with the 60% supply share.** Baxter's Oct 9 allocation increase was "to 60%", a different number with the same value. Keep them separate in the script.
