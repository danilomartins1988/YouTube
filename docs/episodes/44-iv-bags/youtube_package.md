In September 2024, Hurricane Helene flooded Baxter's North Cove plant in Marion, North Carolina, a single factory that, according to the American Hospital Association, made about 60% of the IV solutions used in the U.S. each day. Within days, hospitals were put on allocation and told they could order only a fraction of their usual fluids. How did a bag of salt water become a single point of failure? We go inside the factory and the flood, the rationing in hospitals, the airlift of fluids from Baxter plants abroad, the line-by-line restart, and the economics of cheap medicine that keep creating the same weak point.

Every figure is sourced below: Baxter's Helene updates and North Cove fact sheet, the AHA, FDA roundups, the ASHP survey, Baxter's and ICU Medical's 10-K filings, B. Braun, the FDA's report on the root causes of drug shortages (2019, updated 2020), GAO-14-194 and the FDA's 2017 drug shortage report. The 60% share is an AHA estimate; recovery dates and airlift figures are Baxter's. Not medical advice; not affiliated with any company.
