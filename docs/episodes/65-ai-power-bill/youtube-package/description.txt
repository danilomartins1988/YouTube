Are AI data centers raising your electric bill? Meet Maya, a homeowner in Northern Virginia with a $150 electric bill and some very hungry new neighbors: giant AI data centers that hum day and night on the same grid as her fridge. This summer that grid bought its future electricity at the highest price its rules allow and still came up short, and the grid's own market monitor says data centers are a big reason why. We follow one household's bill through the wholesale market, the power-plant auction, the utilities' building plans and the new rules for giant customers, to answer a simple question: who really pays for the AI boom's electricity, the tech giants or you?

In this film:
- How much electricity AI data centers use, and what the forecasts say (forecasts, not facts)
- The four slices of an electric bill, and how data centers can push on each one
- Why the PJM capacity auction hit its price cap, and was still short
- Who pays for new transmission lines, and what Virginia's own cost study found
- The new minimum-bill rules for data centers in Ohio and Virginia
- Big Tech's nuclear deals, and the strongest counterpoints
- The verdict: rising, not exploding nationally, and the three dials that decide your bill

Key figures (exact values; the film rounds them)
- U.S. data centers used 176 TWh in 2023; DOE/LBNL forecast 325-580 TWh by 2028. Homes equivalent (~17 million) is our calculation using EIA's 2024 average of 863 kWh per home per month.
- PJM 2028/29 capacity auction (July 2026): $325/MW-day, at the FERC-approved cap; 6,831 MW short of the reliability requirement; $16.4 billion. 2024/25 price: $28.92/MW-day.
- PJM Market Monitor (July 2026, its estimates): $6.3B of the $16.4B (38%) and $29.4B (46%) over four auctions resulted from data center load; wholesale cost Jan-Jun 2026 $114.50/MWh vs $76.16 a year earlier; $11.11/MWh attributed to data center load. "About $11 a month" = our illustration for 1,000 kWh, if fully passed through.
- PJM 2026 load forecast: summer peak ~222 GW by 2036 (forecast), 3.6% a year vs 0.3% in the 2021 forecast.
- Planned 2026-2030 spending (2025 10-Ks): Dominion $65.7B; AEP $12.2B + $59.7B (our sum of both: $137.6B).
- Virginia SCC final order PUR-2025-00058 (Nov 2025): typical 1,000 kWh home +$11.24/month (7.5%) in 2026; ROE 9.8%; future GS-5 class earned 3.01% on transmission vs 22.41% for other large customers; GS-5: 25 MW+, 14-year contracts, minimum charges 85% (wires) / 60% (generation).
- AEP Ohio data center tariff (PUCO, July 2025): 25 MW+, pay for at least 85% of subscribed capacity, up to 12 years, exit fee.
- Disclosed deals: Microsoft-Constellation, 835 MW Crane restart, 20 years; Meta-Constellation, 1,121 MW Clinton, 20 years from June 2027; Amazon-Talen, 1,920 MW through 2042 (as reported).
- EIA STEO (Nov 2026): residential price 16.5 cents/kWh (2024) to 18.7 cents (2027 forecast), about +13% (our calculation).
Maya and Dev are fictional characters; the $150 bill split is illustrative.
