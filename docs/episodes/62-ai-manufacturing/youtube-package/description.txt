Could AI make American factories cheaper than China's? In this animated documentary about AI manufacturing, robots and the US vs China manufacturing race, Mel, who in Episode 1 could not buy a toaster that wasn't made in China, decides to build one herself, in a highly automated factory in Ohio. We tour what AI really does on a factory floor today, run an illustrative $100 three-factory experiment (a conventional Chinese plant, an automated Ohio plant, an automated Chinese plant), show which switches flip the winner, and then reveal the twist: China installs more industrial robots than the rest of the world combined. The verdict: AI doesn't end China's manufacturing dominance; it changes the test. America can win back competitiveness product by product, without winning back the same jobs, and China can automate too.

Questions we answer: Can robots make reshoring manufacturing cheaper? What can AI do in factories today, and what is still a demo? How many robots does China have, per factory and per worker? Who captures the money?

This is Episode 2 of our factory story. Episode 1: "Why the World Still Depends on Chinese Factories".

The numbers behind the story (exact figures, dated)
- Industrial robots installed in 2025: China 354,226 (59% of the world), US 38,428 (now the #2 market), world 603,307, a record; China/US ≈ 9 to 1 (our calculation). Chinese brands: 55% of China's installations in 2025, about 30% in 2019-2021. (IFR World Robotics 2026)
- Robots per 10,000 manufacturing employees, 2025: South Korea 1,066, US 323, China 193 (on a National Economic Census employment base), world 152. (IFR, Nov 2026)
- AI maturity: machine vision is "established and industry-grade"; predictive maintenance and AI inspection "increasingly used"; general-purpose physical AI "not yet industry-grade"; most humanoids are prototypes or pilots. (IFR World Robotics 2026)
- US factories, 2021: payroll ≈ 13 cents and materials ≈ 55 cents of every $1 of shipments (Census Annual Survey of Manufactures; our calculation). US factory output about doubled from June 1979 to Oct 2026 while factory jobs fell from 19.6M to 12.7M (Fed G.17, BLS; our calculation).
- US factory production workers ≈ $29/hour vs ≈ $11,200/year at larger Chinese firms, 2025: roughly five times per hour (BLS, NBS at the Fed's 2025 rate; rough, our calculation). China ≈ 27% of world manufacturing value added, 2025 (World Bank; our calculation). Japan ≈ 38% of world robot production (IFR, Jul 2025). China 85% of magnet rare-earth refining, 2025 (IEA).
- The $100 experiment is an ILLUSTRATIVE MODEL with our assumptions, not any company's costs. Totals per toaster delivered to a US warehouse: China conventional 100, Ohio automated 102, China automated 95. Tariff at 25%: 112 / 102 / 107 (Ohio wins). Ohio robots idle half the time: Ohio 116. No tariff: 91 / 102 / 87.
