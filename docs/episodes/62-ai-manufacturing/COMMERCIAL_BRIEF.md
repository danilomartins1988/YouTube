## 3. Human question
"Could America make things as cheaply as China if robots did most of the work?" and the follow-up: "and would that bring
the jobs back?"

## 4. First 30 seconds
Hypothetical Ohio plant on a Sunday afternoon: arms working, cameras checking parts, software scheduling (all capabilities
the IFR calls industry-grade or increasingly used). "Now imagine what comes out the door costs about the same as the same
product made in China. That factory is hypothetical. But almost every piece of it already exists somewhere." Red
HYPOTHETICAL banner on screen. The exact title question is asked at 0:25, then the channel intro.

## 5. Story beats (seven acts, internal only)
1. The factory that changes everything: hook, stakes, US output ×2.1 since 1979 with 35% fewer factory jobs (Fed G.17,
   BLS; our calculation).
2. The old formula (brief): one explicit line to Episode 1; China ≈ 27% of world manufacturing; US vs China pay.
3. When labor matters less: what is proven (vision, arms), growing (predictive maintenance, AI inspection, scheduling),
   not yet (general-purpose physical AI), humanoids = prototypes (IFR). Census: payroll ≈ 13¢ of each $1 of US factory
   shipments; materials ≈ 55¢. The presenter pulls labor out of the box; robots bring a bill of their own.
4. The $100 experiment (illustrative): China conventional 100, Ohio automated 102 (almost a tie); the switches (tariff 25% →
   Ohio wins; robots idle half the time → Ohio loses); China automated 95 wins the base case.
5. **Twist at the midpoint (~6:30):** China installed 354,226 industrial robots in 2025, 59% of the world, ≈ 9 for every one
   in the US; Chinese brands 55% of them; per worker China is still below the US (193 vs 323), so it has room to grow.
6. Who captures the money: the Ohio plant still imports arms, gears, sensors, chips and magnets; Japan 38% of robot
   production; China 85% of magnet rare-earth refining; illustrative value chain and money river.
7. Verdict: which products could come back, jobs evidence (Acemoglu & Restrepo), what could stop it, and the payoff.

## 7. Payoff
AI doesn't end China's manufacturing dominance; it changes the test, from "who has the cheapest workers" to "who combines
intelligent machines, reliable energy, patient capital and a complete supply chain". America can regain competitiveness
product by product without regaining the same jobs; China can automate too. No dated prediction.
