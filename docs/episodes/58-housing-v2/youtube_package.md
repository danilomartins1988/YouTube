Why Americans can't afford homes anymore comes down to simple math that broke in two places at once. The typical existing home sold for $416,700 in October 2026 (NAR), while the typical household earned a record $87,460 in 2025 (Census). With 30-year mortgage rates above 7% (Freddie Mac), buying that home takes about $108,000 a year by our calculation. We follow it like an episode of an animated sitcom with Maya, a first-time buyer: the price that climbed to almost five years of income, the rate rollercoaster that added about $900 a month to the same house, the owners locked in by cheap older loans, a decade of homes America never built, and the zoning board that decides what may be built at all. The first-time buyer is now 40.

In this video:
- Why are houses so expensive compared with incomes?
- How much more does the same house cost per month at 7% than at 2.65%?
- What is the mortgage rate lock-in effect, and why does it push prices up?
- How big is the housing shortage, and why do estimates disagree?
- How does zoning make the cheapest homes the hardest to build?
