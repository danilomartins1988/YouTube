Brazil's economy, explained through one question: if robots build the next factory, could it be built in Brazil? Meet Gus, a factory scout for a robot company, who must find the best spot on Earth for a plant where robots build robots. Because it barely needs workers, the map changes. Bia, an engineer from Minas Gerais, shows him why Brazil suddenly fits: mostly renewable power, iron ore, almost all of the world's niobium, rare earths, lithium, food and a big home market. Then, at every stop, his cost backpack gets heavier: taxes piled on taxes, paperwork, trucks, expensive loans, the "Custo Brasil". (Our answer: it could, but not with this backpack. Robots make Brazil's ingredients matter more, but costs decide where factories go; the 2023 tax reform is a start, and it runs to 2033. Conditional, not a forecast.)

Key figures (exact values; the film rounds them)
- Electricity: renewables were 86.8% of Brazil's electricity matrix in 2025 (88.2% in 2024) (EPE, Brazilian Energy Balance 2026).
- Iron ore, 2025 estimate: Australia 980 Mt, Brazil 420 Mt (second), India 310 Mt, China 290 Mt of usable ore; Brazil supplied 58% of US iron ore imports in 2021-24 (USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries 2026).
- Niobium: Brazil ≈93% of world production (104,000 t of 112,000 t, 2025 estimate); most comes from CBMM's mine at Araxá, Minas Gerais (USGS; CBMM).
- Rare earths: reserves China 44 Mt, Brazil 11 Mt (second), Australia 6.3 Mt; Brazil mined ≈2,000 t in 2025 vs China 270,000 t (USGS).
- Lithium: Brazil 12,000 t (lithium content, 2025), from Minas Gerais (USGS).
- Soybeans: Brazil is the world's largest exporter (USDA FAS).
- Population: 213.4 million (IBGE estimate, July 1, 2025).
- Manufacturing: 11.8% of 2025 GDP, 13.7% of value added (our calculation from IBGE quarterly national accounts, current prices); 13.1% of GDP in 1996.
- Freight: roads carry 65.8% of Brazil's cargo (CNT Transport and Logistics Plan 2026, an industry study). World Bank Logistics Performance Index 2023: Brazil 3.2, rank 51.
- Tax paperwork: 1,501 hours a year, the highest in the world, in the World Bank's last Doing Business comparison (2020 edition, 2018 data; the series was discontinued).
- Policy rate: Selic 13.75% (Banco Central do Brasil, held on Nov 4, 2026).
- "Custo Brasil": producing in Brazil costs companies R$1.7 trillion a year more than the OECD average, about 19.5% of GDP (Movimento Brasil Competitivo with the Ministry of Development, Industry and Trade, FGV update, 2023; an industry estimate, also cited by CNI). Our conversion: ≈US$304 billion.
- Tax reform: Constitutional Amendment 132/2023 and Complementary Law 214/2025 create a dual VAT (CBS federal, IBS state and municipal) with credits, replacing five consumption taxes; test year 2026, CBS in full 2027, ICMS and ISS phased down 2029-2032 and extinguished in 2033.
- Camaçari, Bahia: Ford idled its plant in January 2021; BYD took over the site and began production on July 1, 2025 (company announcements).
- Data centers: Law 15,504/2026 (Redata), a special tax regime for data centers supplied by renewable or low-emission power.
Gus, Bia, the boss and Boltwell Robotics are fictional. The film takes no political side: one argument for, and the obstacles. USD only: BRL at the Federal Reserve G.5A 2025 average (5.5891 per USD).
