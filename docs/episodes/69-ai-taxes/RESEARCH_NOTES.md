## Who pays Washington (Treasury, primary)
- Monthly Treasury Statement, Table 4, FY to Sep 30, 2026, via `api.fiscaldata.treasury.gov` (`mts_table_4`, record_date 2026-09-30):
  total net receipts $5,403.3bn; individual income taxes $2,845.5bn; social insurance and retirement receipts $1,811.8bn (of which
  employment and general retirement, i.e. Social Security and Medicare payroll taxes, $1,754.3bn; unemployment insurance $49.1bn; other
  retirement $8.4bn); corporation income taxes $381.4bn; customs duties $173.2bn; excise $106.0bn; off-budget (Social Security)
  $1,341.5bn.
- FY2025 comparison: total $5,234.6bn; individual $2,656.0bn; social insurance $1,748.3bn; corporate $452.1bn.
- OUR CALCULATION: (2,845.5 + 1,811.8) / 5,403.3 = 86.2% ("almost nine out of every ten dollars"); corporate 381.4 / 5,403.3 = 7.1%
  ("about seven cents"). Caveat kept in FACT_CHECK: individual income tax also taxes interest, dividends and capital gains, so "from
  people" (income and paychecks), not "from wages" alone.
- CBO Monthly Budget Review, Summary for FY2026 (Nov 2026): deficit $2.0 trillion (+$217bn); individual income taxes the largest
  source; corporate receipts fell (larger deductions for certain investments, July 2025 law). Read via CBO page excerpts (403).

## Social Security (Trustees 2026)
- Combined OASI+DI reserves depleted Q3 2034 (same as 2025 report); then ~83% of scheduled benefits payable (81% last year); OASI alone
  Q4 2032 (78%). Source: 2026 Trustees Report summary (ssa.gov 403) confirmed in the American Academy of Actuaries 2026 one-pager (PDF

- Payroll-tax link: Social Security's income is mainly payroll taxes; Treasury MTS shows off-budget receipts (OASDI payroll taxes)
  $1,341.5bn in FY2026.

## Tax mix across rich countries (OECD Revenue Statistics 2025, 2023 data)
- Social security contributions 25.5% of total tax revenue (largest category; OECD page excerpt); personal income tax 23.7% and VAT
  20.5% (Accounting Times citing the report); personal + corporate income taxes 36.4% (OECD excerpt), so corporate ≈ 12.7% (our calc).
  Tax-to-GDP 34.1% in 2024 (preliminary record). Spoken as "about half" (23.7 + 25.5 = 49.2%, our calc).
- The US is the only OECD country without a national VAT (OECD Consumption Tax Trends).

## Proposals (neutral)
- Robot tax: Bill Gates, Quartz interview, Feb 17, 2017 ("you'd think that we'd tax the robot at a similar level"), via Fortune/WEF
  coverage. European Parliament rejected a robot-tax proposal on Feb 16, 2017 (same coverage).
- South Korea, Aug 2017 tax revision plan: reduced the tax credit for automation-equipment investment (then 3-7%) by up to 2 points;
  not a tax on robots; nicknamed the first robot tax (Korea Times, Aug 7, 2017). Later accounts disagree on the exact post-cut rates
  (Korea Times 2025 op-ed; ITIF 2026), so only the direction is spoken. ITIF (advocacy) cites Hotmann et al. (2025): robot
  installations -28% vs Japan, insignificant employment effect (not spoken).
- Profit shifting: OECD BEPS (mechanism; 15% global minimum tax).
- Consumption tax regressivity: CBO budget option 54820 (VAT likely regressive; lower-income families spend a larger share).
- Alaska PFD: PFD Division (Ep 48 FACT_CHECK carries amounts; none repeated here).

## Cash pilots (verified results only)
- OpenResearch / NBER w32719 (Vivalt et al., July 2024, revised Sept 2025; PDF read): 1,000 people $1,000/month for 3 years vs 2,000
  controls at $50/month; income excluding transfers -$1,800/yr; labor force participation -3.9 points; hours -1 to -2 a week; leisure up;
  no job-quality gain; spending up (non-durables); well-being up in year 1 then reverts. (July 2024 version: -$1,500, -2.0 points,
  -1.3 to -1.4 hours; the newer revision is used.)
- Stockton SEED (first-year analysis, Mar 2021): 125 residents, $500/month for 24 months from Feb 2019; full-time employment 28% -> 40%
  vs control 32% -> 37%; preliminary, small sample.
