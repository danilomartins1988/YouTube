## Narration in the Short

> In Stockton, California, a pilot sent a small group of residents $500 a month, no strings attached. Plenty of critics expected people to stop working. Instead, after a year, more of them had full-time jobs than in a comparison group that got nothing. Then came a much bigger, carefully controlled study, run by a research group called OpenResearch. It sent low-income people a thousand dollars a month, for years, and compared them with people who got almost nothing. This time, people worked a little less, on average about an hour or two a week. They spent more on everyday needs, and they had more choices. But the money didn't lead to better jobs, and the happiness boost faded. So, a cushion, not a cure.
