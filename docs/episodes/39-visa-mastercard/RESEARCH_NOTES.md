# Ep 39 — How Visa and Mastercard Became Financial Gatekeepers: research notes

Research date: 2026-11-10. American English, US dollars.
Scope: history, network effects, competition, regulation. Episode 30 already covered swipe-fee mechanics (interchange 1–3%, $160bn in fees, margins, rewards), so this episode does not repeat them.

Method: each bullet was read on the page or document linked. SEC filings were downloaded from EDGAR, and court and DOJ PDFs were text-extracted and searched. "My calc" marks arithmetic of mine that is not printed in the source. Anything not verified on a primary page is in the **Flags** section at the end.

## 1. Origins

### BankAmericard (1958) and the Fresno drop
- **"Visa U.S.A. traces its history back to 1958, when Bank of America launched its blue, white and gold BankAmericard."**
  - URL: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1403161/000119312507200042/ds4a.htm (Visa Inc. Form S-4/A, restructuring proxy-prospectus)
  - Date: filed September 13, 2007
- **"Bank of America launches BankAmericard, the first card with a 'revolving credit' feature"** (1958). The same timeline says 1976: **"BankAmericard becomes Visa–a name that sounds the same in all languages."**
  - URL: https://corporate.visa.com/en/about-visa.html (Visa corporate "About Visa" timeline)
  - Date: undated page; its stats are labeled as of mid/late 2025
- **"The origins of the Visa network, for example, trace to 1958 when Bank of America launched BankAmericard in California."**
  - URL: https://www.federalreservehistory.org/essays/electronic-point-of-sale-payments (Federal Reserve History, "Electronic Point-of-Sale Payments")
  - Date: September 25, 2024
- **"In 1958, Bank of America began operating the BankAmericard credit card system, the predecessor of Visa, as a unitary system."** The paper says Bank of America did both the issuing (cardholder) and acquiring (merchant) work itself.
  - URL: https://www.kansascityfed.org/Payments%20Conferences/documents/7409/PSCP2005_Evans-Schmalensee.pdf (Evans & Schmalensee, "The Economics of Interchange Fees and Their Regulation: An Overview," Kansas City Fed payments conference volume)
  - Date: 2005 conference. Note that the authors were industry economists; Evans had consulted for Visa.
- **Fresno mailing, secondary source only.** "In September 1958, Bank of America decided to try an experiment." It "sent out 60,000 pieces of mail to people in Fresno." "Bank of America had recruited about 300 merchants in Fresno." The program "lost about $20 million" (placed in about 1959).
  - URL: https://99percentinvisible.org/episode/the-fresno-drop/transcript (99% Invisible, ep. 196, "The Fresno Drop")
  - Date: January 19, 2016
  - **This is not a primary source.** See Flags.

### 1966: licensing and Interbank
- **"Bank of America began licensing the credit card to other banks in the U.S. and abroad in 1966."**
  - URL: https://www.federalreservehistory.org/essays/electronic-point-of-sale-payments
  - Date: September 25, 2024
- **"In 1966, Bank of America began to bring other banks into the system as franchisees."** Under the original rule the acquiring bank had to pass the full merchant discount to the issuer. That "blunted the incentives for all banks to sign up merchants."
  - URL: https://www.kansascityfed.org/Payments%20Conferences/documents/7409/PSCP2005_Evans-Schmalensee.pdf
  - Date: 2005
- **"The MasterCard network began in 1966 under the name Interbank, a cooperative formed by several banks in New York."**
  - URL: https://www.federalreservehistory.org/essays/electronic-point-of-sale-payments
  - Date: September 25, 2024
- **"Our business originated in 1966 when a number of banks in the United States formed the Interbank Card Association."** The same passage says that by 2006 Mastercard served "nearly 25,000 financial institutions in 210 countries and territories."
  - URL: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1141391/000119312506097358/ds1a.htm (MasterCard Inc. Form S-1/A)
  - Date: May 3, 2006

### Master Charge and the rename to MasterCard
- **"We were established in 1966 when a group of banks with proprietary or regional card systems formed the Interbank Card Association ("ICA"). In 1968, ICA began to develop a global network by forming associations and alliances with banks outside the United States. In 1969, ICA acquired exclusive rights to the "MasterCharge" name and the interlocking circles device for its members. After over a decade of expansion, ICA changed its name and the name of its trademarks to MasterCard..."**
  - URL: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1141391/000095012301505657/y49195s-4.txt (MasterCard Inc. Form S-4)
  - Date: filed August 15, 2001
  - Note: this filing gives **1969** for Master Charge, not 1966–67. It does **not** give the year of the MasterCard rename. See Flags for 1979.

### 1970: member-owned association and Dee Hock
- **"In 1970, Bank of America transferred ownership of the BankAmericard program to the financial institutions that issued the card, forming National BankAmericard Inc., a Delaware non–stock corporation. In 1977, National BankAmericard Inc. changed its name to Visa U.S.A. Inc."**
  - URL: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1403161/000119312507200042/ds4a.htm
  - Date: September 13, 2007
- **"In 1974, National BankAmericard Inc. incorporated IBANCO Ltd. as a non–stock corporation in Delaware to administer the BankAmericard program internationally. In 1977, IBANCO Ltd. changed its name to Visa International Service Association, National BankAmericard changed its name to Visa U.S.A. Inc., and the BankAmericard brand was changed to Visa."**
  - URL: same S-4/A
  - Date: September 13, 2007
  - Note: Visa's own SEC filing says **1977** for the legal name and brand change. Visa's marketing pages and the Fed say **1976**. See Flags.
- **"BankAmericard was spun off into its own company in 1970 and rebranded as Visa in 1976."**
  - URL: https://www.federalreservehistory.org/essays/electronic-point-of-sale-payments
  - Date: September 25, 2024
- **"In 1970 the BankAmericard system was converted into a membership corporation, a multiparty system. This cooperative association established an interchange fee in 1971 ... initially set at 1.95 percent."**
  - URL: https://www.kansascityfed.org/Payments%20Conferences/documents/7409/PSCP2005_Evans-Schmalensee.pdf
  - Date: 2005
  - This is useful for the origin of interchange as a cooperative's internal price. Do not go into Ep 30 mechanics.
- **Dee Hock.** He "gathered a hand-picked group of bankers to a hotel in Sausalito in 1969." "Dee was charged with redefining the struggling BankAmericard program." For the 1976 name change he "turned to his internal team, via a contest, to surface Visa." "Dee left Visa in 1984." He died "at age 93 at his home in Olympia, Washington."
  - URL: https://corporate.visa.com/en/sites/visa-perspectives/company-news/dee-hock-in-memoriam.html (Visa, "Dee Hock: In memoriam")
  - Date: July 20, 2022
- **"Although Visa's history dates back as far as 1958 with the launch of the first BankAmericard, the Visa brand itself was first formed in 1976. At that time it consisted of two companies: Visa USA, which served the US market under the leadership of Dee Hock, and Visa International which served the rest of world. In 2007 the company restructured to form Visa Inc...."**
  - URL: https://usa.visa.com/dam/VCOM/download/corporate/media/visa-inc-factsheet.pdf (Visa Inc. fact sheet)
  - Date: undated
- **Both networks began as bank co-ops, which matters for network effects.** "Visa and MasterCard began as bank cooperatives and thus almost every bank that offers credit cards is in the Visa or MasterCard network."
  - URL: https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/17pdf/16-1454_5h26.pdf (Ohio v. American Express, majority opinion)
  - Date: June 25, 2018

### First antitrust fight over interchange (NaBanco)
- **"Until 1979, few outside the Visa and MasterCard systems had any idea what an interchange fee was. In that year, the National Bancard Corporation (NaBanco) filed a lawsuit contending that when the Visa member banks determined the interchange fee, those banks engaged in illegal price fixing ... An appeals court found in favor of Visa in 1986."**
  - URL: https://www.kansascityfed.org/Payments%20Conferences/documents/7409/PSCP2005_Evans-Schmalensee.pdf
  - Date: 2005

## 4. Regulation and antitrust

### 4a. DOJ v. Visa USA and MasterCard (1998–2004): the exclusionary rules
- **What was challenged.** "Count II challenged Visa's Bylaw 2.10(e) and MasterCard's Competitive Programs Policy (CPP)." These "exclusionary rules" let member banks issue Visa, MasterCard and bank-controlled cards but barred them from issuing Amex or Discover cards. The district court found that, as intended, the rules meant "no bank in the continental United States has agreed to issue American Express" or Discover cards.
  - URL: https://www.justice.gov/osg/brief/visa-usa-inc-v-united-states-opposition
  - Date: August 2004
- **Remedy.** The district court (163 F. Supp. 2d 322) "ordered Visa and MasterCard to repeal their exclusionary rules" and to refrain from "enacting, maintaining, or enforcing" similar rules.
  - URL: same brief
- **Second Circuit.** "The judgment of the court of appeals was entered on September 17, 2003" (344 F.3d 229). The court held: "the defendants have failed to show that the anticompetitive effects of their exclusionary rules are outweighed by procompetitive benefits."
  - URL: same brief
- **Mastercard is also under investigation.** "In 2023, Mastercard received a Civil Investigative Demand ('CID') from the U.S. Department of Justice Antitrust Division ... The CID focuses on Mastercard's U.S. debit program and competition with other payment networks and technologies."
  - URL: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1141391/000114139126000013/ma-20251231.htm
  - Date: February 11, 2026

### 4b. Durbin Amendment (2010) and Regulation II
- **Statute.** "section 920 of the Dodd-Frank Act required it [the Fed] to issue regulations on debit card fees."
  - URL: https://www.federalreservehistory.org/essays/electronic-point-of-sale-payments
  - Date: September 25, 2024
- **What Durbin requires on routing (DOJ description).** "Congress passed the Durbin Amendment, which requires that each debit card support at least two unaffiliated debit networks—one on the front of the card and at least one on the back of the card."
  - URL: https://www.justice.gov/atr/media/1400051/dl (US Opposition to Visa's Motion to Dismiss)
  - Date: January 19, 2025
- **Current cap (Fed).** An issuer "may not receive, for any electronic debit transaction, an interchange fee that exceeds $0.21 plus 0.05 percent multiplied by the value of the transaction, plus a $0.01 fraud-prevention adjustment, if eligible."
  - URL: https://www.federalreserve.gov/paymentsystems/regii-average-interchange-fee.htm
  - Date: last updated December 19, 2025
  - The same page gives 2024 data. The average covered (regulated) debit interchange fee was $0.23, or 0.47% of the transaction. Exempt issuers averaged $0.51 (1.21%). Across all transactions, the average transaction was $46.32.
- **The 2023 proposal.** For an average $50 debit transaction, the cap "would decline from 24.5 cents under the current rule to 17.7 cents under the proposal."
  - URL: https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/bcreg20231025a.htm
  - Date: October 25, 2023
  - The proposed parameters are 14.4¢ + 4.0 bps + 1.3¢. I saw these only in search summaries of the Federal Register notice (88 FR 78100, Nov 14, 2023); I did not read them on the page.
- **Status: not finalized as of the latest filings.**
  - The Fed's Regulation II page (last updated May 17, 2024) still lists only the October 25, 2023 proposal, with no final rule: https://www.federalreserve.gov/paymentsystems/regii-about.htm
  - Visa's FY2026 10-K (filed Nov 5, 2026) still describes the Fed as "proposing further rate reductions with automatic biennial adjustments." It says "Recent conflicting federal court decisions on the validity of Regulation II's interchange fee standard have created additional uncertainty and could result in a significantly lower interchange cap for debit transactions." https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1403161/000140316126000134/v-20260930.htm
  - Mastercard's FY2025 10-K (filed Feb 11, 2026) says: "In August and September of 2025, two separate Federal district courts issued conflicting decisions regarding the validity of the interchange cap set by the Federal Reserve in Regulation II ... One court vacated the interchange cap while the other upheld it. The interchange cap remains in effect while the litigation continues." https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1141391/000114139126000013/ma-20251231.htm
- **Court detail, from a law-firm source (secondary).** In *Corner Post, Inc. v. Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System*, D.N.D., ruling of August 6, 2025, the court "vacated the entirety of Regulation II" but stayed the vacatur pending appeal. The aim was "to prevent interchange transaction fees from becoming a completely unregulated market."
  - URL: https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2025/2025-08-15-district-court-vacates-regulation-iis-debit-card-interchange-fee-standard
  - Date: August 15, 2025
  - Secondary source. The scope ("entirety" versus only the fee standard) conflicts with Mastercard's wording. See Flags.

### 4c. US v. Visa (DOJ, filed Sept 24, 2024): debit monopolization. All of these are allegations.
- **Filing (Visa's description).** "On September 24, 2024, the U.S. Department of Justice filed a complaint in the U.S. District Court for the Southern District of New York against Visa alleging violations of the Sherman Act. The complaint alleges Visa has monopolized and attempted to monopolize general purpose debit network services and card-not-present debit network services in the United States through agreements with merchants, acquirers, and others..."
  - URL: Visa FY2026 10-K, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1403161/000140316126000134/v-20260930.htm
  - Date: November 5, 2026
- **Share and fees (alleged).** More than 60% of US debit transactions run on Visa's network, and Visa charges "over $7 billion in fees each year." The complaint also alleges: "Visa charges over $7 billion in network fees on U.S. debit volume annually, earning Visa $5.6 billion in net revenue." "Visa debit is core to its North American business, where Visa enjoys operating margins of 83%."
  - URL: https://www.justice.gov/atr/media/1370481/dl (Complaint)
  - Date: September 24, 2024
- **"Moat" language.** The complaint quotes Visa recognizing that network effects create "an enormous moat" around its business. It says "Visa's market shares demonstrate just how deep that moat is."
  - URL: same complaint
- **Status.** "On June 23, 2025, the court denied a motion to dismiss filed by Visa." Judge John G. Koeltl wrote: "Visa's motion to dismiss is denied."
  - Visa 10-K: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1403161/000140316126000134/v-20260930.htm (Nov 5, 2026)
  - Opinion: https://www.justice.gov/atr/media/1407066/dl (June 23, 2025)
  - Visa's FY2026 10-K reports no later ruling, settlement or trial date. The case is still pending as of November 5, 2026.
- **Related securities suit.** A shareholder class action based on the DOJ allegations was dismissed without leave to amend on June 29, 2026.
  - URL: Visa FY2026 10-K

### 4d. Merchant class action: In re Payment Card Interchange Fee and Merchant Discount Antitrust Litigation (MDL 1720, E.D.N.Y., filed 2005)
All of the following come from the Visa FY2026 10-K (Note 20, filed November 5, 2026; https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1403161/000140316126000134/v-20260930.htm) unless another source is given.
- **2012 settlement.** "Pursuant to the 2012 Settlement Agreement, the Company deposited approximately $ 4.0 billion from the U.S. litigation escrow account and approximately $ 500 million attributable to interchange reductions..." The Second Circuit later "vacated the district court's certification of the merchant class, reversed the approval of the settlement and remanded." The case was then split into a "Damages Class" and an "Injunctive Relief Class."
- **Damages Class settlement (2018, approved 2019).** "On September 17, 2018, Visa, Mastercard and certain U.S. financial institutions reached an agreement ... (Amended Settlement Agreement)." It "required an additional settlement payment from all defendants totaling $ 900 million, with the Company's share of $ 600 million ... The additional settlement payment was added to the approximately $ 5.3 billion previously deposited into settlement accounts by the defendants pursuant to the 2012 Settlement Agreement."
  - "On December 13, 2019, the district court granted final approval of the Amended Settlement Agreement ... Based on the percentage of class members (by payment volume) that opted out of the class, $ 700 million was returned to defendants."
  - "On March 15, 2023, the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Second Circuit affirmed the final approval."
  - My calc: $5.3bn + $0.9bn − $0.7bn = about **$5.5bn net**. The popular "$5.5bn" or "$5.54bn" figure is not printed as a single number in the 10-K.
  - On September 29, 2026, the district court found the settlement's forward-looking release valid. Three merchants filed a notice of appeal on October 1, 2026.
- **Injunctive Relief Class.** It was certified on September 27, 2021, "without opt out rights," covering all merchants accepting Visa or Mastercard in the US since December 18, 2020. The plaintiffs sought an injunction against default interchange, "the honor-all-cards rule," and other rules and fees.
- **2024 settlement rejected.** "On March 25, 2024, Visa and Mastercard entered into a settlement agreement to resolve the Injunctive Relief Class claims. Following the agreement, the plaintiffs filed a motion for preliminary approval of the settlement, which was denied on June 25, 2024."
- **2025 revised settlement.** "On November 10, 2025, Visa and Mastercard entered into a superseding amended settlement agreement to resolve the Injunctive Relief Class claims, and the plaintiffs moved for preliminary approval of the settlement, which was granted on June 9, 2026. On July 15, 2026, the Injunctive Relief Class plaintiffs filed a motion for final approval of the settlement."
  - **Status as of Nov 5, 2026: preliminary approval granted. Final approval is pending.**
- **Terms of the Nov 10, 2025 settlement (Visa 8-K):**
  - "Credit surcharging. Merchants will have more options to surcharge, including if they do not surcharge other credit networks."
  - "Honor All Cards. Merchants will be able to choose whether to accept U.S. credit cards in distinct categories—commercial, premium consumer and standard consumer."
  - "Lower interchange. The settlement provides for a reduction of the U.S. combined average effective credit interchange rate by 10 bps for five years."
  - "Interchange rate certainty. The settlement will cap posted U.S. credit interchange rates for five years. In addition, standard U.S. consumer credit rates will be capped at 125 bps through the term of the agreement."
  - "The settlement agreement is subject to approval by the court."
  - URL: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1403161/000140316125000093/v-20251110.htm (Visa Form 8-K)
  - Date: November 10, 2025
- **Opt-out merchants are still suing.** "Since May 2013, more than 50 cases have been filed ... by hundreds of merchants." Visa's estimate of interchange at issue in unresolved damages claims was about $15.9bn as of October 1, 2026, down from about $17.4bn as of May 11, 2026. Visa's litigation accrual was $623m as of September 30, 2026.
- Mastercard's account: "In November 2025, the parties reached a revised settlement agreement that, if approved by the court, would resolve the litigation." Mastercard had accrued "$ 637 million" for the US MDL cases as of December 31, 2025.
  - URL: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1141391/000114139126000013/ma-20251231.htm
  - Date: February 11, 2026

## 5. Network effects and the two-sided market

- **Supreme Court (syllabus).** Amex operates "what economists call a 'two-sided platform,' providing services to two different groups (cardholders and merchants) who depend on the platform to intermediate between them. Because the interaction between the two groups is a transaction, credit-card networks are a special type of two-sided platform known as a 'transaction' platform. The key feature of transaction platforms is that they cannot make a sale to one side of the platform without simultaneously making a sale to the other." It continues: "two-sided platforms exhibit 'indirect network effects,' which exist where the value of the platform to one group depends on how many members of another group participate."
  - URL: https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/17pdf/16-1454_5h26.pdf (Ohio v. American Express Co., No. 16-1454; argued Feb 26, 2018)
  - Date: decided June 25, 2018. The opinion was by Justice Thomas, 5–4; Justice Breyer dissented, joined by Ginsburg, Sotomayor and Kagan.
- **The same opinion on why Visa and Mastercard are entrenched.** "This makes it very likely that the average consumer carries, and the average merchant accepts, Visa or MasterCard. As a result, the vast majority of Amex cardholders have a Visa or MasterCard, but only a small number of Visa and MasterCard cardholders have an Amex."
- **DOJ complaint (allegation).** "...the more issuers that place a network on a card, and therefore, the more accountholders who may present the debit network for payment, the more likely it is that merchants will accept the network, and vice versa. This feedback loop is known as network effects."
  - URL: https://www.justice.gov/atr/media/1370481/dl
  - Date: September 24, 2024
- **DOJ brief.** Success in debit "depends on 'network effects,' a feedback loop in which small networks face the 'herculean task' of simultaneously gaining widespread acceptance by merchants and widespread enablement by issuers."
  - URL: https://www.justice.gov/atr/media/1400051/dl
  - Date: January 19, 2025
- **Economists' framing.** "There are typically positive indirect network externalities between the two groups." The paper's example: the more men who use a dating service, the more attractive it is to women, and vice versa.
  - URL: https://www.kansascityfed.org/Payments%20Conferences/documents/7409/PSCP2005_Evans-Schmalensee.pdf
  - Date: 2005
- **Ohio v. Amex is still shaping the merchant case.** In early 2024 the MDL court "granted in part and denied in part defendants' motion for summary judgment under Ohio v. American Express."
  - URL: Visa FY2026 10-K
  - Date: November 5, 2026
