In fiscal 2026, 355 billion payments carried Visa's name, close to a billion a day (Visa 10-K). Yet Visa and Mastercard don't lend you money, and neither started as a company: both began as clubs owned by the banks that used them. How did two bank cooperatives become the gate almost every card payment in America passes through? The story: BankAmericard in 1958, Interbank and Master Charge, the member-owned network that chose the name Visa in 1976, the IPOs of 2006 and 2008, the network effects the Supreme Court described in 2018, and the rules, lawsuits and bills that keep trying to pry the gate open.

Every figure is sourced below, mostly from Visa and Mastercard SEC filings, the Federal Reserve, the Supreme Court and the Justice Department. DOJ figures are allegations in a pending case. The ≈$5.5 billion merchant settlement net and the Visa IPO gross are our own calculations. The November 2025 merchant settlement and the Credit Card Competition Act were not final as of November 2026. Not affiliated with Visa, Mastercard or any bank.
