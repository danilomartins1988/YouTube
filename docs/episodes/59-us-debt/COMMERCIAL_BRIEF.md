## 3. Human question
"If America owes forty trillion dollars, who is actually holding the IOU, and does it matter to me?"

## 4. First 30 seconds

-> reason to stay ("So who holds the rest?") -> promise of the tour -> channel intro.

## 6. Animated explanatory sequences (presenter interacts)
1. **The IOU in two boxes**: the presenter draws the total, splits it into "held by the public" and "intragovernmental", and points to
   the Social Security share.
2. **Follow the bond from auction to owner**: the auction hall (paddles rise, results board types, SOLD!) -> the dinner table where
   foreign plates are stacked by holdings -> the holders' street where bonds pop into the windows of funds, banks, pensions and
   houses -> the Fed's warehouse whose shelves thin out and refill. Presenter board: holdings drawn to scale (bars, then the same
   holdings as tiny squares in the corner of the whole debt).
3. **The savings loop**: the presenter draws you -> fund -> Treasury -> interest -> fund -> you ("one person's debt is another
   person's savings").
4. **The trust fund round trip**: the drawer of IOUs empties through a tube to the Treasury while the presenter draws payroll taxes
   -> trust fund -> Treasury (IOU) -> cash for benefits -> borrowing from the public; then the pie shows the IOU moving from the small
   box to the big one.
5. **The meter vs the scale**: a taxi-style interest meter runs to $1,052B and outweighs a defense pan on a balance scale; the
   presenter then draws defense, interest, Medicare and Social Security as bars.

## 7. Payoff
Specific answer: of the $40.6T, foreign holders ≈ a quarter (Japan more than China; China ≈ 1.5%), the Fed ≈ a ninth, the
government's own trust funds ≈ a fifth (mostly Social Security), and close to half sits with other investors, mostly American:
money funds, households, mutual funds and ETFs, banks, state and local governments, pensions and insurers. Broader insight: the
biggest lender to the United States is the United States, so every dollar of interest is both a cost to taxpayers and income to
whoever holds the IOU. (Neutral: no judgement on whether the debt is "too high"; CBO's projection is reported as a projection.)
