The U.S. Postal Service has lost about $118 billion since 2006, according to the GAO: $9.5 billion in 2024, $9.0 billion in 2025 and $6.5 billion in fiscal 2026. It delivers to more than 170 million addresses, six and often seven days a week, and generally receives no tax dollars for operating expenses. So why do the losses never stop? We follow the money through the universal-service network, the 2006 law that required fixed retiree-health payments, the 2022 reform, the collapse of First-Class Mail from 103.7 billion pieces in 2001 to 39.9 billion in 2026, the 2021 ten-year plan, and the $15 billion borrowing limit the Postal Service has now reached.

Every figure is sourced below: USPS fiscal-year results and the FY2026 10-K, GAO-26-107336, P.L. 109-435 and P.L. 117-108, the USPS Historian and the 2021 Delivering for America plan. The 2006 payments total and the stamp-price percentage are our own calculations. Not affiliated with the Postal Service.
