# Episode 47: "Why the Post Office Loses $9 Billion a Year" - Research Notes

Checked: 2026-11-10. All figures are US dollars, USPS fiscal years (Oct 1 - Sep 30) unless noted.
"Fetched" = primary page/PDF opened and read. "Snippet" = seen only in search results.

> **Headline issue:** USPS published FY2026 results **today (Nov 10, 2026)**: GAAP net loss **$6.5bn**, not ~$9bn. The "$9 billion a year" title matches FY2024 ($9.5bn) and FY2025 ($9.0bn) only. See Flags.

## 2. What drives the losses

- **Controllable loss definition:** "net loss adjusted for costs outside of management's control, including workers' compensation expense caused by actuarial revaluation and discount rate changes and the amortization of the CSRS and FERS unfunded liabilities." - FY2025 release (snippet of same text; fetched page confirms figures) - Nov 14, 2025
- **Workers' comp swings with interest rates:** 10-K excludes "actuarial revaluation of existing claims and discount rate changes, which can fluctuate significantly and over which we have no control." FY2025 $972M expense -> FY2026 $(1,444)M benefit; FY2024 was $2,164M. - FY2026 10-K; FY2025 release - fetched
- **Pension amortization (CSRS/FERS), $M:** CSRS 3,245 (FY24) / 3,122 (FY25) / 2,970 (FY26); FERS 2,286 / 2,159 / 1,879. - FY2025 + FY2026 releases - fetched
- **Unfunded pensions:** CSRS $59.1bn + FERS $38.0bn = **$97.1bn** unfunded (FY2026, OPM estimate); of this, "$23.1 billion and $12.7 billion" already invoiced but unpaid. - FY2026 10-K p.12-13 and pension table - fetched
- **USPS skipped 2026 pension payments:** PRC granted a "Temporary Conditional Waiver" on April 9, 2026; USPS "decided not to remit either full or partial amortization payments to OPM on the 2026 obligations." Also suspended employer FERS normal-cost contributions from April 10, 2026: "deferred approximately $2.6 billion." - FY2026 10-K p.12, p.59 - fetched
- **Retiree health top-up defaulted:** "annual top-up payment for retiree health benefits, which we defaulted on in 2026" ($726M). Unfunded retiree health obligation $44.1bn (FY26) vs $29.9bn (FY25). - FY2026 10-K p.34 - fetched
- **2006 PAEA prefunding schedule** (P.L. 109-435, enacted Dec 20, 2006): fixed payments of $5.4bn (2007), $5.6bn (2008), $5.4bn (2009), $5.5bn (2010), $5.5bn (2011), $5.6bn (2012), $5.6bn (2013), $5.7bn (2014), $5.7bn (2015), $5.8bn (2016). Total as written = $55.8bn (my sum). - https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PLAW-109publ435/html/PLAW-109publ435.htm - fetched
- **PAEA price cap:** "subjected our Market-Dominant services to a price cap rigidly tied to the CPI-U." - FY2026 10-K - fetched
- **PSRA 2022 (P.L. 117-108, "Approved April 6, 2022"):** Sec. 102(c)(1): any unpaid payment required under section 8909a "is canceled." Medicare: annuitant "may not enroll in a Program plan unless" also "enrolled in Medicare part B." Six-day delivery: "Delivery shall occur at least six days a week." - https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PLAW-117publ108/html/PLAW-117publ108.htm - fetched
- **PSRA amounts:** "canceled $57 billion of USPS's missed payments"; Medicare integration "reduced USPS's retiree health benefits unfunded liability by $61.2 billion" (~51%). Top-ups "about $750 million in fiscal year 2026 and grow to about $1.4 billion in fiscal year 2031"; retiree health fund "depleted in fiscal year 2031." - GAO-26-107336 - Dec 16, 2025 - fetched
- **FY2022 one-off:** USPS booked ~$57bn noncash reversal ($56.975bn) making FY2022 net income ~$56bn. - USPS FY2022 release / OIG - snippet only

## 5. Stamp prices and Delivering for America

- **Forever stamp (first-ounce letter) now 82 cents, since July 12, 2026.** History: 55c (Jan 27, 2019) -> 58c (Aug 29, 2021) -> 60c (Jul 10, 2022) -> 63c (Jan 22, 2023) -> 66c (Jul 9, 2023) -> 68c (Jan 21, 2024) -> 73c (Jul 14, 2024) -> 78c (Jul 13, 2025) -> 82c (Jul 12, 2026). 2006: 39c. - https://about.usps.com/who/profile/history/domestic-letter-rates-since-1863.htm - Aug 2026 - fetched. (55->82 = +49%, my calculation; 8 increases in 5 years.)
- July 2026 filing: "approximately 4.8 percent"; reason: "the severe financial crisis facing the Postal Service and continued rising operational costs." Docket R2026-1. - https://about.usps.com/newsroom/national-releases/2026/0409-usps-recommends-new-prices-for-july.htm - Apr 9, 2026 - fetched
- 10-K confirms July 12, 2026 "4.8% average price increase for all mailing services products"; July 13, 2025 Market-Dominant +7.4%. - FY2026 10-K - fetched
- **Delivering for America (Mar 23, 2021) goals:** "Operates at break-even by FY2023 avoiding $160 billion in projected losses over the next 10 years"; "$40 billion in capital investments"; "Aims to meet or exceed 95 percent network-wide, on-time delivery." - https://about.usps.com/newsroom/national-releases/2021/0323-usps-unveils-10-year-plan-to-achieve-financial-sustainability-and-service-excellence.htm - fetched
- **Results vs plan (GAO):** revenue "about $24 billion (8 percent) more than projected from fiscal years 2021 through 2024"; expenses "about $36 billion (11.9 percent) higher than USPS originally projected"; break-even in FY2023 not achieved. - GAO-26-107336 - fetched
- Actual: no break-even; losses $9.5bn (FY24), $9.0bn (FY25), $6.5bn (FY26). FY2026 releases/10-K no longer name "Delivering for America" (Q3 release: generic "transformation plan"). - fetched

## Surprising / visual ideas

- Mail per address fell from 5.5 to 2.3 pieces a day (2007 -> 2026), while 1.7-1.8 million new addresses are added each year. Picture a mail carrier walking more stops with a thinner bag.
- In 2026 the Postal Service skipped its pension payments and its retiree health payment ("defaulted on"), and paused employer FERS contributions. The 10-K's own word is "defaulted."
- The debt limit has sat at $15.0bn since 1992, and USPS is at the cap.
- The FY2026 loss shrank by $2.5bn mostly because of an accounting gain from interest rates on workers' comp (a swing from a $972M expense to a $1,444M benefit). Show interest rates as a seesaw.
- The 2006 law set ten fixed checks of $5.4-5.8bn a year. Show stacked giant checks.
- FY2022 shows a $56bn profit on paper, from the cancelled bills (snippet).
- The stamp went from 39c in 2006 to 82c in 2026, with 8 increases since 2021.
- Packages now bring in more revenue than First-Class Mail ($34.1bn vs $26.2bn).
- Total mail peaked at 213bn+ pieces in 2006.

## Flags / not verified

1. **Title vs FY2026:** the latest loss is $6.5bn (FY2026), not $9bn. Safe wording: "lost about $9 billion in each of 2024 and 2025, and $6.5 billion in fiscal 2026," or retitle ("Why the Post Office Keeps Losing Billions"). If "$9 billion a year" stays, say "in recent years" and show the years.
2. **FY2026 10-K PDF header says "Draft (6.0)"** as downloaded today. The numbers match the official FY2026 press release, but re-download before lock and cite the press release for the headline figures.
3. **FY2025 volumes restated:** the FY2025 release gives First-Class 42,049M and S&P 6,837M; the FY2026 release's prior-year column gives 42,014M and 6,813M; the Historian table gives 42,182M. Cite one source per number and don't mix them.
4. **PAEA "$5.5bn a year":** the statutory schedule ($5.4-5.8bn) is verified. Later laws changed some years (e.g., the 2009 payment was reportedly reduced), and USPS defaulted on payments from about FY2011/2012. Not verified on a primary page. Safe wording: "the 2006 law scheduled payments of roughly $5.5 billion a year for ten years." Avoid "pay 75 years of benefits in 10 years" (not verified).
5. **"$57 billion cancelled" / FY2022 $56bn net income:** $57bn is verified via GAO (fetched). The FY2022 net income and $56.975bn figures are snippet only; fetch https://about.usps.com/newsroom/national-releases/2022/1110-usps-reports-fiscal-year-2022-results.htm before use.
6. **Q1/Q2 FY2026 quarterly losses:** snippet only.
7. **Privatization resolutions:** congress.gov was blocked (403), so these are snippet only. No official executive-branch privatization proposal was found on a primary page. Safe wording: "Privatization has been floated in Washington, and both chambers saw resolutions introduced in 2025 opposing it." Don't attribute privatization statements to named officials without a primary source.
8. **"No tax money":** always say "generally receives no tax dollars for operating expenses." USPS does receive small appropriations (e.g., revenue forgone for free mail for the blind and overseas voting); not verified here.
9. **Delivering for America status in 2026** (formally ended or renamed?): not verified. Safe wording: "the 2021 plan promised break-even by 2023; that didn't happen."
10. **Percent changes** (-59% First-Class since 2001, +49% stamp since 2019, $55.8bn PAEA total) are my calculations from primary figures. Label them as calculated, not as USPS statements.
