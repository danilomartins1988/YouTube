The picks and shovels of AI: which countries could win the AI economy without building AI? In the California Gold Rush, the man known as the richest in California never mined; he sold the miners what they needed. Today's gold rush is AI, and while everyone watches the chatbot builders, some countries are getting rich the old way. Meet Hattie, a hardware-store owner, and Nugget, a talking AI chip with a passport. We follow the stamps to Taiwan's chip foundry, the Netherlands' lithography machine, South Korea's HBM memory, Japan's tools and chemicals, the Gulf's money and sunshine, and Chile's and Australia's copper and lithium. (Our answer: yes, some countries already are winning without building AI. But shovel sellers can ride a rush down as well as up. The shovels anyone can sell, like metal, money and power, rise and fall with the rush; the shovels nobody else can make set their own price. The winners own a shovel with no second shop, and keep inventing the next one.)
