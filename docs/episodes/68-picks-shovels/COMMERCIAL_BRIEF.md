"If everyone is racing to build AI, who gets rich selling them the shovels, and can that last?"

## 4. 30-second hook
California, 1849: crowds rush into the hills and most find mud, while a storekeeper has the best year of her life selling shovels.
Today's gold rush is AI; everyone watches the chatbot builders, but some countries are getting rich the old way. So which countries
could win the AI economy without building AI at all? (Recognizable saying + contradiction + reason to stay; channel intro follows.)

## 6. Payoff (specific)
Yes, countries can win the AI economy without building AI, and some already are: Taiwan's foundry booked record sales and America
became Taiwan's biggest customer for the first time in 26 years; the Netherlands holds the only shop for the most advanced
lithography machine; South Korea's HBM made SK hynix out-earn all of Samsung and pushed chip exports to a record; Japan sells the
tools and coatings nobody can skip; the Gulf buys a seat in every shop (MGX in Stargate); Chile and Australia dig the metals. The
catch, from 1849 (Sam Brannan died poor) and from the 2023 memory crash: shovels priced by the rush (metal, money, power) rise and fall
with it, while shovels nobody else can make set their own price. The winners own a shovel with no second shop and keep inventing the
next one.
