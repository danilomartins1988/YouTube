# Research notes: MF Global, The $1.6 Billion That Vanished

Checked 2026-11-10. All figures are US dollars. "Fetched" means I read the primary page or PDF myself. "Snippet" means I saw it only in search results or secondary coverage.

Key abbreviations:
- MFGH: MF Global Holdings Ltd. (the parent)
- MFGI: MF Global Inc. (the US broker-dealer and futures commission merchant (FCM))
- RTM: repo-to-maturity

## 1. Collapse, shortfall, filing date, size

- **Filing date, parent and subsidiary.** Quote: "On October 31, 2011, MF Global Holdings Ltd. (MF Global) filed for bankruptcy under Chapter 11 of the U.S. Bankruptcy Code. On the same day, the Securities Investor Protection Corporation began liquidation proceedings for MF Global's U.S.-based subsidiary, MF Global, Inc. (MFGI)". Source: [SR], Preface p.1. Fetched.
- **SEC 8-K on the Chapter 11 filing.** Date of report Oct 31, 2011; signed Nov 3, 2011. Quote: MFGH and MF Global Finance USA Inc. "filed a voluntary petition for relief under Chapter 11 of Title 11 of the United States Code." https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001401106/000119312511295417/d252941d8k.htm Fetched.
- **$1.6bn shortfall (official).** Quote: "Although initial reports estimated that $700 million in customer funds required to be housed in separate accounts for safekeeping were missing, it is now known that MF Global's collapse resulted in a $1.6 billion shortfall in customer funds." Source: [SR] p.1. Fetched.
- **$1.6bn split (trustee), June 4, 2012.** The shortfall "continues to be approximately $1.6 billion." This comprises an "approximate $900 million deficiency" in domestic accounts (commodities and securities) and an "approximate $700 million deficiency related to trades by customers on foreign exchanges." The release also mentions a separately reported "approximately $952 million shortfall in segregated funds." https://prnewswire.com/news-releases/james-w-giddens-trustee-for-the-liquidation-of-mf-global-inc-submits-report-of-the-trustees-investigation-and-recommendations-156966945.html Fetched (trustee press release). Note the split is $900M US + $700M foreign-exchange customers. It is NOT $1.2bn US + $700M UK.
- **How the estimate grew.**
  - Nov 21, 2011: the trustee said the shortfall "may be as much as $1.2 billion or more" and that "these are preliminary numbers that may well change." https://prnewswire.com/news-releases/statement-from-the-office-of-the-trustee-for-the-liquidation-of-mf-global-inc-134243588.html Fetched.
  - Dec 23, 2011: "the shortfall is not less than $1.2 billion." https://prnewswire.com/news-releases/statement-from-the-office-of-the-trustee-for-the-liquidation-of-mf-global-inc-136138508.html Fetched.
  - Feb 2012: the estimate was raised to about $1.6bn, now including about $700M tied to the UK. Snippet only (InvestmentNews/Salon/AP). https://www.investmentnews.com/regulation-and-legislation/trustee-mf-global-coming-up-16b-short/42210
- **Customers affected.** Quote: "approximately 36,000 futures customers and 318 securities customers … some of the individual futures customers were farm co-operatives representing up to 35,000 farmers." Source: [SR] p.1. Fetched.
- **Size ranking.** Sen. Pat Roberts at the hearing: "amid the eighth largest bankruptcy in the history of the United States, has somehow lost $1.2 billion of your customers' money, and none of you know where it went." Source: [SAG], Dec 13, 2011. Fetched. The CRS report R42091 also says "eighth-largest bankruptcy in U.S. history" (https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/R42091.html, fetched; CRS is secondary but official). "Largest since Lehman" was NOT found in any primary source (see Flags).
- **The final week.**
  - Oct 24, 2011: Moody's downgraded MF Global to "Baa3," one notch above junk.
  - Thursday, Oct 27: "Moody's and Fitch both downgraded MF Global's credit rating to junk."
  - Source: [SR] pp.~59-61. Fetched.
- **Quarterly loss.** Oct 25, 2011 earnings release (8-K Ex. 99.1): "GAAP net loss applicable to common shareholders was $191.6 million" for the quarter ended Sept 30, 2011. https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001401106/000119312511279409/d247384dex991.htm Fetched.

## 2. European sovereign repo-to-maturity (RTM) trades

- **$6.3bn, as disclosed by the company.** Oct 25, 2011 earnings release: "net long position of $6.3 billion in a short-duration European sovereign portfolio … financed to maturity (repo-to-maturity), including Belgium, Italy, Spain, Portugal and Ireland." Weighted average maturity Oct 2012; end-date maturity Dec 2012. Position as of Sept 30, 2011. Source: SEC 8-K Ex. 99.1 (URL above). Fetched.
- **Peak sizes (state the source when using).**
  - [SR]: "By August 2011, the company's net European RTM position had reached approximately $7.4 billion." This "represented almost 14% of MF Global's assets and four-and-a-half times its total equity." Fetched.
  - Trustee, June 4, 2012: exposure "peaked at nearly $7 billion in October 2011," "more than four-and-a-half times the firm's total equity." Fetched.
  - FINRA testimony, Dec 8, 2011: "approximately $7.6 billion in European sovereign debt." Fetched.
  - These figures differ by source and measurement date. Use "$6.3 billion (as of Sept 30, 2011)" as the anchor.
- **How it worked and why it was off the balance sheet.** Quote: the bonds are "'derecognized' from the company's balance sheet because they are deemed to be sold by the borrowing company at the time it enters into the RTM transaction." Also: MF Global "could realize an immediate profit on the difference between the interest the issuer of the bonds paid to MF Global and the rate the company paid to its counterparty … and that it could derecognize the bonds from its balance sheet." Source: [SR] pp.~38-39. Fetched.
- **FINRA's view of the accounting.** Stephen Luparello (FINRA Vice Chairman), House Agriculture Committee, Dec 8, 2011: "According to U.S. GAAP, RTMs are afforded sale treatment and therefore not recognized on the balance sheet." Also: "[T]he firm remained subject to market and credit risk throughout the life of the repo." https://www.finra.org/media-center/speeches-testimony/testimony-committee-agriculture Fetched.
- **The FINRA capital charge, summer 2011.**
  - Luparello: "The firm fought this interpretation throughout the summer … before eventually conceding in late August." "The SEC agreed with our assertion that the firm should be holding capital against the positions." Source: FINRA testimony (URL above). Fetched.
  - [SR] figures: FINRA "calculated regulatory haircuts of approximately $257 million." MF Global's own proposal would have produced a charge totaling "$55.8 million." "MF Global increased MFGI's excess net capital by $183 million to $287 million as of August 24, 2011." Because the charge was applied retroactively, MFGI had "to amend its most recent FOCUS report to reflect a $150 million deficiency in net capital for the month of July." Fetched.
  - Key dates from [SR]: the CFTC first learned of the charge "on August 25, 2011." The charge "became public on September 1, 2011, when MF Global filed its amended 10-Q." It was not widely known until a Wall Street Journal article on the morning of Oct 17, 2011. Fetched.
- **Corzine and the strategy.** Quote: "Under Corzine's direction, MF Global's net position in European sovereign debt increased to $6.3 billion just weeks before the company's collapse." Source: [SR] p.~3. Fetched.
- **Epigraph quote** (Corzine, May 20, 2010, quoted on the [SR] title page): "The goal here is not to be a prop trader. … I don't think that we will be in a risk-taking position, substantial enough to have it be the kind of thing that the rating agencies would say 'holy cow, these guys got a different business strategy' than what we told them we had." Fetched.

## 3. Jon Corzine: role and outcome

- **Appointment.** MFGH 8-K, March 2010: Corzine named Chairman and CEO under an employment agreement dated March 23, 2010. Predecessor Bernard Dan's resignation was effective March 23, 2010. https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001401106/000119312510064637/d8k.htm Snippet only. I saw the 8-K content via search summary and did not fetch the page directly; re-fetch before final script.
- **Background, from the same 8-K.**
  - Governor of New Jersey, Jan 2006 to Jan 2010
  - US Senator, 2001 to 2006
  - Chairman and Senior Partner of The Goldman Sachs Group, L.P., Dec 1994 to June 1998
  - Joined Goldman as a bond trader in 1975
  - Snippet (same caveat as above). Safe wording: "former New Jersey governor, U.S. senator, and head of Goldman Sachs."
- **MFGI CEO date.** The CFTC says Corzine was CEO of MFGI "from September 1, 2010" until liquidation began Oct 31, 2011. He was CEO and Chairman of the parent MFGH from March 2010. Source: CFTC release 7508-17 (below). Fetched.
- **CFTC civil case filed.** The CFTC filed a civil complaint on June 27, 2013, against MFGI, MFGH, Corzine and Edith O'Brien. Source: CRS R42091 (fetched). The date also appears on the CFTC order PDF URL: https://cftc.gov/idc/groups/public/%40lrenforcementactions/documents/legalpleading/enfmfglobalorder062713.pdf (not opened).
- **Jan 5, 2017 consent order.** Source: CFTC Release 7508-17, https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases/7508-17. Fetched. Terms:
  - Entered "January 5, 2017" by Judge Victor Marrero (SDNY).
  - Corzine to pay "a $5 million civil monetary penalty."
  - He undertakes he "will never register with the CFTC in any capacity" and "will never act as a principal, agent, officer, director, or employee of a Futures Commission Merchant (FCM)."
  - He may not get the penalty reimbursed by insurance (snippet wording).
  - He neither admitted nor denied the findings (snippet from search summary of the same release).
  - The release says MF Global unlawfully used "nearly one billion dollars" of customer segregated funds in late October 2011.
  - O'Brien: "$500,000 civil monetary penalty" and an 18-month bar.
  - Basis for Corzine's liability: as a controlling person and for supervision failures (snippet wording: "controlling person").
  - Precise wording for the script: it is an undertaking never to register and never to work for an FCM. "Lifetime ban from registering" is a fair paraphrase; say "agreed never to register with the CFTC or work for a futures broker again."
- **Criminal charges.** No primary page states that he was not charged (it is an absence). See Flags.

## 4. CFTC consent orders against MF Global entities

- **MFGI order, 2013.** CFTC Release 6776-13, dated Nov 18, 2013. Order "entered on November 8, 2013," by Judge Marrero (SDNY). It requires "$1.212 billion in restitution to customers of MF Global" and a "$100 million civil monetary penalty," payable after customers and certain priority creditors are paid in full. The release says that in "the last week of October 2011" MF Global unlawfully used customer segregated funds to support its own proprietary operations and those of its affiliates. https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases/6776-13 Fetched.
- **Parent (MFGH) order, 2014.** CFTC Release 7095-14, order entered Dec 23, 2014. MFGH liable for $1.212bn restitution (or whatever is needed to pay customer claims in full) plus a $100M penalty; MFGH admitted liability based on its agents' acts. https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases/7095-14 Snippet only.
- **Restitution paid.** CFTC (2017): "restitution has been paid to satisfy all customer claims." Release 7508-17. Fetched.

## 5. Recovery: customers made whole

- **Securities customers.** SIPC, Nov 6, 2013: "100 percent of securities customers' property has already been returned." About $305 million was allocated from the MFGI general estate to pay commodities customers "by the end of the year." https://www.sipc.org/news-and-media/news-releases/20131106 Fetched.
- **Final distribution, announced April 3, 2014.** Trustee press release; checks began going out Friday, April 4, 2014. https://prnewswire.com/news-releases/trustee-giddens-achieves-milestone-final-100-percent-distribution-to-all-former-mf-global-inc-customers-begins-april-4-2014-253728891.html Fetched. Figures and quotes:
  - Total: "$6.7 billion will have been returned to over 26,000 securities customers and commodities futures customers."
  - 24,020 "4d" (US futures) customers: $5.4bn
  - 2,047 "30.7" (foreign-exchange futures) customers: $880M
  - 428 securities customers: $376M
  - Giddens: "checks are going in the mail that will make all public customers of MF Global Inc. 100 percent whole."
  - Funded partly by "a temporary loan of general estate property."
  - The release refers to "the unprecedented customer property segregation failure that led to MFGI's liquidation."
- **SIPC confirmation.** SIPC, April 3, 2014: "Total of $6.7 Billion Will Be Returned" to "26,000 Former Securities and Commodity Futures Customers." https://www.sipc.org/news-and-media/news-releases/20140403 Fetched.
- **Context for the script.** Customers were made 100% whole about 2.5 years after the collapse (Oct 31, 2011 to April 2014). That time without their money was itself a cost; the trustee release mentions "the length of time customers have been without their property" (snippet wording).

## 6. Who was hurt, and the segregation rule

All quotes in this section are from the Senate Agriculture hearing record, Dec 13, 2011 [SAG], fetched, unless another source is given.

- **Dean Tofteland, farmer, Luverne, MN.**
  - "My wife and I raise corn, soybean, and pigs as well as four kids 8 to 13 years old."
  - "These funds were not an investment in MF Global. These funds were not a loan to MF Global. These funds were simply collateral required by the exchange as a guarantee for my promise to deliver the bushels I had priced."
  - His broker told him "'no customer has ever lost a penny in segregated accounts.'"
  - "The hedge account at this time contained $253,000."
  - His positions moved "to a new broker with only 15 percent of the required collateral," he had to re-margin "within 24 hours," was "forced to liquidate my hedges," and had "well over an additional $100,000 in losses."
  - "commingling money is stealing money, especially when it disappears."
  - "This money was real money in real banks. It was not under somebody's mattress. And now it is missing."
  - "The bank knows if you are overdrawn one penny."
- **C.J. Blew, farmer/rancher and chairman of Mid Kansas Cooperative:** "Segregated funds are segregated funds for a reason. We must ensure the sanctity of segregated funds."
- **Sen. Pat Roberts:** "this is a violation of the most sacred rule of the futures industry, you do not break the glass in regards to segregated funds. It has not happened before until this incident."
- **Chairwoman Debbie Stabenow:**
  - "Our farmers and ranchers have lost trust in the system."
  - "A fundamental principle of futures trading is that customer money must always be kept separate from the firm's money."
  - "It is estimated that as much as $1.2 billion in customer money is missing." (That was the estimate in Dec 2011.)
- **The rule itself: Commodity Exchange Act §4d(a)(2), 7 U.S.C. §6d(a)(2).** Customer money "shall be separately accounted for and shall not be commingled with the funds of such commission merchant or be used to margin or guarantee the trades or contracts, or to secure or extend the credit, of any customer or person other than the one for whom the same are held." https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/7/6d Fetched (statute text).
- **The phrase "customer money is sacred."** It is NOT a verbatim primary quote that I found. Use Roberts' "most sacred rule of the futures industry" or Blew's "sanctity of segregated funds" instead.

## 7. Regulatory changes after

- **CFTC customer protection rules.** "Enhancing Protections Afforded Customers and Customer Funds Held by Futures Commission Merchants and Derivatives Clearing Organizations." 78 FR 68505 (Vol. 78, No. 220). Published Nov 14, 2013. "Effective date: January 13, 2014." Approved by the Commission on Oct 30, 2013, by a 3-1 vote (O'Malia dissenting; vote count is snippet only). https://www.cftc.gov/LawRegulation/FederalRegister/finalrules/2013-26665.html Fetched (first 100k characters).
  - The rule's preamble cites MFGI's SIPA liquidation on Oct 31, 2011, and the trustee's "potential $900 million shortfall" for futures customers.
  - It also cites Peregrine Financial Group (PFG): the CFTC's complaint of July 10, 2012, alleges PFG "falsely represented that it held in excess of $220 million of customer funds" when it held about $5.1 million.
  - Measures include: residual interest targets set by senior management and approved in writing by the board, CEO or CFO; daily segregation schedules; and senior management approval plus notice for withdrawals of "25 percent or more" of excess segregated funds (as described in the rule's NFA context).
- **Gensler statement, Oct 30, 2013.** Segregation is "the core foundation of the commodity futures and swaps markets." "Segregation must be maintained at all times." Regulators "will be able to see electronically custodial accounts and cash accounts on a daily basis." FCMs must "significantly enhance their supervision of and accounting for customer funds." There is a five-year phase-in on residual interest. https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/SpeechesTestimony/genslerstatement103013d Fetched.
- **LSOC** ("legally segregated, operationally commingled"). This is the CFTC model for cleared SWAPS customer collateral. After a double default, the clearinghouse can reach only defaulting customers' collateral. It is described in the 2013 rule text (fetched). CAUTION: LSOC was a Dodd-Frank cleared-swaps rule adopted around early 2012 and was not a response to MF Global. Its timing is from memory, not verified here. Do not present it as an MF Global reform.
- **Accounting.** The Capuano addendum (Dec 3, 2012) credits FASB and FINRA "for changing the treatment of repo-to-maturity transactions." Snippet only. https://www.webharvest.gov/congress115th/20181223173530/http:/capuano.house.gov/news/2012/pr120312.htm

## Visual / surprising angles (cartoon, no real people drawn)

- **Money in a glass box marked "SEGREGATED."** A hand reaches in during "the last week of October 2011." Roberts: "you do not break the glass."
- **The disappearing balance sheet.** Bonds labelled Italy, Spain, Portugal, Ireland, Belgium are "deemed sold" and vanish from the balance sheet, but a risk shadow stays attached. FINRA: "remained subject to market and credit risk."
- **The size of the bet.** Show the $6.3bn stack next to the firm's equity. The bet was about 4.5x equity ([SR]; trustee).
- **The ticking clock.** Aug 24-25: FINRA charge. Sept 1: amended 10-Q (public, but nobody noticed). Oct 17: WSJ story. Oct 24: Moody's downgrade to one notch above junk. Oct 25: $191.6M loss and $6.3bn disclosed. Oct 27: junk. Oct 31: bankruptcy.
- **The farmer's ledger.** $253,000 in hedge collateral is frozen. He re-margins with "15 percent," has "24 hours," and loses "well over an additional $100,000." Broker's line: "no customer has ever lost a penny."
- **"If there is a debit, there has got to be a credit. Where is the credit?"** (Tofteland). A detective-style search for the missing money.
- **Estimate creep.** $700M (initial reports), then $1.2bn (Nov/Dec 2011), then $1.6bn (2012).
- **Twist ending.** Customers got 100% back ($6.7bn to 26,000+ customers, April 2014), but it took about 2.5 years. The CEO's civil penalty was $5M against roughly $1bn misused.

## Flags / not verified (use safe wording)

- **"Largest Wall Street bankruptcy since Lehman."** Not found in any primary source. Verified alternative: "the eighth-largest bankruptcy in U.S. history," per Sen. Roberts at the hearing (Dec 13, 2011) and CRS. If you want "since Lehman," attribute it to press coverage ("widely described as…").
- **"$1.2bn US / $700M UK" split.** Not supported. The trustee's split is about $900M (US domestic accounts) plus about $700M (customers trading on foreign exchanges, largely via the UK). Safe wording: "about $1.6 billion, roughly $900 million in U.S. accounts and $700 million tied to customers trading on foreign exchanges."
- **Shortfall figures disagree.** $1.6bn is the trustee/House-report total. The CFTC says MF Global unlawfully used "nearly one billion dollars," and the restitution order was $1.212bn. Do not mix these up; label each by its source.
- **RTM size varies by source and date.** $6.3bn (company, Sept 30, 2011), ~$7.4bn (Aug 2011, [SR]), "nearly $7 billion" (Oct 2011, trustee), ~$7.6bn (FINRA testimony). Use $6.3bn with its date, or "between $6 and $7 billion."
- **"Never criminally charged."** No primary page confirms an absence. Safe wording: "He faced a civil case from the CFTC, which he settled in 2017 without admitting or denying the findings; he was not criminally charged." The last clause is widely reported; prosecutors announced no charges. Do not say "cleared" or "exonerated."
- **"Lifetime ban."** The CFTC wording is an undertaking: he "will never register with the CFTC in any capacity" and never act as a principal, officer, director or employee of an FCM. Paraphrase accordingly.
- **March 2010 8-K** (Corzine bio and appointment date) was read via search summary only. Re-fetch https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001401106/000119312510064637/d8k.htm before the script locks.
- **MFGH 2014 CFTC order** (7095-14) is snippet only. Fetch it if you cite it on screen.
- **UK customers (MF Global UK Ltd).** They were handled by UK administrators, which I did not verify. Their recovery is not covered by the US trustee's 100% statement. The trustee figure covers MFGI's "30.7" foreign-futures customers ($880M). Safe wording: "all public customers of MF Global Inc." Do not claim "UK customers got 100%."
- **"Customer money is sacred."** Not a verbatim quote found. Use the Roberts or Blew quotes above.
- **LSOC.** A swaps-clearing model, not an MF Global reform (timing from memory, unverified). Leave it out, or describe it carefully.
- **Feb 2012 $1.6bn first announcement.** Snippet only. The June 4, 2012 trustee release and the Nov 2012 House report (both fetched) are the citations to use.
- **FINRA capital charge dollar amounts** ($257M haircuts, $150M July deficiency, $183M infusion) come from [SR]. FINRA's own testimony confirms the $150M July deficiency but gives no infusion amount.
