In October 2011, a Wall Street firm run by a former U.S. senator and governor collapsed in eight days, and about $1.6 billion of customer money was missing (SIPA trustee). Farmers who used the firm only to hedge their crops found their accounts frozen. So how does money that the law says must never be mixed with the firm's own simply vanish? We follow the $6.3 billion bet on European government bonds, the capital charge the firm fought all summer, the downgrade, the last chaotic week, where the customer money went, how customers were eventually made whole, what the CFTC settled with the firm and with its CEO, and the rules written afterwards.

Every figure is sourced below: the House Financial Services Committee staff report (2012), MF Global 8-K filings, the SIPA trustee and SIPC, CFTC releases (2013 and 2017), Senate Agriculture Committee and FINRA testimony, and the Federal Register. Jon Corzine settled a civil case without admitting or denying the allegations and was never criminally charged. Amounts are nominal U.S. dollars.
