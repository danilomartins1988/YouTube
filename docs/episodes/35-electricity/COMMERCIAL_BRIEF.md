## 4. First 30 seconds (as rendered)
Twenty-eight dollars and ninety-two cents. Then two hundred and sixty-nine dollars and ninety-two cents. That is how much the price of keeping enough power plants ready jumped, in a single auction, on America's largest power grid. More than nine times higher, by our calculation. And that cost does not stay in the auction room. It flows, month after month, into the electricity bills of millions of homes. So why is the most ordinary bill in America, the one for the lights, suddenly rising faster than almost everything else?

## 7. Payoff (as rendered)
So why are America's electricity bills rising? Not because electricity got harder to make. Because the system that delivers it is being rebuilt, the fuel became volatile, and a new generation of machines is competing with your home for the same power. The light switch looks the same as ever. The economics behind it have changed.
