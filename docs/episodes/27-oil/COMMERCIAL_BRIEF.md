## 4. First 30 seconds (as rendered)
What would you do if someone offered you a barrel of oil, and paid you to take it? On April twentieth, twenty twenty, that is exactly what happened. The price of American crude oil for delivery in May fell below zero, for the first time since that contract began trading in nineteen eighty-three. At the end of the day it settled at minus thirty-seven dollars and sixty-three cents a barrel. People who owned oil were paying others to take it off their hands. To understand how, you have to understand that an oil price is really a promise, and on that day the promise came due. What happens to oil when the world stops moving?

## 7. Payoff (as rendered)
So what does negative oil teach about prices? That a price is not just about how useful something is. It is also about where it is, when it arrives, and whether you have room for it. On April twentieth, the oil had not lost its value. The world had simply run out of places to put it. And a contract that looked like a piece of paper turned out to be a promise to take delivery of something very real.
