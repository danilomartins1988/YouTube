## Verified (CFTC / EIA / CRS, read on the page)
- May 2020 WTI settled at −$37.63 on 20 Apr 2020. Intraday low −$40.32 at 2:29 p.m. ET. From $0 to −$39.55 between 2:08 and 2:28 p.m. ET. First time below $0 since the contract began in 1983.
- Friday 17 Apr close: $18.27. Sunday open: $17.73. The price fell $58.05 to the low. It expired on 21 Apr, settling at +$10.01.
- All other WTI expirations settled positive on 20 Apr. The May–June spread went from −$6.76 to −$58.06.
- Holders at expiry must make or take delivery at Cushing, OK. Some paid counterparties to exit (EIA).
- Cushing working capacity was 76 million barrels; it held 60M on 17 Apr (≈ 76% full) and 81% on 24 Apr. It peaked at 83% (EIA). It had been about 50% full in mid-March (CFTC). The three largest weekly builds on record came in April.
- Floating storage rose by almost 69M barrels to about 127M in the first three weeks of April (CFTC, citing Bloomberg data).
- OPEC+ cut of 9.7 million b/d, the largest single cut in history (CFTC).
- Open interest fell from 634,727 contracts at the start of April to 108,593 on the morning of 20 Apr. On 20 Apr, 173 of 448 reportable traders still held May contracts (CFTC).
- June contract: $20.43 on 20 Apr and $11.57 on 21 Apr (−43%) (CRS). WTI was back at $40/b on 1 Jul 2020 (EIA).
- CME sent testing notices on 3, 8, 13 and 15 April for negative prices (cited via the CFTC report, footnote 42).
- Demand: analysts estimated a fall of about 30% of global consumption, about 30 million b/d (CRS).
