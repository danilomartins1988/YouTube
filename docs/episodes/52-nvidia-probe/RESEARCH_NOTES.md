## 3. Megaspeed

**Corporate facts** (Reuters factbox, Nov 10, 2026, via Investing.com, https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/factboxwhat-we-know-about-megaspeed-singaporebased-customer-of-nvidia-5014126, Fetched)
- "Incorporated in 2015 as 7Road International and renamed Megaspeed International in March 2023."
- It is wholly owned by Swiftdata, a Singapore company incorporated in October 2023.
- Its website calls it an "Nvidia Cloud Partner" in Asia-Pacific offering GB200 NVL72 and HGX H100 systems.
- Per business filings, assets were $3bn in 2024, up from $33 million in 2023. Revenue was $294 million in 2024, up from zero.
- Per Reuters, the woman (as Huang Le) ended an eight-month tenure as managing director in October 2023. Other outlets call her a current or "former" executive (see Flags).
- Singapore says it does not enforce "unilateral export controls imposed by other countries."
- Singapore's Home Affairs Ministry rebutted remarks attributed to it by WSJ: "neither the Singapore Government nor its officials said this." CNBC says Singapore "has not investigated" her alleged intelligence ties.

**How much it bought**
- NYT, Oct 2025: Megaspeed, "which spun off from a Chinese gaming company in 2023," bought "nearly $2 billion worth of Nvidia's most advanced products" through its Malaysian subsidiary (CNBC Oct 10, 2025, Fetched).
- Later reports: "$4.8 billion" over two years (TechCrunch, Fetched, original source not named). The figures conflict; attribute whichever you use.

**Sen. Tom Cotton letter to Commerce Secretary Lutnick (Oct 13, 2025, released Oct 14)**
- He asked: "I respectfully request the Commerce Department investigate Megaspeed."
- He described "diversion of American advanced chips to China through a network of shell companies and subsidiaries."
- He said "export-controlled U.S. chips still in boxes" were found at a Malaysian datacenter.
- He called Megaspeed a spin-off of Chinese state-owned 7Road.
- This is a senator's allegation, not a finding. Fetched.

**US government action**
- **BIS/Commerce:** It investigated for months. The probe was paused at DOJ's request after Nvidia GC Tim Teter emailed the agents in August 2025, saying senior Commerce official Jeff Kessler had approved the outreach. Kessler reportedly replied that the case should be handled "strictly by the book." Commerce's spokesman said it handled the case by the book. Source: TNW relaying NYT (Fetched secondary); NBC lede (Fetched).
- **Entity List:** Other agencies proposed adding Megaspeed, but Commerce has not done so. Megaspeed is **not** on the Entity List per this reporting. I did not search the Federal Register myself.
- **DOJ:** It is reportedly running a criminal inquiry and declined comment. No charges.
- **Singapore police:** An investigation is ongoing (Oct 2025 statement).

**Nvidia's past statements**
- Oct 2025: "Megaspeed is running a small commercial cloud, like many other companies throughout the world." Nvidia also said it had found no "reason to believe products have been diverted." Source: CNBC Oct 10, 2025. Fetched.
- Dec 2025 (Bloomberg, via Fortune): Megaspeed is "a cloud service permitted under the export control rules."
- Oct 29, 2026: "thoroughly investigated and debunked last year" (Fortune, Fetched).
- Nov 9, 2026 (Rizzo, via NYT/TNW): Nvidia talks to officials to answer questions and seek guidance.

**Megaspeed's position**
- It calls the reporting "misleading information and incorrect insinuations."
- It says BIS, Malaysia's trade ministry and Nvidia found "no evidence of any violations concerning the illicit transfer of chips or any illegal activities."
- It says it is running its own investigation.
- Source: CNBC, Nov 9, 2026, https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/09/megaspeed-nvidia-jensen-huang-alice-huang.html (Fetched).

## 4. Export controls and the Singapore question

**Rules timeline, as Nvidia describes it ([10Q], Fetched)**
- "In August 2022, the USG announced export restrictions and export licensing requirements targeting China's semiconductor and supercomputing industries ... and specifically impact our A100 and H100 integrated circuits." BIS's formal rule was issued Oct 7, 2022 (BIS public information page, https://www.bis.doc.gov/index.php/policy-guidance/advanced-computing-and-semiconductor-manufacturing-items-controls-to-prc, Snippet).
- "In October 2023, the USG announced new and updated licensing requirements for exports to China and Country Groups D:1, D:4, and D:5 ... including, but not limited to, the A100, A800, H100, H800, L4, L40, L40S, RTX 4090, GB200 NVL72, and B200." BIS press release of Oct 17, 2023: https://www.bis.gov/press-release/commerce-strengthens-restrictions-advanced-computing-semiconductors-semiconductor-manufacturing-equipment (Snippet).
- Jan 2025, AI Diffusion IFR: "In May 2025, the USG announced that it would rescind the AI Diffusion IFR and implement a replacement rule."
- "In April 2025, the USG informed us that it requires a license for export to China ... of our H20 integrated circuits." Nvidia took a "$4.5 billion charge in the first quarter of fiscal year 2026."
- "Beginning in August 2025, the USG granted licenses ... to ship certain H20 products ... but such sales were restricted by the PRC government."
- 2026: "Beginning in February 2026, the USG granted licenses that would allow us to ship small amounts of H200 products to specific China-based customers, but such sales were restricted by the PRC government." H200s go through US inspection and "are subject to a 25% tariff upon importation into the United States." H200 shipments are "less than 1% of Data Center revenue in our most recent quarter." Nvidia took a $0.4bn H200 charge in 1H FY2027.
- Nvidia says it is "effectively foreclosed from competing in China's data center computing/compute market."
- Pending legislation: Remote Access Security Act (RASA) and the GAIN AI Act (Senate NDAA, Oct 2025).

**Singapore billing vs shipment (the key nuance)**
- FY2025 10-K footnote: "Singapore represented 18% of fiscal year 2025 total revenue based upon customer billing location. Customers use Singapore to centralize invoicing while our products are almost always shipped elsewhere. Shipments to Singapore were less than 2% of fiscal year 2025 total revenue." Singapore billing revenue was $23,684M, out of $130,497M total. Source: [10K25]. Fetched.
- Q2 FY2026 10-Q: "Singapore represented 22%, and 21% of the second quarter and first half of fiscal year 2026 total revenue based upon customer billing location ... Over 99% of controlled Data Center compute revenue billed to Singapore was for orders from U.S.-based customers." Singapore billing was $10,156M out of $46,743M for the quarter. Source: [10Q-Q2FY26]. Fetched.
- **Change of method.** From Q3 FY2026 (quarter ended Oct 26, 2025), Nvidia reports by customer headquarters, so Singapore is no longer broken out. [10K26] footnote: "In the third quarter of fiscal year 2026, we changed to revenue based upon the location of our customers' headquarters as we believe it provides a better representation of the geographic profile of our revenue. Prior period information has been recast to reflect this change." Fetched.
- **China share (customer-HQ basis).**
  - FY2026: $19,677M of $215,938M ≈ 9.1% (our calc), per [10K26].
  - Q2 FY2027: $7,880M of $96,221M ≈ 8.2% (our calc), per [10Q].
  - "Shipments of Data Center Hopper products to China during the second quarter of fiscal year 2027 were less than 1% of Data Center revenue." Source: [10Q]. Fetched.
- The 10-Q caveat: "The end customer and shipping location may be different from our direct customers' headquarters location." Source: [10Q]. Fetched.

**DOJ chip-smuggling cases (fetched from justice.gov; all are allegations unless a plea is noted)**
- **Nov 20, 2025, M.D. Fla.** Four defendants (two US citizens, two PRC nationals) were charged.
  - They allegedly exported 400 Nvidia A100s to China via Malaysia and Thailand between Oct 2024 and Jan 2025. H100 and H200 attempts were stopped.
  - Allegedly more than $3.89M was wired from China.
  - https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/us-citizens-and-chinese-nationals-arrested-exporting-artificial-intelligence-technology
- **Dec 8, 2025, S.D. Tex. ("Operation Gatekeeper").** Alan Hao Hsu and Hao Global pleaded guilty (Oct 10, 2025).
  - They allegedly exported or attempted to export "at least $160 million" in H100 and H200 GPUs, with chips relabeled "SANDKYAN."
  - More than $50M was seized.
  - The press release does not mention Singapore or Malaysia, but a related complaint reportedly does (Snippet).
  - https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/us-authorities-shut-down-major-china-linked-ai-tech-smuggling-network
- **Oct 1, 2026, C.D. Cal.** Greg Lui (Earthmade Computer) was indicted for allegedly sending more than $300M of GPU servers to Malaysia and Singapore for reshipment to China (2023–24).
  - https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/california-man-arrested-smuggling-more-300-million-export-controlled-computer-servers-china
- **Aug 2025, ALX Solutions.** Two Chinese nationals in California were charged over H100 shipments via Singapore and Malaysia freight forwarders. Snippet only (Al Jazeera, https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2025/8/6/us-charges-chinese-nationals-with-illegally-shipping-nvidia-chips-to-china). Fetch justice.gov before use.
- **Not checked:** the SDNY "$510 million" diversion case (Snippet) and the Singapore Feb 2025 fraud case.
- Nvidia is not a defendant in any of these cases.
