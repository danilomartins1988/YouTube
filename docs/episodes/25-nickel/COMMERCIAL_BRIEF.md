## 4. First 30 seconds (as rendered)
What happens when the price of a metal doubles in a single morning, and the market where it trades decides that none of it happened? In March twenty twenty-two, it happened in London. The price of nickel, a metal used in stainless steel and batteries, shot past one hundred and one thousand three hundred and sixty-five dollars a tonne. The London Metal Exchange suspended trading. Then it cancelled every trade made that morning, worth about thirteen point four billion dollars. Some traders lost fortunes they thought they had made. Others were saved from ruin. Who sets the price of the world's metals?

## 7. Payoff (as rendered)
So what does the nickel crisis teach about markets? That a price is only as real as the system behind it. When enough people bet one way, and the bets are hidden from view, a market can break in a few hours. The exchange saved itself by rewriting the morning. That kept the system standing. But it also showed that, in a real crisis, the rules of the market can change while the game is being played. And the people who paid for that were not always the ones who made the bet.
