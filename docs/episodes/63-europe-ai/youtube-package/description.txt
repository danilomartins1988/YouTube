Everyone says Europe is falling behind. Is Europe declining? The facts are real: the EU's economy has slipped further behind America's since 2002, its population is aging, its companies pay two to three times US electricity prices, and only four of the world's top 50 tech companies are European. But Europe still builds the only machines that can print the most advanced chips, and the EU sells the world far more medicine than it buys. We follow Theo, a fictional engineer in an aging European factory town, and his sister Nora, a drug researcher, as they test a hypothesis about Europe's economic future: if AI and robots make labor matter less, owning the technology, the patents and the machines may matter more. Through Novo Nordisk, Roche, Novartis, AstraZeneca, Sanofi, GSK, ASML, Siemens and Airbus, we follow who captures the value when the robots do the work, and what Europe would need to turn that into a comeback.

In this video:
- Why is Europe falling behind America and China, and how big is the gap? (EU, IMF and Draghi report figures)
- Could automation offset Europe's shrinking workforce?
- Why European drugmakers capture value from medicines made and sold worldwide
- How ASML, Siemens and Airbus earn from factories they don't run
- Owning vs. operating: a made-up company shows where profit goes when a factory automates
- Could cheap energy (Brazil as an example) pull automated factories elsewhere, and who would still profit?
- What would it take for Europe's economy to come back? A plausible path, not a promise.
