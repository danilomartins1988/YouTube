## Verified
- **CMS NHE Fact Sheet** (modified 2026-06-24), https://www.cms.gov/data-research/statistics-trends-and-reports/national-health-expenditure-data/nhe-fact-sheet
  - "NHE grew 7.2% to $5.3 trillion in 2024, or $15,474 per person, and accounted for 18.0% of Gross Domestic Product (GDP)."
  - Hospitals: $1,634.7bn (+8.9%). Prescription drugs: $467.0bn (+7.9%).
  - Highlights PDF: share of the economy went from 17.7% (2023) to 18.0% (2024).
- **Peterson-KFF, "How does health spending in the U.S. compare to other countries?"** (2026-03-11, OECD basis, not to be mixed with CMS), https://www.healthsystemtracker.org/chart-collection/health-spending-u-s-compare-countries/
  - $14,775 per person in the US vs $7,860 in peer countries; "almost $5,000 more per person than ... Switzerland".
  - 17.2% of GDP vs a peer average of 11.2%.
- **Peterson-KFF, life expectancy** (2026-03-31), https://www.healthsystemtracker.org/chart-collection/u-s-life-expectancy-compare-countries/
  - US 79 years in 2024, "3.7 years below the comparable country average of 82.7 years".
- **KFF / Peterson-KFF, prices vs utilization** (2025-09-04), https://www.healthsystemtracker.org/chart-collection/how-do-healthcare-prices-and-use-in-the-u-s-compare-to-other-countries/
  - "higher prices – as opposed to higher utilization – explain the United States' high health spending".
  - About half as many in-person doctor consultations per person.
  - Peers had 57% more hospital discharges per capita (2021).
  - Average hospital stay: 9.7 days in peers vs 6.5 in the US.
  - Heart bypass (CABG): $44,149 in US traditional Medicare vs $24,847 in peers.
  - Angioplasty: 2.9× the peer cost.
  - MRI: US private plans pay more than 3× Medicare (a comparison between US payers, not countries).
  - Medicare-negotiated drug prices: 2.8× the average of peer countries.
- **JAMA 2018, Papanicolas et al.** (2018-03-13), https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2674671
  - Utilization "largely similar".
  - Administration 8% in the US vs 1–3% elsewhere (2016 data).
  - Drug spending per capita $1,443 vs $466–$939.
- **Peterson-KFF, spending over time** (2026-01-22), https://www.healthsystemtracker.org/chart-collection/u-s-spending-healthcare-changed-time/
  - Insurance and program administration = 7.0% of national health expenditures in 2024 (partial quote).
- **RAND Round 5.1** (RR-A1144-2-v2, Dec 2024), https://www.rand.org/pubs/research_reports/RRA1144-2-v2.html
  - Read via the Vermont Legislature PDF copy.
  - Private insurers paid 254% of Medicare in 2022 (outpatient facility 279%, professional 184%).
  - Caveats: limited sample; the hospital association (AHA) disputes the method.
- **RAND, international drug prices, 2022 data** (2024), https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11147645/
  - All drugs 278% of the 33 OECD countries; brand-name 422%; generics 67%.
  - Brand-name after rebates: 308%. These are list prices before rebates.
- **KFF Employer Health Benefits Survey 2026, news release** (2026-10-22), https://www.kff.org/other-health/workers-now-pay-7471-on-average-annually-for-family-health-coverage-up-9-or-621-from-2025-benchmark-employer-health-benefits-survey-finds/
  - Family premium $28,303 (+5%); workers pay $7,471.
