In 2026, health insurance for the average American family with a job-based plan cost $28,303 a year (KFF). The US spent $5.3 trillion on health care in 2024, $15,474 per person and 18% of GDP (CMS). Yet Americans see a doctor about half as often as people in other rich countries, spend fewer days in hospital, and live 3.7 years shorter than the peer average. The reason is prices: private insurers paid hospitals 254% of Medicare rates (RAND), brand-name drugs cost 422% of prices in 33 other rich countries before rebates (RAND), and administration takes a bigger share than elsewhere.
