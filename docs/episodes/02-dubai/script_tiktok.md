# The Real Business Behind Dubai's Skyscrapers

_Who builds Dubai's towers, who pays for them, and what happened the last time the music stopped._

## hook

In 2025, 917 billion dirhams worth of property deals were done in Dubai. That isn't just a skyline. It's a business.

> `re_2025` [source_located] Dubai real estate recorded over 270,000 transactions worth AED 917 billion in 2025, up from AED 761 billion in 2024. (sources: dmo_realestate_2025, dld_2024)  

## builders

Who builds it? Companies close to the state. Emaar, the city's best-known developer, now has a government-owned group, Dubai Holding, as its largest shareholder.

> `emaar_owner` [source_located] Since May 2026 Dubai Holding, a government-owned group, is Emaar Properties' largest shareholder with 29.73%, after buying the Investment Corporation of Dubai's 22.27% stake. (sources: dmo_emaar_2026)  
> "Best-known" is editorial framing; ownership is sourced.  

## buyers

Who buys? Mostly cash buyers. The IMF says about 83 percent of home purchases in 2024 used no mortgage. And villa prices more than doubled between early 2020 and the middle of 2025.

> `imf_prices` [source_located] IMF (2025 Article IV) - Dubai prices rose 117.6% for villas and 46.2% for apartments between 2020Q1 and 2025Q2; 83.4% of 2024 residential transactions were non-mortgaged. (sources: imf_cr25327)  

## crash

It has happened before. When the global financial crisis hit, Dubai property prices fell by about half. In 2009, the state conglomerate Dubai World asked to pause about 26 billion dollars of debt.

> `crash_2009` [source_located] Dubai property prices fell by about half from their mid-2008 peak by mid-2009 (Colliers International, as reported by Gulf News). (sources: gulfnews_prices_2009)  
> `standstill_2009` [source_located] On 25 November 2009 Dubai asked creditors of Dubai World and its property units for a standstill on about $26 billion of debt. (sources: gulfnews_dubai_world_2009)  

## bailout

Abu Dhabi provided 10 billion dollars. Weeks later, the world's tallest tower opened, and Burj Dubai became Burj Khalifa, named after Abu Dhabi's ruler.

> `bailout_2009` [source_located] On 14 December 2009 Abu Dhabi provided $10 billion to Dubai, part of which repaid a maturing Nakheel Islamic bond. (sources: ibtimes_bailout_2009)  
> `burj_renamed` [source_located] The world's tallest building opened on 4 January 2010 and was renamed from Burj Dubai to Burj Khalifa, after Abu Dhabi's ruler and UAE President Sheikh Khalifa. (sources: csm_burj_2010)  

## warning

Today the IMF warns that if capital flows reverse, or investors change their minds, prices could face an unexpected correction.

> `imf_correction_risk` [source_located] The IMF warns the real estate sector may be affected by a slowdown or reversal of capital flows or a shift in investor sentiment that could lead to an unexpected price correction. (sources: imf_cr25327)  

## outro

The skyline is a business. And businesses have cycles.
