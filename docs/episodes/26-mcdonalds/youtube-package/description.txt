On January 31, 1990, people in Moscow began lining up as early as 4 a.m. to buy a hamburger. The first McDonald's in the Soviet Union served 30,000 customers that day, a company record for an opening day. How it took 14 years and $50 million to get in, why McDonald's built its own food plant and taught Soviet farmers to grow its potatoes, how the business grew to more than 850 restaurants, and what it cost to leave Russia in 2022: a $1.3 billion charge, and the same restaurant on the same square reopening under a new name. Every figure is sourced below.

All amounts are in US dollars as published. 1990 Soviet prices are not converted (the currency was not convertible); they are described as a share of average wages.
