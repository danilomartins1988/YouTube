## 4. First 30 seconds (as rendered)
Why would thousands of people stand in the snow before dawn, in the capital of the Soviet Union, just to buy a hamburger? On January thirty-first, nineteen ninety, people began lining up near Pushkin Square in Moscow as early as four in the morning. By the end of the day, thirty thousand customers had walked through the doors of the first McDonald's in the Soviet Union, a record for any opening day in the company's history. For a few years, that hamburger became a symbol of the Cold War coming to an end. Thirty-two years later, it became a symbol of something else. How do you open a burger restaurant in a country where private business is not allowed?

## 7. Payoff (as rendered)
So what does one hamburger restaurant teach us about the world economy? That a business is never only a business. In nineteen ninety, a burger in Moscow meant the walls between East and West were coming down. It took fourteen years of politics to open the door, and one war to close it. The kitchens are still there, and so are the customers. What left was the name, and the idea that trade alone would keep the world together.
