By April 2024, US car insurance prices were up 22.6% in a single year, the biggest jump since 1976 (BLS; comparison is ours). Over five years they rose about 49%, more than twice as fast as prices overall. What made car insurance so expensive, and who made money? Four forces: the cars (used-car and repair prices, sensor-packed bumpers), the crashes (road deaths peaked at 43,230 in 2021), the courts and the states (legal costs, Florida's 2023 reform, credit-score rules), and the insurers' own books (from a $1.39 billion loss at Allstate in 2022 to $10.17 billion of net income in 2025). And the twist: in 2026, prices have started to fall.
