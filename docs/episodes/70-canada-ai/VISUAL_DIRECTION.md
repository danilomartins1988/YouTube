# Visual direction: Why Canada Could Be the Biggest Winner of the AI Boom (Ep 70)

Applied file: `projects/why-canada-could-win-the-ai-boom/visual_direction.yaml`. Bespoke kit: `remotion/src/animation-engine/CartoonStage/canada.tsx`
(registered in CartoonStage.tsx with one import and three spread entries; plus a tiny shared addition: items flagged `hud: true` render in
screen space, used so every caption stays inside the frame while the camera moves).

- Genre: prime-time TV-cartoon energy at genre level only (thick outlines, flat colour, squash and stretch); original cast and sets; day scenes.
- Cast (all fictional): MAYA (young AI researcher in Montreal, purple top, round glasses, teal scarf), WHIRR (walking data-center cabinet
  with LED eyes and a belly fan that spins fast and sweats in the desert, slows, puffs frosty breath and gains a red scarf in the north),
  LUC (her dad, a miner with a gold cap and hi-vis orange), MAYA'S UNCLE (car-parts shop).
- Sets: caapt, cadesert, cachart (illustrative chart, labelled), camap (original stylized map, not a traced outline; simplified border),
  cadam (variants quebec / bc), castage, cacold, camine, calab, caborder, careceipt, catitle, cabumper.
- No logos, no flags, no maple leaves, no real person drawn (Hinton, Bengio named in narration only); companies appear only as plain
  plaques (HYDRO-QUÉBEC) or not at all. Places are landscapes (rivers, rock and lakes, snow, triplex staircases), never stereotypes.
- Every on-screen number carries a claim pill; one big number on screen at a time.
