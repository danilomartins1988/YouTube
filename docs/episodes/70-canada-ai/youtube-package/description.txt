Is Canada's economy crashing? The headlines say so. But the AI boom is shopping for four things, and Canada has all of them: clean hydro power, a cold climate that cools servers for free, critical minerals, and the labs where deep learning grew up. Meet Maya, a fictional young AI researcher in Montreal weighing a job offer from California, and Whirr, a fictional overheating data center fleeing the desert for the north. Following them through dams, snowfields, mines and labs, we test the crash belief against the map, and then look hard at the catch: tariffs and dependence on the US, power that now has to be built, mines that take years, and talent and startups pulled south. (Our answer: Canada could be one of the big winners of the AI boom, but having the shopping list isn't the same as winning. It wins only if it builds: power lines, mines, data centers, and companies that grow up at home.)

Key figures (exact values; the film rounds them). US dollars at the Federal Reserve G.5A 2025 average, 1.3973 CAD per USD.
- Economy: real GDP fell 0.4% in Q2 2025 (after +0.5% in Q1) as goods exports dropped under US tariffs (Statistics Canada). Bank of Canada, Oct 28, 2026: Q2 2026 growth 3.3% annualized, about 2% indicated for Q3; growth expected to slow in Q4 under new US tariffs and pick up in 2027 (FORECAST); unemployment around 6.5%.
- Power: about 60% of Canada's electricity comes from hydro; Canada was the world's third-largest hydro generator in 2023 (Canada Energy Regulator).
- Quebec (same figures as our Ep 67): a 50 MW industrial customer's bill, rates of Apr 1, 2025, excluding taxes: Montréal 100 vs Boston 445 (22%; Hydro-Québec's own comparison, an interested party); Montréal ≈ 5.52 CAD cents/kWh ≈ 3.95 US cents. PROPOSED data-center Rate CD: $32.704/kW + 8.710 cents/kWh, ≈ 14.0 CAD cents/kWh at an 85% load factor (≈ 10.0 US cents), ≈ 2.5× today (our calculation; BLG: "roughly double"); not approved, Régie decision pending. Loads of 5 MW or more need ministerial authorization.
- British Columbia: AI and data-centre projects of 10 MW or more now compete for power; the first two-year call (opened Jan 30, 2026) targets up to 400 MW for AI and data centres (BC government). Outcome not covered here.
- Cold: QScale says cooling at its Lévis, Quebec site is free up to 80% of the year (company claim, 2022; depends on design).
- Money arriving (company PLANS): Microsoft C$19bn in Canada 2023-2027 ≈ US$13.6bn (our calculation), incl. C$7.5bn+ over two years from Dec 2025; Bell AI Fabric aims for up to 500 MW of hydro-powered AI compute across six B.C. facilities, with waste heat to Thompson Rivers University in Kamloops.
- Minerals: 34 critical minerals on Canada's list (June 2024 update), including nickel, copper, uranium, lithium and graphite (NRCan); Canada is the world's second-largest uranium producer, about 15% of world output in 2022 (CER). Major mines that came online in 2010-2019 took over 16 years on average from discovery to first production (IEA 2021, global sample). McIlvenna Bay (Saskatchewan): referred to the Major Projects Office Sept 2025, first copper concentrate June 2026 (about 88% built by Jan 2026).
- Brains: Geoffrey Hinton (University of Toronto) shared the 2024 Nobel Prize in Physics with John Hopfield. Mila, founded by Yoshua Bengio, describes itself as the world's largest academic research center for deep learning. Vector Institute launched in 2017. CIFAR: the 2017 Pan-Canadian AI Strategy was the world's first national AI strategy. ISED opened its AI Sovereign Compute Infrastructure Program call on Apr 15, 2026 (a Canadian-owned AI supercomputer).
- Talent: Statistics Canada (Jul 2025) finds retention of STEM graduates in Canada has improved; international graduates still leave at higher rates.
- Trade: 71.7% of Canada's merchandise exports went to the US in 2025 (75.9% in 2024) (Statistics Canada).
