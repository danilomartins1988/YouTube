# Owner production prompt (2026-11-18): THE HIDDEN ECONOMICS — EPISODE 1: Why the World Still Depends on Chinese Factories

Verbatim owner brief (formatting condensed). Applies with docs/COMMERCIAL_DIRECTIVE.md, docs/SEO_DIRECTIVE.md, docs/PRODUCTION_STANDARD.md.
Note: Ep 60 (docs/episodes/60-china-factories) covered the same subject in the sitcom format; this is a NEW production to this brief (series Episode 1 of a two-part arc; Episode 2 = "Could AI End China's Manufacturing Dominance?"). Ep 60 is kept, not overwritten.

## Mission
Executive Producer, Head Writer, Investigative Researcher, YouTube Growth Strategist and Animation Director. A complete, premium, commercially compelling documentary on how China became the world's manufacturing powerhouse and why replacing its industrial ecosystem is so difficult. An economic thriller, not a lecture.
Primary title: Why the World Still Depends on Chinese Factories. Alternatives to evaluate: Why the World Can't Stop Buying From China; Why America Can't Replace Chinese Factories; The Manufacturing Empire the World Can't Escape; Why Everything Still Comes From China; China's Real Advantage Isn't Cheap Labor.
Audience: mainstream American viewers (money, technology, business, global power, competition). 11–13 min. Premium animated documentary 1920×1080. Natural American English. Objective: qualified clicks, satisfaction, watch time, recommendations, evergreen discovery. Follow all existing branding, narration, animation, character, research and SEO standards.

## 1. Big commercial idea
Why does the world still depend on Chinese factories after years of tariffs, tension and relocation attempts? Begin with a familiar American product (smartphone, laptop, EV, appliance); show the networks of factories, suppliers, machinery, logistics and expertise behind it. Central mystery (hypothetical, not a prediction): "If China stopped manufacturing tomorrow, how much of the global economy would actually stop?" The audience must discover it's far more than cheap labor.

## 2. Opening hook (20–30 s)
Direction: "Imagine waking up tomorrow and discovering that the world's largest manufacturing economy had stopped producing. Your next phone might become harder to buy. Factories thousands of miles away could run short of components. Companies that spent years trying to leave China might discover they never really left. How did one country become so deeply embedded in the world's economy?" Rewrite for maximum impact with verified examples, no unsupported claims. No history lecture first. Real mystery, immediate stakes. Channel intro after the hook.

## 3. Structure
Act 1 The World Without China (0:00–1:30): hypothetical disruption; recognizable products through their supply chains; hidden dependencies.
Act 2 How China Built the Machine (1:30–3:15): reforms, investment, infrastructure, clusters, trade, global demand; concise, not a chronological list of political events.
Act 3 The Advantage Nobody Sees (3:15–5:45): supplier proximity, specialization, scale, logistics, engineering expertise, development speed, infrastructure, automation, component availability; moving one factory ≠ recreating a supply chain.
Act 4 Why Companies Struggle to Leave (5:45–8:00): relocation to Vietnam, India, Mexico, the US; economics of relocating; final assembly moves while Chinese components remain; verified examples.
Act 5 The Hidden Cost of Dependence (8:00–10:15): geopolitical risk, trade restrictions, concentration, business interruption, inventory and logistics, strategic vulnerabilities; efficiency creating systemic dependence.
Act 6 The Surprising Conclusion (10:15–12:30): answer the question; which advantages are hard to reproduce, which may erode; introduce the next mystery (AI and robotics changing manufacturing economics) and transition to Episode 2 without making this one feel incomplete.

## 4. Research (latest verified figures with reporting years)
China's share of global manufacturing value added; major manufacturing export categories; supply-chain concentration; industrial robot adoption; ports and logistics; manufacturing wages and productivity; industrial clusters; foreign manufacturing investment; reshoring and friendshoring; dependence on Chinese intermediate goods. Prioritize UNIDO, World Bank, OECD, WTO, International Federation of Robotics, US Census, USITC, corporate financial and supply-chain disclosures, reputable investigative financial journalism. No fabricated numbers; never claim everything is made in China. Distinguish manufacturing output, value added, exports and final assembly.

## 5. Visual sequences (at least five, presenter builds and explains)
A The $1,000 Smartphone: illustrative supply chain — components, assembly, logistics, value creation across countries; verified product data where available, otherwise labeled hypothetical.
B The Manufacturing Ecosystem: one Chinese factory connected to dozens of suppliers, transport networks, engineering teams, industrial services.
C The Factory Relocation Experiment: move a hypothetical factory out of China; reveal which suppliers, machines, components and services still need importing.
D The Global Dependency Map: selected verified trade and manufacturing links among China, the US, Europe and other hubs.
E The Hidden Economics of Scale: supplier concentration, volume, fixed costs, logistics vs cost per unit; simple numerical example with labeled assumptions.
No repetitive stock images in place of meaningful animation.

## 6. Storytelling rules
Every scene advances the story. Use mysteries, unexpected dependencies, recognizable products, clear financial consequences, surprising comparisons, visual reveals, concrete numbers. Avoid long history, political speeches, generic AI narration, repetitive drama, jargon, unsupported geopolitical predictions, repeated static factory footage.

## 7. Title, thumbnail, SEO
Research demand and comparable documentaries; 12-month and 5-year history where accessible; no invented volumes. Ten title candidates, three thumbnail concepts. Preferred thumbnail: a visually enormous Chinese industrial complex, a recognizable China symbol, and a network of global products or trade routes; very short text such as "THE WORLD NEEDS CHINA?" or "CAN'T REPLACE CHINA?"; complements the title; mobile-legible. Optimized description, chapters, tags, source links, three standalone Shorts.

## 8. Ending and cross-episode strategy
Complete, satisfying answer; then: "For decades, the world built its factories around China. But what if the next industrial revolution changes the cost of making things altogether?" Connect to "Could AI End China's Manufacturing Dominance?" Clickable end-screen reference only if Episode 2 is already published.

## Final creative direction
Don't merely explain why China manufactures so much; reveal the hidden industrial system that makes the global economy dependent on Chinese production. Viewer starts thinking "Why can't companies just move their factories?" and finishes understanding "Because moving a factory is not the same as moving an entire industrial ecosystem." Fascinating, visually distinctive, rigorous, commercially competitive.
