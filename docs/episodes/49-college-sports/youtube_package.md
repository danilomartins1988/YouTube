For more than a century, college athletes could not be paid by their schools. Since July 2025, each school can pay its athletes up to $20.5 million a year, rising to $21.58 million in 2026-27 (College Sports Commission). In year one, schools paid about $1.77 billion to 34,915 athletes. So where does that money come from, who gets it, and what does it cost the rest of college sports? We follow the court settlement that created the payroll, the cap formula, the new roster limits, the conference TV money behind it, the sports schools cut, the Title IX question, the clearinghouse that checks every endorsement deal, and the fight over whether athletes are employees.

Every figure is sourced below: the College Sports Commission, the House v. NCAA final approval order (2025), the NCAA, the SEC, Ohio State and Stephen F. Austin, the Third Circuit (Johnson v. NCAA), the White House and the Senate Commerce Committee. The 2034-35 cap is the plaintiffs' expert estimate; shares and differences marked as ours are our own calculations.
