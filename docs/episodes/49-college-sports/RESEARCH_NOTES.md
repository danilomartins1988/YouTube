# Episode 49: "College Sports Just Got a $20.5 Million Payroll". Research notes

Fact-check date: 2026-11-10. All amounts are in US dollars. Each bullet gives the claim, then the exact quote or figure, the URL, and the date published. "Fetched" means the primary page or PDF was opened and read. "Snippet" means the figure came only from search results or secondary coverage and has not been read on a primary page.

## 1. The House v. NCAA settlement (In re College Athlete NIL Litigation, N.D. Cal. No. 4:20-cv-03919-CW)

- **Final approval: June 6, 2025, Judge Claudia Wilken.** The document is "OPINION REGARDING ORDER GRANTING MOTION FOR FINAL APPROVAL OF SETTLEMENT AGREEMENT", Case 4:20-cv-03919-CW, Document 978, "Filed 06/06/25", 76 pages. Source: the court PDF, copied by SwimSwam: https://swimswam.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/Grant-House-vs-NCAA-Order-Granting-Final-Approval-to-Settlement.pdf . Fetched; the text was extracted from the court-stamped PDF. The copy is hosted by a third party, but every page carries the court's header.
  - NCAA LSDBi corroborates the date: Proposal 2025-11 was "Adopted Final, based on approval of the House settlement" on Jun 6, 2025. URL: https://web3.ncaa.org/lsdbi/search/proposalView?id=108635 . Fetched.
- **Class size.** "Plaintiffs represent that there are 389,700 current members of the settlement classes." Source: Order p. 2 (same PDF). Fetched.
- **Damages total: $2.576 billion, not $2.78bn.** The order says: "Defendants have agreed to pay $2.576 billion in total to compensate members of the damages classes. Damages payments will be paid yearly over the course of ten years following the Effective Date." Order p. 9. Fetched.
  - Split: an NIL Settlement Fund of "$1.976 billion" (BNIL $1.815bn, videogame $71.5M, lost-opportunity NIL $89.5M) and "a $600 million fund" for "Additional Compensation Claims" (pay-for-play). Order pp. 9–10. Fetched.
  - The order describes the BNIL money as going to the "Football and Men's Basketball Class and Women's Basketball Class". Footnote 16 says the allocation favors football and men's basketball "on the basis that schools and conferences received far more revenues from those sports". Order p. 63. Fetched.
- **The revenue-sharing pool: 22%.** "...additional direct benefits and compensation to Division I student-athletes that are worth up to 22% of the Power Five schools' average athletic revenues each year, subject to specified yearly increases (hereinafter, the 'Pool')." Order p. 11. Fetched.
- **Growth path (plaintiffs' expert, an ESTIMATE).** "Dr. Rascher estimates that the annual Pool cap will start at more than $20 million per school in the 2025-26 school year and grow to $32.9 million per school in 2034-35." He also estimates "additional spending of up to $1.6 billion for 2025-26, growing to $2.3 billion in 2034-35, and totaling at least $19.4 billion for the 10-year period." Order p. 11. Fetched. Label these as estimates on screen.
- **2025-26 cap: $20.5M. CONFIRMED (CSC).** "Each participating school could share up to $20.5 million with its athletes in 2025-26 and decide how to divide that money across its sports and rosters." URL: https://www.collegesportscommission.org/college-sports-commission-tracks-1-8-billion-in-payments-to-student-athletes-during-first-year-of-revenue-sharing/ . Published Oct 1, 2026. Fetched (raw page text checked).
- **2026-27 cap: $21.58M. CONFIRMED (CSC).** "The 2026-27 cap year began July 1, 2026, and because the revenue-sharing cap increases annually under the settlement, each participating school can now share up to $21.58 million with its student-athletes." Same URL, Oct 1, 2026. Fetched (raw page text checked).
  - Our calculation, not an official figure: $21.58M is about a 5.3% rise on $20.5M.
  - The secondary figure of "$21.3 million", which equals a 4% rise, is NOT what the CSC states. Use $21.58M.
- **What year one actually paid out. CSC, official.**
  - "The total amount of distributions tracked in CAPS was $1,975,973,365, which included $1,770,453,776 in revenue-sharing payments, $42,034,090 in Alston Awards and $163,485,499 in new and incremental scholarship spending."
  - "covering 34,915 student-athletes at 307 schools across 33 conferences and 45 sports."
  - "Of the 319 opted-in schools, 307 compensated student-athletes directly and 68 reached the cap or came within five percent of it."
  - Headline: "$1.8 billion in direct payments". The cap year ended June 30, 2026.
  - URL as above, Oct 1, 2026. Fetched.
- **Expert compared to the pros (estimate).** Rascher "estimates that, in 2025-2026, the combined value of existing benefits ... and the Pool benefits ... will represent approximately 50% of Division I revenues ... similar to the share of professional sports revenue that is shared with professional athletes." Order pp. 30–31. Fetched.
- **Scholarship limits removed.** Rascher "estimates that the elimination of the scholarship limits could result in more than 115,000 additional scholarships being made available to Division I athletes on an annual basis." Order p. 11. Fetched. This is an estimate, and it is a maximum, not money actually spent.

## 2. Roster limits and Title IX

- **Scholarship caps were replaced by roster caps.** "Moving forward, NCAA rules for Division I programs will no longer include sport-specific scholarship limits." Schools "will have the option to offer scholarships to any and all of those student-athletes". The changes are "effective July 1"; the opt-in deadline was June 30. URL: http://www.ncaa.org/media-center-di-board-of-directors-formally-adopts-changes-to-roster-limits/ . Published June 23, 2025. Fetched.
- **Roster limits by sport.** NCAA Bylaw 17.2, Proposal 2025-11, effective July 1, 2025.
  - Main sports: football 105; men's and women's basketball 15 each; baseball 34; softball 25; men's and women's soccer 28 each; women's volleyball 18; women's rowing 68; men's and women's golf 9 each; men's and women's tennis 10 each; women's bowling 11.
  - The rule applies to schools in defendant conferences or that opt in.
  - URL: https://web3.ncaa.org/lsdbi/search/proposalView?id=108635 . Fetched.
- **"Designated student-athletes" are grandfathered.** Athletes whose roster spots were or would have been taken away "will be exempt from roster limits at any Division I school for the duration of their college athletics careers." Order p. 12. Fetched. The NCAA release of June 23, 2025 repeats this. Fetched.
- **Judge on Title IX.** The court overruled the Title IX objections: "schools will be free to allocate those benefits and compensation in a manner that complies with Title IX." The order also says "the SA does not require class members to release claims arising out of Title IX". Class members "will have the right to file lawsuits" if schools violate Title IX. Order pp. 63–64. Fetched.
  - Safe wording: "The judge didn't decide how Title IX applies. She left that fight for later."
- **Appeals are PENDING.** Ninth Circuit No. 25-4137 consolidates several objector appeals, several of them raising Title IX. Back-damages payments wait on the appeals; revenue sharing continues. Source: secondary coverage (Venable, Front Office Sports), for example https://www.venable.com/insights/publications/2025/12/a-settlement-that-remains-unsettled-title-ix . Snippet only. No ruling was found as of 2026-11-10.
- **Federal Title IX guidance has flipped.** The Education Department's Office for Civil Rights (OCR) fact sheet of January 2025 treated school NIL payments as athletic financial aid subject to proportionality. It was rescinded on Feb 12, 2025. Source: law-firm summaries, for example https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/education/1593148/department-of-education-rescinds-title-ix-guidance-on-nil-payments . Snippet. The ed.gov release was not fetched.
- **Executive Order 14400, "Urgent National Action to Save College Sports".** Signed April 3, 2026.
  - Sec. 4(b)(iv): revenue sharing should be implemented "in a manner that preserves or expands scholarships and collegiate athletic opportunities in women's and Olympic sports."
  - It also proposes "a prohibition on the use of Federal funds" for NIL and revenue-sharing payments.
  - "Sections 3 through 6 of this order shall be effective on August 1, 2026."
  - URL: https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/04/urgent-national-action-to-save-college-sports/ . Fetched.
  - Federal Register: 2026-06961, 91 FR 18267 (from the snippet).

## 3. The College Sports Commission and NIL Go

- **What the CSC reviews.**
  - Third-party NIL deals worth "$600 or more in aggregate" must be reported through NIL Go.
  - The CSC rates each deal "cleared, not cleared, or returned for revision".
  - The test is whether a deal serves a "valid business purpose" and falls within "a reasonable range of compensation".
  - URL: https://www.collegesportscommission.org/about/ . Fetched.
  - Revenue-sharing payments are tracked separately in "CAPS". Source: the Oct 1, 2026 CSC release (fetched).
  - The CSC describes itself as "an independent organization established in 2025".
- **NIL Go launch date.** "Since the NIL Go platform launched on June 11, 2025". Source: CSC NIL Deal Flow Report of Mar 10, 2026, PDF: https://assets.tina.io/29b83311-e587-42b1-861e-87ebde9aa253/NIL%20Deal%20Flow%20Report%203.10.2026.pdf . Fetched (text extracted).
- **Latest official totals, launch through Oct 31, 2026.**
  - Cleared: 56,570 deals, $775.11M.
  - Not cleared: 2,877 deals, $177.14M.
  - Sept 1–Oct 31, 2026: 10,092 cleared ($192.62M), of which associated deals were 6,599 ($140.53M); 581 not cleared ($20.21M).
  - Deals in arbitration as of 10/31/26: 0. Average deals actioned per day: 175.
  - URL: https://www.collegesportscommission.org/nil-data-report/nil-data-report-september-1-october-31-2026/ . Published Nov 9, 2026. Fetched.
  - Our calculation: about 4.8% of decided deals were not cleared (2,877 of 59,447). By value it is about 18.6%. Not-cleared deals average far larger amounts. Label this as our math.
- **Earlier snapshot (Mar 10, 2026 report).** 21,025 cleared ($166.50M) and 711 not cleared ($29.30M) as of Feb 28, 2026. Registered users: 38,242 student-athletes. Fetched (PDF above).
- **"Associated" money surge.** In Jan–Feb 2026, at A4 (Power 4) schools: "Associated A4 aggregate deal value increased by 364%". Associated deals were "79% of the total deal value of A4 deals". "Associated deals are subject to increased scrutiny". Same PDF. Fetched.
  - An associated entity is, roughly, a collective or booster-linked entity. The full definition is quoted in the PDF.
- **First report, corrected.** "Deals cleared: 6,090 (total value $35.42M)" for June 11–Aug 31, 2025. The originally posted total was "$79.8M", described as "Total deals in the NIL GO system". URL: https://www.collegesportscommission.org/nil-data-report/correction-nil-data-report-september-5-2026/ (the URL slug says 2026 but the report is dated Sept 5, 2025). Fetched.
- **First-year NIL total.** "46,478 NIL deals were cleared in NIL Go with an aggregate value of $582.49 million through August 31, 2026." URL: https://www.collegesportscommission.org/college-sports-commission-compensation-data/ . Published Oct 30, 2026. Fetched.
- **Deloitte built and runs NIL Go: SNIPPET ONLY.** None of the fetched CSC pages mention Deloitte. Front Office Sports reported that the CSC blamed Deloitte for the first report's data error: https://frontofficesports.com/nil-go-corrects-its-data-from-80m-in-deals-cleared-to-35m-blames-deloitte/ . See Flags.

## 5. Are athletes employees?

- **Johnson v. NCAA, 3d Cir. No. 22-1223, filed July 11, 2024.** Fetched from https://www2.ca3.uscourts.gov/opinarch/221223p.pdf .
  - Holding: "we will vacate and remand for application of an economic realities analysis grounded in common-law agency principles."
  - "college athletes cannot be barred as a matter of law from asserting FLSA claims simply by virtue of a 'revered tradition of amateurism' in D-I athletics."
  - And: "we will not use a 'frayed tradition' of amateurism..."
  - Safe wording: "The court didn't say athletes ARE employees. It said they can't be ruled out just because they're 'amateurs'."
  - An amending order of July 25, 2024 corrected an amicus's surname only.
- **NLRB / Dartmouth.** The union (SEIU Local 560) withdrew the Dartmouth men's basketball petition on Dec 31, 2024. The NLRB General Counsel's memo GC 25-05 of Feb 14, 2025 rescinded a batch of memos, reportedly including GC 21-08 on "players at academic institutions". Both are SNIPPETS (AP, Ogletree). The nlrb.gov pages were not fetched (404 at the guessed URL).
- **SCORE Act (H.R. 4312, 119th Congress).** Introduced July 2025.
  - It was pulled from the House floor twice: in December 2025 and in May 2026 (Bloomberg Government, May 19, 2026).
  - SNIPPET only: congress.gov returned 403 to our fetch. Rules Committee print 119-29: https://rules.house.gov/bill-text/rules-committee-print-119-29 (not fetched).
  - Status: no House passage found as of 2026-11-10.
- **Protect College Sports Act (S. 4668).** "September 28, 2026": the Senate passed it "77-22". The bill is by Sens. Cruz and Cantwell and moves to the House. URL: https://www.commerce.senate.gov/press/rep/release/senate-overwhelmingly-passes-protect-college-sports-act/ . Fetched.
  - The release does not describe the bill's employee-status provision. Coverage says it would keep athletes from being deemed employees (snippet).
  - The House had not voted as of early November 2026; lame-duck consideration is uncertain (AP, Nov 5, 2026, snippet). PENDING.

## Flags / not verified (use safe wording)

- **"$2.78 billion" or "$2.8 billion" damages.** The court order says **$2.576 billion**. The "$2.8 billion" figure is press shorthand, and SFA's own release also uses it. Say "about $2.6 billion in back damages", or "a deal often called the $2.8 billion settlement" if quoting the headline.
- **2026-27 cap.** Use the CSC's **$21.58 million**. Do NOT use "$21.3 million" (secondary sources only). Do not state an exact annual growth rate. The order says only "specified yearly increases". "About 4% a year" appears only in secondary sources, and the actual 2026-27 increase was larger. Safe: "the cap rises every year under the settlement".
- **"22% of average Power conference revenue".** The order's wording is "22% of the Power Five schools' average athletic revenues each year". Use that phrasing.
- **$1.8 billion vs. $1.77 billion.** The CSC headline says $1.8B in "direct payments". The precise revenue-sharing figure is $1,770,453,776. The CAPS total including Alston awards and scholarships is $1.976B. Pick one and label it.
- **Deloitte.** Not on any fetched CSC page. Safe: "a clearinghouse called NIL Go, built with an outside consulting firm (reported to be Deloitte)", or simply omit the firm's name.
- **Sport-by-sport and gender split of revenue sharing.** The CSC compensation pages fetched did not show sport or gender breakdowns; the separate report PDFs were not opened. Do not claim "X% went to football".
- **Title IX.** Ninth Circuit appeals (No. 25-4137) are PENDING, and the SFA litigation is PENDING. The 2025 OCR rescission is a snippet only. Safe: "Whether Title IX requires equal splits is still being fought in court."
- **SCORE Act and Protect College Sports Act.** Neither is law as of 2026-11-10. SCORE status is from snippets because congress.gov blocked our fetch. Safe: "Congress has bills; none has become law."
- **NLRB / Dartmouth and GC memos.** Snippets only. Safe: "The Dartmouth players' union petition was withdrawn at the end of 2024."
- **CFP $7.8B, Big Ten/SEC TV contract values, Big Ten per-school averages.** Not officially disclosed or not fetched. Use "reportedly".
- **Texas $375.9M spending; Ohio State FY2024 deficit.** Secondary only.
- **Rascher numbers** ($32.9M by 2034-35, $19.4B, 115,000 scholarships, about 50% revenue share): all expert ESTIMATES quoted in the order. Label them on screen.
- **Roster-limit list.** This is the NCAA LSDBi version, effective July 1, 2025. Women's flag football (25, from 2027-28) was added later (snippet).
