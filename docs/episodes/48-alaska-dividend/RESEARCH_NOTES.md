## 2. Eligibility and headcounts

- **2026 eligibility checklist, as worded by the PFD Division:** "I was a resident of Alaska during all of calendar year 2025"; "On the date I apply for the 2026 Permanent Fund Dividend, I have the intent to remain an Alaska resident indefinitely"; "I was physically present in Alaska for at least 72 consecutive hours at some time during 2024 or 2025." The page also lists rules on absences longer than 180 days, felony sentences and incarceration, and claims of residency elsewhere. https://pfd.alaska.gov/Eligibility/Eligibility-Requirements. Fetched.
- **Statute AS 43.23.005(a):** an individual is eligible if the individual "(1) applies to the department; (2) is a state resident on the date of application; (3) was a state resident during the entire qualifying year; (4) has been physically present in the state for at least 72 consecutive hours at some time during the prior two years before the current dividend year". The statute also includes citizenship and lawful-status conditions. https://www.akleg.gov/basis/statutes.asp (sec. 43.23.005). Fetched.
- **2025 (latest full year on the table):** 665,327 applications received, 618,863 paid, $619,648,403.43 disbursed. State population was 738,737. Summary table. Fetched.
- **2024:** the summary table shows 666,213 received, 624,489 paid, $1,062,880,278.00. The 2024 Annual Report instead says 621,902 qualified applicants and about $1,058,477,204 "by the end of calendar year 2024". Fetched. The two documents count at different cut-off dates, so cite one of them and name it.
- **Visual stat:** roughly 619,000 paid out of a population of about 739,000 in 2025. That is about 84% of residents **[calc: 618,863 / 738,737]**. Fine for a cartoon crowd shot, labelled as an approximation.

## 3. The Permanent Fund

- **Creation, 1976:** HJR 39 (SLA 1976) proposed adding Article IX, Section 15. The text: "At least twenty-five per cent of all mineral lease rentals, royalties, royalty sale proceeds, federal mineral revenue sharing payments and bonuses received by the State shall be placed in a permanent fund, the principal of which shall be used only for those income-producing investments specifically designated by law as eligible for permanent fund investments. All income from the permanent fund shall be deposited in the general fund unless otherwise provided by law." https://www.akleg.gov/pdf/09/Bills/Final/HJR%2039%20SLA%201976.pdf. Fetched (a scanned PDF; the OCR was cleaned up when quoting). APFC's September 2026 release says "Established in 1976". Fetched.
- **The statute goes beyond the 25% floor:** AS 37.13.010(a) sets 25% for leases issued on or before Dec. 1, 1979, and **50%** for leases issued after Dec. 1, 1979, and 50% of bonuses from leases issued after Feb. 15, 1980. https://www.akleg.gov/basis/statutes.asp (sec. 37.13.010). Fetched. Safe wording: "the Constitution requires at least a quarter; for newer leases, state law sends half."
- **Current fund value (audited, latest):** "As of June 30, 2026, the end of FY26: The Alaska Permanent Fund had an audited total Fund balance of $91.9 billion." Source: APFC news release, 2026-09-02. https://apfc.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/202609_EAC-and-BOT-Regular-Meeting-Press-Release.pdf. Fetched. The same release calls it "the largest sovereign wealth fund in the U.S." (APFC's own claim).
- **Principal vs Earnings Reserve Account (same release):** "The value of the permanent savings in the Principal amounted to $59.4 billion, with $14.4 billion associated unrealized appreciation." The ERA "contained a $4.0 billion commitment for the FY27 Percent of Market Value (POMV) draw, $10.6 billion in realized earnings, and $3.5 billion in unrealized appreciation." Fetched.
- **FY26 return and flows (same release):** "The total Fund return for the fiscal year was 12.42% net of fees." "The FY26 POMV draw to the general fund amounted to $3.8 billion, and mineral royalty deposits to the corpus totaled $535 million." "Given the lack of appropriation, there was no transfer from the ERA to the Principal for FY26 inflation proofing, which is estimated to be $1.5 billion." Fetched.
  - Visual angle: in FY26 the fund's returns brought in far more than new oil royalties added. Realized statutory net income was $8.2 billion against $535 million of royalty deposits (same release).
- **The 2018 POMV law:** SB 26 (30th Legislature) is "An Act relating to the earnings of the Alaska permanent fund and the earnings reserve account; relating to the mental health trust fund...". Status: "SIGNED INTO LAW 6/13 CHAPTER 16 SLA 18". https://www.akleg.gov/basis/Bill/Detail/30?Root=SB%2026. Fetched.
- **POMV rule now in statute, AS 37.13.140(b):** "The amount available for appropriation is five percent of the average market value of the fund for the first five of the preceding six fiscal years, including the fiscal year just ended". https://www.akleg.gov/basis/statutes.asp (sec. 37.13.140). Fetched.
- **Fund vs oil in the unrestricted general fund (DOR Spring 2026 Revenue Forecast, Table 2-2)**, https://tax.alaska.gov/programs/documentviewer/viewer.aspx?2001r. Fetched.
  - FY2025 (history): Permanent Fund $3,657.3M; unrestricted petroleum $1,910.5M; total UGF $6,342.5M. So the Fund supplied about **57.7%** of UGF and oil about **30.1%** **[calc]**.
  - FY2026 (forecast): Permanent Fund $3,798.9M; petroleum $1,846.8M; total UGF $6,491.9M, about **58.5%** vs **28.4%** **[calc; forecast]**.
  - DOR wording: "The largest single component of unrestricted revenue is the Percent of Market Value (POMV) transfer from the Permanent Fund of $3.8 billion for FY 2026 and an estimated $4.0 billion for FY 2027." APFC (2026-09-02) says the Fund "serves as the primary source of revenue for Alaska's unrestricted general fund." Both fetched.
  - Script line: "Today the savings account, not the oil, is Alaska's biggest paycheck: nearly three of every five unrestricted dollars."

## 4. The statutory formula vs what was actually paid

- **The formula in law today:** AS 37.13.140(a): "Income available for distribution equals 21 percent of the net income of the fund for the last five fiscal years". AS 37.13.145(b): the corporation "shall transfer from the earnings reserve account to the dividend fund ... 50 percent of the income available for distribution". AS 43.23.025 sets the per-person amount: the total available divided by the number of eligible individuals. akleg.gov statutes. Fetched. Plain English: half of 21% of five years of net income goes to dividends (my paraphrase; confirm on screen).
- **The 2016 veto:** Wielechowski v. State, Alaska Supreme Court, Opinion No. 7194, Supreme Court No. S-16558, August 25, 2017. The opinion reads: "In May 2016 the legislature passed an appropriation bill that included an estimated $1.362 billion transfer ... But in June Governor Bill Walker exercised his line-item veto power and reduced the estimated $1.362 billion transfer to $695.65 million. The legislature met in July but did not vote to override the governor's veto. This resulted in 2016 Permanent Fund dividend payments of $1,022 to eligible Alaskans, about half of what had been expected". Slip opinion PDF: https://media.ktoo.org/2017/08/sp-7194.pdf. Fetched. This is a news outlet's copy of the court's slip opinion; for the on-screen cite use 403 P.3d 1141 (Alaska 2017) (snippet only) or the courts.alaska.gov copy.
- **The holding:** "The plain language of the 1976 constitutional amendment creating the Permanent Fund does not exempt Permanent Fund income from the constraints of the anti-dedication clause." and "Absent another constitutional amendment, the Permanent Fund dividend program must compete for annual legislative funding just as other state programs." Same PDF. Fetched.
- **Statutory vs actual, 2026:** the Governor's FY2027 budget (released Dec. 11, 2025) proposed a "Full statutory Permanent Fund Dividend ($3,650 per Alaskan)". https://gov.alaska.gov/fy-2027-budget-at-a-glance/. Fetched. The enacted amount was $1,000 + $200 (Section 5). The proposed full statutory dividend was about 3x the amount actually paid **[calc, using the governor's estimate]**.
- **Since 2016 (paid amounts, summary table):** 2016 $1,022; 2017 $1,100; 2018 $1,600; 2019 $1,606; 2020 $992; 2021 $1,114; 2022 $3,284 (incl. $662 relief); 2023 $1,312; 2024 $1,702 (incl. $298.17 relief); 2025 $1,000; 2026 $1,200 (incl. $200 relief). The year-by-year "statutory" comparison was NOT found on a primary page (see Flags).

## 5. 2026 budget: HB 263 (34th Legislature)

- **HB 263** is "APPROP: OPERATING BUDGET;FUNDS;SUPP". Status: "(H) SIGNED INTO LAW 6/24 CHAPTER 34 SLA 26". The House record shows "PASSED Y21 N19" (later "PASSED ON RECONSIDERATION Y22 N18" / "Y21 N19"). The bill went through a conference committee with HB 265, and the bill page lists a "LINE ITEM VETO AND REDUCTIONS" document. https://www.akleg.gov/basis/Bill/Detail/34?Root=HB%20263. Fetched.
- **Enrolled HB 263, Sec. 18(d):** "The amount necessary for the payment of a permanent fund dividend of $1,000 to each eligible individual and for administrative and associated costs, estimated to be $674,071,200, is appropriated from the general fund to the dividend fund". https://www.akleg.gov/PDF/34/Bills/HB0263Z.PDF. Fetched.
- **Enrolled HB 263, Sec. 18(c):** "The sum of $3,996,865,095 is appropriated from the earnings reserve account (AS 37.13.145) to the general fund." This is the POMV draw. Sec. 18(f) appropriates inflation-proofing "estimated to be $1,491,000,000" from the ERA to principal. Same PDF. Fetched. Whether any of this was vetoed is unchecked; the veto PDF is a scan.
- **The $200 energy relief was conditional:** it was paid only if FY2026 actual UGF revenue, "as determined by the commissioner of revenue on August 31, 2026, exceeds $6,300,000,000". From the excess, money goes "(1) the amount necessary, estimated to be $127,340,800, to pay a one-time energy relief payment of $200 as part of the permanent fund dividend", then "$115,000,000 to the Department of Education and Early Development" for school districts. Same PDF (p. 82-83). Fetched. The PFD Division's Sept. 21 notice confirms it was paid.
  - Script angle: "Part of this year's check depended on oil prices. It paid out only if state revenue cleared $6.3 billion."
- **Tax-exemption intent language:** HB 263 directs the department to work with the Department of Law and the IRS "to seek a tax exemption for the energy relief payment portion of the 2026 Permanent Fund Dividend, consistent with the tax-exempt status of the 2022 energy relief payment." Same PDF. Fetched. This is intent only; the outcome is not verified.
- **Budget-fight path (news only):** the Senate draft had a $1,000 PFD + $150 energy relief, and the compromise reached $200 (Alaska Beacon, 2026-04-24 and 2026-05-18; Alaska Public Media, 2026-05-08). Snippet only. The House Finance vote rejecting a full statutory PFD 4-7 was seen only on a secondary site. Do not use these without an akleg journal page.

## 6. Research on effects (label as RESEARCH on screen)

- **Jones & Marinescu, employment:** "The Labor Market Impacts of Universal and Permanent Cash Transfers: Evidence from the Alaska Permanent Fund", NBER Working Paper 24312 (Feb 2018, rev. Jan 2020), published in American Economic Journal: Economic Policy 14(2): 315-340 (2022). Abstract: "we show that the dividend had no effect on employment, and increased part-time work by 1.8 percentage points (17%)" ... "Overall, our results suggest that a universal and permanent cash transfer does not significantly decrease aggregate employment." https://www.nber.org/papers/w24312. Fetched.
- **Watson, Guettabi & Reimer, crime:** "Universal Cash and Crime", Review of Economics and Statistics 102(4): 678-689 (2020). Abstract: "We find a 14% increase in substance-abuse incidents the day after the payment and a 10% increase over the following four weeks. This is partially offset by an 8% decrease in property crime, with no changes in violent crimes. On an annual basis, however, changes in criminal activity from the payment are small." https://ideas.repec.org/a/tpr/restat/v102y2020i4p678-689.html. Fetched (RePEc abstract page).
- **Chung, Ha & Kim, birth weight:** "Money Transfer and Birth Weight: Evidence from the Alaska Permanent Fund Dividend", Economic Inquiry 54(1): 576-590 (2016). Abstract: "income has a significantly positive, but modest effect on birth weight. We find that an additional $1,000 ($2,331 in 2011 dollars) increases birth weight by 17.7 g and substantially decreases the likelihood of a low birth weight (a decrease of around 14% of the sample mean)." https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/ecinqu/v54y2016i1p576-590.html. Fetched.
- **Kueng, spending:** "Excess Sensitivity of High-Income Consumers", Quarterly Journal of Economics 133(4) (2018), starting p. 1693. Abstract: "On average, the marginal propensity to consume (MPC) is 25% for nondurables and services within one quarter of the payments." It also finds high-income households with lots of liquid assets "have MPCs above 50%". https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/qjecon/v133y2018i4p1693-1751..html. Fetched.
- **ISER review (Guettabi, 2019):** "What do we know about the effects of the Alaska Permanent Fund Dividend?", Institute of Social and Economic Research, UAA, dated 5-20-2019. https://scholarworks.alaska.edu/uaa_iser_articles/17. The landing page was fetched (abstract only; no PDF attached). Its reported conclusions came through search snippets only: no negative labor-market effect, poverty reductions for rural Alaska Natives, and "the PFD increases income inequality in both the short and long run". Snippet only; get the PDF before quoting.
