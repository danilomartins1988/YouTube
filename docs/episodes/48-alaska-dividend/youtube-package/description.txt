Every year, almost every Alaskan gets a check from the state just for living there. In 2026 it was $1,200, including a $200 energy relief payment (Alaska PFD Division). By the formula in the state's own law, the Governor's office put the full amount at about $3,650 per person. So where does this money come from, and why does the check keep shrinking? We follow the oil royalties into the Permanent Fund, now worth about $91.9 billion, the 2016 veto and the 2017 court ruling, the 2018 five-percent draw that now pays for both the dividend and the state government, the 2026 budget, and what economists found when they studied the checks.
