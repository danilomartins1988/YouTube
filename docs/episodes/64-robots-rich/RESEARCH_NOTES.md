## Income split (labor vs capital)
- **BLS nonfarm business labor share** (FRED PRS85006173, index 2017=100): 2026 Q3 = 93.729, the series minimum (1947 Q1-2026 Q3);
  peak 117.123 (1960 Q4); 1947-2000 range 106.75-117.12; 2000 avg 111.6; 2010 avg 99.5; 2019 avg 100.0. −20.0% from peak (our calc).
  Latest quarters are preliminary and revised.
- **BEA shares of gross domestic income** (FRED): compensation of employees 58.4% (1970, series peak) → 50.9% (2025); domestic
  corporate profits after tax 4.5% (1970), 4.0% (1980) → 9.3% (2025; 2021 peak 9.4%). GDI 2025 ≈ $30.9T. Our arithmetic:
  7.5 points × $30.9T ≈ $2.3T a year (spoken as "more than two trillion dollars a year"; illustrative, not "money lost").
  Note: the other ≈40% includes depreciation, taxes on production and imports, net interest, rental and proprietors' income.
- Causes debated: Autor, Dorn, Katz, Patterson & Van Reenen (NBER w23396) "causes remain uncertain", superstar firms;
  Acemoglu & Restrepo (NBER w24196): automation raises output per worker more than wages and lowers labor's share; new tasks
  are the strongest countervailing force.

## AI, productivity and wages
- FINDING: Brynjolfsson, Li & Raymond (NBER w31161; QJE 2025): 5,179 support agents; +14% issues resolved per hour on average,
  +34% for novice/low-skilled, minimal for experienced.
- FINDING: Humlum & Vestergaard (NBER w33777, rev. Mar 2026), Denmark: precise null effects on earnings and hours; effects > 2%
  ruled out two years after ChatGPT's launch.
- DESCRIPTIVE: Brynjolfsson, Chandar & Chen, "Canaries in the Coal Mine?" (Stanford, rev. Aug 12, 2026; ADP data to June 2026): no
  widespread displacement; ages 22-25 in AI-exposed occupations 19% below the counterfactual path; adjustment via hiring, not base pay;
  "early, descriptive indicators ... rather than causal estimates".
- FINDING (robots): Acemoglu & Restrepo (NBER w23285; JPE 2020), U.S. 1990-2007: +1 robot per 1,000 workers → employment-to-population
  −0.18 to −0.34 pp, wages −0.25% to −0.5%.
- FORECAST: Acemoglu (NBER w32487, 2024): TFP gain ≤ 0.66% over 10 years (possibly < 0.53%); AI predicted to widen the capital-labor
  income gap.

## History
- Allen (2009), "Engels' pause", Explorations in Economic History 46(4): 418-435 (abstract via RePEc): Britain, first half of the
  19th century, real wage stagnated while output per worker expanded; profit rate doubled; profit share rose; after mid-century real
  wages grew in line with productivity and shares stabilized. Engels' book: 1845.
