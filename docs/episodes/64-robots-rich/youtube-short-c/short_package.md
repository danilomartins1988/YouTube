## Narration in the Short

> And this has happened before. Britain, the age of steam. Machines multiplied what every worker could make. Factory owners grew rich. Workers' pay barely moved. Economic historian Robert Allen measured it. Let me draw his story. Output per worker kept climbing. Real wages stayed flat. The profit rate doubled. Historians call it Engels' pause, after Friedrich Engels, who described the factory workers of Manchester. Then, around the middle of the eighteen hundreds, wages finally began to rise with productivity. The pause ended. But it lasted decades. For the workers who lived through it, eventually came too late.
