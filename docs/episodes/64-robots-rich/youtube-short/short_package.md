## Narration in the Short

> So Dana asks the obvious question. Who owns the machines? Mostly, whoever owns the company. In other words, stocks. So let's line up every household in America, from the least wealthy to the most, and share out a hundred dollars' worth of the stock they own. The whole bottom half of the country, all together, gets less than a dollar. And the richest tenth of households? They get almost ninety. Nearly the whole pile. Now connect the dots. If robots move money from paychecks to profits, the money climbs this ladder, to whoever holds the shares. For generations, getting ahead meant getting a better job. When machines do more of the work, owning a piece of them could matter just as much.
