Out of every dollar Americans spend on food, at stores and restaurants combined, only 11.8 cents reaches the farm (USDA Economic Research Service, 2024). And one of America's biggest grocers kept less than two cents of profit per dollar of sales. So where does the rest of your food dollar go, and why did the bill at the checkout jump so much? We follow the dollar from the farm gate to your cart: processing, wholesale, trucks, energy, advertising, stores and restaurants; then eggs and bird flu, beef, and the whole grocery bill; the thin-margin business of supermarkets; the FTC's 2024 report versus the grocers' own numbers; shrinkflation; and the narrow parts of the chain, from meatpacking to the blocked Kroger–Albertsons merger.

Every figure is sourced below: USDA ERS, the Bureau of Labor Statistics, company 10-K filings, the FTC and trade groups (FMI, PLMA), each attributed. The +32% rise in grocery prices since January 2020 and the Kroger profit per dollar are our own calculations. Prices are nominal. Latest CPI month: September 2026. We make no assumption of abnormal profits; the FTC and the industry use different measures, and both are shown. Not affiliated with any retailer or brand.
