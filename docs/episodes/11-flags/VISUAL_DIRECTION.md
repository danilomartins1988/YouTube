# Visual direction: Why Almost Half the World's Ships Fly Three Small Flags

Generated by `tools/visual_direction.py` from the script, then reviewed. The YAML twin (`projects/why-ships-fly-foreign-flags/visual_direction.yaml`) is applied to the render: palette, scene tones, tone shifts, presenter demeanour and the music bed all follow it.

1. **Primary emotional tone:** trade
2. **Secondary tones:** history, reflective
3. **Colour palette:** ocean blues, cargo colours, bright but no faces in the sky
4. **Lighting and contrast:** daylight, long horizons
5. **Animation intensity:** exploratory, steady motion
6. **Character behaviour:** warm: walks through ports, draws routes (demeanour `warm`, at least 4 presenter sequences, only where they explain)
7. **Background design:** seas, ports, canals, cranes
8. **Charts and diagrams:** maps with moving routes, flows
9. **Transitions:** pans along routes
10. **Music and sound:** C-major pad, 5 s chords; the bed changes with each chapter's tone and crossfades at turning points
11. **Key emotional turning points:**
    - chapter "How it started": trade → history
    - chapter "Why owners do it": history → trade
    - chapter "The dark side": trade → reflective
    - chapter "Why it survives": reflective → trade
12. **Scene-specific requirements:**
    - Open sea and ports, daylight, long horizons; the sun has no face
    - How it started: archival sepia for the 1920s-1940s history
    - The dark side: abandoned crews told with restraint (reflective), no playful fish or smiles
    - Presenter warm and exploratory: draws routes and registries
