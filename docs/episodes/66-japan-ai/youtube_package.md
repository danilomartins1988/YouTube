Is Japan's economy collapsing, or about to get rich again? Meet Hiro, a fictional workshop owner in a small Japanese mountain town who makes precise metal parts for machines around the world and can't find a single worker. His country has the oldest population of any big nation, and since its 1995 peak it has lost about 13.5 million working-age people. Yet Japan is also the world's leading robot maker. We follow Hiro, his granddaughter Aoi and the small robot arm they install to answer one question: can AI and robots turn Japan's biggest problem, too few workers, into an advantage?

In this documentary: why Japan's aging population is shrinking its workforce; why the robot superpower sells most of its robots abroad; which robots are really working in Japan (arms, not humanoids); the catches (a weak currency, slow software adoption, debt); and why Japan's economy looks very different per worker than per citizen.
