Is Japan's economy collapsing, or about to get rich again? Meet Hiro, a fictional workshop owner in a small Japanese mountain town who makes precise metal parts for machines around the world and can't find a single worker. His country has the oldest population of any big nation, and since its 1995 peak it has lost about 13.5 million working-age people. Yet Japan is also the world's leading robot maker. We follow Hiro, his granddaughter Aoi and the small robot arm they install to answer one question: can AI and robots turn Japan's biggest problem, too few workers, into an advantage?

In this documentary: why Japan's aging population is shrinking its workforce; why the robot superpower sells most of its robots abroad; which robots are really working in Japan (arms, not humanoids); the catches (a weak currency, slow software adoption, debt); and why Japan's economy looks very different per worker than per citizen.

Key figures (exact values; the film rounds them; JPY converted at the Federal Reserve G.5A 2025 average, 149.57 yen per dollar)
- Working-age population (15-64): 87.26M at the 1995 census peak (IPSS) vs 73.73M on Oct 1, 2024 (Statistics Bureau): -13.5M, our calculation. Tokyo: about 14.3M residents (TMG, Nov 2025).
- IPSS 2023 projection (medium): 45.35M working-age people in 2070, about 52% of the peak (a projection).
- Age 65+: 29.6% of the population (Sept 2026), the highest among countries with 40M+ people.
- Job openings per applicant: 1.19 (Sept 2026, MHLW, seasonally adjusted).
- Trucking: 34% capacity shortfall in FY2030 if nothing is done (MLIT White Paper 2024, estimate); driver overtime cap since April 2024.
- Care workers: about 2.72M needed by FY2040, +570,000 vs FY2022 (MHLW estimate).
- Robots: Japan = 38% of global robot production (IFR, Jul 2025). Robot density 2025 per 10,000 factory workers: Korea 1,066, Singapore 873, Germany 469, Japan 452, US 323, world 152 (IFR, Nov 12, 2026).
- Japan's first home-built industrial robot: Kawasaki, 1969 (Unimation license, 1968).
- FANUC, fiscal year to March 2026: net sales ≈ $5.74B (+7.6%); robot division ≈ $2.53B (+14.9%), sales in Japan down, China up; 87% of sales to customers outside Japan (our calculation).
- Yaskawa, fiscal 2025 (to Feb 2026): robotics revenue ≈ $1.65B (+4.0%), robotics operating profit -14%.
- Telexistence restocking robots: about 1,000 drinks a day each (company claim); Seven-Eleven Japan humanoid "Astra": target 2029 (a plan).
- Nursing homes: robot adoption accompanied by more employment and retention (Lee, Iizuka & Eggleston, NBER w33116).
- Exchange rate: 106.78 yen per dollar (2020) vs 149.57 (2025): the yen lost about 29% of its dollar value (our calculation).
- Output per hour worked 2024: $60.1 (PPP), 28th of 38 OECD countries (Japan Productivity Center).
- Gross public debt about 207% of GDP; net 89-186% depending on assets counted (IMF, Sept 2026).
- Real GDP: +1.2% in 2025, +0.1% in 2024 (Cabinet Office).
- GDP per working-age adult 1998-2019: Japan +31.9% vs US +29.5% (Fernández-Villaverde, Ventura & Yao, NBER w31914).
Hiro, Aoi, Kobo and Hoshimachi are fictional. Industrial robots (deployed) and humanoids (demos and plans) are shown separately.
