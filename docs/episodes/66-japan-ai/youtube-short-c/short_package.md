## Narration in the Short

> Step behind the drink fridge in a Tokyo convenience store, and you may find a robot arm sliding cold bottles onto the shelves. Its maker, a startup called Telexistence, says one arm can restock about a thousand drinks a day. When it fumbles a bottle, a person somewhere else puts on a headset and finishes the job remotely. That's not a demo. That's a deployment. Now compare that with the robots in the headlines. Japan's most famous walking humanoid, Honda's ASIMO, charmed crowds for years and retired without ever getting a real job. Today, Seven-Eleven Japan and Telexistence are developing a humanoid helper, with a target of putting it in stores by twenty twenty-nine. That's a plan, not a product. In Japan, the robots doing the work right now are arms, not androids.
