Costco collected $5.9 billion in membership fees in its 2026 financial year, an amount equal to about half of its $11.7 billion operating profit (Costco 10-K; the share is our calculation). On the goods themselves, it keeps a gross margin of about 11%. So how does a store that sells almost at cost become one of the strongest retailers in America? Four parts of the machine: the thin margin, the membership card (84 million paid members, 92.3% renewal in the U.S. and Canada), the short shopping list (fewer than 4,000 products per warehouse, Kirkland Signature), and selling goods before paying the suppliers ($22.6 billion owed vs $19.3 billion of inventory).
