## 1. The name
- **Philadelphia Inquirer, Nov 25, 2011** ("Did Philly cops coin 'Black Friday'?"): cites Bonnie Taylor-Blake's report to the
  American Dialect Society. A 1961 *Public Relations News* article: "in Philadelphia, it became customary for officers to refer to
  the post-Thanksgiving days as Black Friday and Black Saturday"; stamp dealer Earl Apfelbaum (1966): "It is not a term of
  endearment to them." The term referred to traffic problems.
- **History.com** (published Nov 23, 2015; updated 2026): in the 1950s Philadelphia police used the term for the chaos after
  Thanksgiving; "Not only were Philadelphia police unable to take the day off, but they had to work extra-long shifts dealing with
  the additional crowds and traffic"; merchants "tried unsuccessfully to change it to 'Big Friday'"; the "in the black" story is
  "officially sanctioned—but inaccurate"; "Sometime in the late 1980s, however, retailers found a way to reinvent Black Friday".
- Note: a 1961 Rochester, N.Y. newspaper use is also reported elsewhere (WGBH); we say only what the Inquirer/History.com document.

## 2. Doorbusters, attach sales, derivatives, vendor money
- Loss leader / doorbuster: generic retail economics (no figures claimed for any retailer).
- **Abito & Salant**, "The Effect of Product Misperception on Economic Outcomes: Evidence from the Extended Warranty Market",
  *Review of Economic Studies* 2018 (summary: Kellogg Insight, Oct 3, 2019): warranties averaged 24% of a product's price across
  electronics; TV failure rates 5-8%; "profit margins can be as high as 62 to 73 percent". Academic estimate, earlier years.
- **Consumer Reports, "Get the Most TV for Your Money"** (James K. Willcox, Sep 26, 2018; Nov 2018 issue): "low-priced TVs made
  specifically for Black Friday and often available through a single retailer"; "derivatives" are "similar to the regular
  production models … but with different model numbers and often fewer features", e.g. fewer HDMI inputs, simpler remote; a 2017
  55-inch derivative was "about $170 cheaper than the TV it was based on" and "performed equally well"; single-retailer models
  can't be price-matched. (We do not name the TV maker on screen.)
- **Best Buy 10-K, fiscal 2026** (filed Mar 18, 2026): Seasonality ("A large proportion of our revenue and earnings is generated in
  the fiscal fourth quarter, which includes the majority of the holiday shopping season"); inventory management includes
  "agreements with vendors relating to reimbursement for the cost of markdowns or sales incentives"; vendor allowances (volume,
  advertising, placement) reduce cost of sales; Services include advertising (Best Buy Ads), warranty-related services.
- **Target 2025 10-K** (filed Mar 11, 2026): "A larger share of annual net sales are traditionally earned during the fourth
  quarter because it includes the November and December holiday sales period"; vendor income "principally earned as a result of
  volume rebates, promotions, certain advertising activities, and markdown allowances"; "employment levels peak in the holiday
  season"; Roundel retail media network. (Also: $593M gain from interchange-fee litigation settlements, not used.)

## 4. Online vs stores
- **Adobe, Jan 7, 2026** (holiday 2025 final): season $257.8bn (+6.8%); Cyber Week $44.2bn (+7.7%); Cyber Monday $14.25bn
  (+7.1%); Black Friday $11.8bn (+9.1%); Thanksgiving $6.4bn (+5.3%); mobile 56.4% of transactions; peak discounts off listed
  price: electronics 30.9%, toys 29.6%, apparel 25.1%, TVs 24.3%, computers 23.4%; BNPL $20bn (+9.8%), Cyber Monday BNPL $1.03bn;
  affiliates & partners 20.4% revenue share (incl. influencers), social 4.6%; returns down 1.2% (not used).
- **Adobe, Dec 2, 2025**: Cyber Monday details; methodology (over 1 trillion visits, 100M SKUs, 18 categories).
- **Adobe forecast, Sep 28, 2026**: 2026 season $275.1bn; Cyber Week $47.5bn; Black Friday $12.9bn (+9.2%); Cyber Monday $15.1bn;
  BNPL $21.3bn. Forecast only.
- **NRF, Dec 2, 2025** (Prosper survey of 3,099 adults, Nov 26-30, ±1.8 pts): record 202.9M shoppers over the five days; 129.5M
  in stores, 134.9M online; Black Friday 80.3M in-store and 85.7M online.
- **U.S. Census Bureau, Q4 2025 e-commerce** (released Mar 10, 2026 after the funding lapse delay): not adjusted $365.2bn = 18.3%
  of total retail sales (adjusted 16.6%); 2025 e-commerce 16.4% of retail.

## 5. Who else gets paid
- **Federal Reserve**, 2023 Interchange Fee Revenue … report (December 2025): debit and general-use prepaid interchange $34.12bn;
  100.7bn transactions, $4.7tn; covered transactions average fee $0.24 (single-message) and $0.22 (dual-message); exempt $0.52;
  network fees $12.95bn ($0.129 per transaction). Regulation II covers debit only (credit is outside the cap).
- **CFPB, Sep 2022** BNPL market report, Table 10 (five lenders): merchant discount fees 3.39% (2019), 2.91% (2020), 2.49% (2021)
  of GMV; average loan $135 (2021); lenders market "incremental sales" and higher average order value to merchants.
- **Amazon 10-K 2025**: advertising services $68,635M (2024 $56,214M).

## 6. Returns and whether deals are real
- **NRF and Happy Returns, Oct 15, 2025**: retailers expect 17% of holiday sales returned; 2025 returns $849.9bn = 15.8% of sales
  (2024: 16.9%, $890bn); online 19.3%; 9% of returns fraudulent. Surveys: 358 e-commerce professionals (merchants > $500M revenue),
  2,006 consumers.
- **Which? (UK), Nov 26, 2024**: deals on 227 products in the 2023 Black Friday fortnight (Nov 20 - Dec 1) at eight UK retailers;
  92% the same price or cheaper at other times of the year. UK evidence only; no comparable U.S. study of the same rigour found.
