How does Black Friday actually make money? At 5 a.m. the doors open and the store starts losing money on purpose. Yet last Black Friday, Americans spent a record $11.8 billion online in one day (Adobe). So how does a day built on discounts make money? We follow it like an episode of an animated sitcom: why Philadelphia police named it in 1961 (and why "in the black" came later), the doorbuster as a loss leader, the cart and the protection plan around it, TVs built for the day, the supplier money that pays for markdowns, the holiday quarter that brings Best Buy about a third of its revenue, the shift from doors to phones, the tolls taken by card networks, buy-now-pay-later lenders and ad sellers, and the January hangover of returns. Plus: are the deals real?
