Why does a promise of love come with a small, clear stone? And who decided that it should? The answer starts with one company, De Beers. For most of the last century, it controlled seventy-five to eighty percent of the world's rough diamonds. And in nineteen forty-seven, its advertising agency came up with four words that changed weddings forever. A diamond is forever. Today, the company that made diamonds priceless is being sold off, at a price that would have seemed impossible not long ago. How do you keep something valuable when the ground is full of it?

## 7. Payoff (as rendered)
So what is the lesson of De Beers? That a price is not only about how rare something is. It is about who controls the supply, and what story people believe. De Beers mastered both for a century. It held the stones back, and it gave them a meaning. The slogan still works. But a story can only hold a price up for as long as people stop asking what the thing would cost without it.
