Americans were charged $160 billion in credit card interest in 2024, up from $105 billion in 2022 (CFPB). And every time you tap a card, the store pays a fee too: typically 1-3% of the purchase, with the biggest slice going to the bank that issued your card, at a rate set by the card network (Congressional Research Service). The three ways the card business makes money, the swipe toll, interest and late fees, and who really pays for your cash back and travel points. Every figure is sourced below: CFPB, Federal Reserve G.19 and SHED, the New York Fed, the Congressional Research Service, and the 10-K filings of Visa, Mastercard and American Express.

All amounts are in US dollars. Swipe-fee totals are estimates cited by CRS; Visa's operating margin is our calculation from its 10-K.
