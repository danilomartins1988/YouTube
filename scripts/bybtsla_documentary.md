## m3_breaks - What's priced in

Our usual reverse math breaks here, and we want to show you why. To justify the price from today's free cash flow, that cash would need to grow about forty-five percent a year with an eight percent discount rate, or about fifty-two percent at ten, every year for a decade. At a yield this small, tiny changes swing the answer, so it isn't a useful number.

> `rdcf` [source_located] OUR illustration (not a valuation), shown to make a point: market cap $1,501bn = 10 years of free cash flow growing at g plus a terminal value at 3%, discounted at r. On TTM FCF $3.30bn: g about 44.7% a year (8%) or 51.8% (10%), year-10 FCF about $133bn-$214bn. At a 0.2% FCF yield the result swings with tiny input changes, so the method breaks. Steady-state lens: for the price to work as a business growing 3% a year from now, free cash flow would need to be about $73bn (8%) to $102bn (10%) a year: 22 to 31 times today's $3.3bn, or about 70% to 98% of today's entire revenue. (sources: deck, px)

It warns that if he pledged shares for loans, he might be forced to sell. And it says the pay package could pull focus toward specific technologies.

(sources: tenq)

Operating expenses last quarter were about four point eight billion dollars, up from about three point four billion dollars a year earlier, which Tesla puts down to its super intelligence and other research projects, and to stock pay, including the C E O's award. ## m5_legal - Risks and upside

Risk three is the courts and regulators. In one Autopilot case, a jury awarded a hundred and twenty-nine million dollars in damages, plus two hundred million dollars in punitive damages, and Tesla is appealing. Regulators are asking about driver assistance and Robotaxi. No agency has found wrongdoing, and no amounts are disclosed.

> `legal` [source_located] 10-Q: Benavides v. Tesla (Autopilot fatality): 'The jury awarded $129 million in total compensatory damages, finding the driver 67% at fault and the Company 33% at fault. (sources: tenq)
