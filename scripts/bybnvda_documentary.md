## m3_gauge - What's priced in

Now the reverse calculation. We ask, how fast does free cash flow have to grow for ten years, and then three percent a year forever, to justify today's price? With an eight percent discount rate, the answer is about five and a half percent a year. With ten percent, about ten percent a year. That's our illustration, not a valuation.

> `rdcf` [source_located] OUR illustration (not a valuation): market cap $3,747.5bn = 10 years of FCF growing at g from TTM FCF $148.6bn, plus a terminal value at 3% growth, discounted at r. Solved: r = 8% gives g ≈ 5.6% a year (year-10 FCF ≈ $255bn); r = 10% gives g ≈ 10.3% (≈ $395bn). With zero growth the same model gives ≈ $2.41tn (8%) or ≈ $1.76tn (10%), so ≈ 36% to ≈ 53% of the price is a bet on growth. Net cash ignored (≈ 0.6% of the price). (sources: pr, q4, px)
