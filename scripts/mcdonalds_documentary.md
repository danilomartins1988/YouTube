# The Hamburger That Became a Symbol of the Cold War's End

_On a freezing morning in January nineteen ninety, people in Moscow began lining up before dawn to buy a hamburger. The first McDonald's in the Soviet Union served thirty thousand customers that day. How a fast food chain spent fourteen years getting in, built its own farms and factory to stay, and how, thirty-two years later, it left._

## h_stakes - A line in the snow

On January thirty-first, nineteen ninety, people began lining up near Pushkin Square in Moscow as early as four in the morning. ## m1_start - Fourteen years

Very slowly. The idea started in the nineteen seventies, with the Canadian arm of McDonald's. Beginning in nineteen seventy-six, one of its executives made the first of dozens of trips to Moscow, to lobby the officials of the Communist Party. In the Soviet Union, the state owned the shops, the farms and the factories. A foreign company could not just rent a building and start selling.

> `deal` [source_located] CBC: 'the culmination of 14 years of work and an investment of $50 million.' Ironside: 'Beginning in 1976, he made the first of dozens of trips to Moscow to lobby Party officials'; the restaurant was 'a joint venture' between McDonald's Canada and the Moscow city soviet; 'the agreement was signed on April 29, 1987'; the city held 'the 51% controlling share'; foreign partners were 'limited to a 49% share'. (sources: cbc, ironside)  

## m1_jv - Fourteen years

The door opened only when the rules changed. In the late nineteen eighties, under perestroika, the Soviet Union began to allow joint ventures with foreign companies. On April twenty-ninth, nineteen eighty-seven, McDonald's Canada signed one with the Moscow city government. But there was a catch. The city kept the controlling share, fifty-one percent. The foreign partner was limited to forty-nine.

## m2_plant - Building a food chain

In nineteen eighty-nine, work began on a giant food plant in Solntsevo, on the edge of Moscow. It covered about one hundred thousand square feet. According to CBC, it was built to turn out fourteen thousand buns an hour, and ten thousand patties an hour. The building opened in nineteen ninety, just in time for the restaurant.

## m4_control - Thirty years

In the new Russia, McDonald's slowly took control. In February nineteen ninety-six, it bought most of the city's shares in the joint venture, raising its own stake from forty-nine percent to eighty percent. The company that had once needed permission to open a single restaurant now ran the business.

> `control` [source_located] Ironside: 'In February 1996, McDonalds bought 31% of the Moscow city government's shares in the enterprise, bringing its share up to 80%.' AP (8 Mar 2022): 'McDonald's owns 84 percent of its Russian locations.' (sources: ironside, ap_mar22)  

## m4_growth - Thirty years

And it grew. By twenty twenty-two, McDonald's had more than eight hundred and fifty restaurants in Russia. It owned about eighty-four percent of them, and ran most of them itself, rather than through franchise owners. They employed sixty-two thousand people. Together with its restaurants in Ukraine, the business brought in about nine percent of the company's annual revenue, around two billion dollars. For a brand built on burgers and fries, Russia had become one of its big markets.

> `size` [source_located] McDonald's 10-Q (Q2 2022): 'Reflects the sale of over 850 restaurants in Russia in the second quarter of 2022, most of which were Company-operated.' McDonald's (16 May 2022), CEO Chris Kempczinski: 'the 62,000 employees who work in our restaurants'. AP (8 Mar 2022): restaurants in Russia and Ukraine 'contributed 9% of its annual revenue, or around $2 billion'. (sources: q2_10q, mcd_exit, ap_mar22)

## m5_close - Leaving

On March eighth, twenty twenty-two, McDonald's announced that it was temporarily closing its restaurants in Russia. Two months later, on May sixteenth, it went further. After more than thirty years in the country, it said, it would leave Russia for good, and it expected to book a charge of about one point two to one point four billion dollars. Its chief executive wrote that the company could no longer keep the Arches shining there.

> `exit` [source_located] McDonald's (16 May 2022): 'This follows McDonald's announcement on March 8, 2022, that it had temporarily closed restaurants in Russia'; 'After more than 30 years of operations in the country, McDonald's Corporation announced it will exit'; Kempczinski: 'we can no longer keep the Arches shining there'; expected charge 'of approximately $1.2-1.4 billion to write off its net investment in the market'. (sources: mcd_exit)  

## m5_sale - Leaving

Three days later, McDonald's agreed to sell the whole business to Alexander Govor, a local businessman who had run twenty-five restaurants in Siberia as a McDonald's licensee. The deal kept the employees in their jobs for at least two years. The price was never made public.

## m5_reopen - Leaving

And then came the strangest part. On June twelfth, twenty twenty-two, the restaurants began to reopen under their new owner, starting with the very same building on Pushkin Square. Same address, and under the deal, the same staff. Only the name had changed. It was announced just a couple of hours before the doors opened. Vkusno and tochka. Roughly, tasty, and that's it.

## payoff - The lesson

So what does one hamburger restaurant teach us about the world economy? That a business is never only a business. In nineteen ninety, a burger in Moscow meant the walls between East and West were coming down. It took fourteen years of politics to open the door, and one war to close it. The kitchens are still there, and so are the customers. What left was the name, and the idea that trade alone would keep the world together.
