# Why Elon Musk Wants His Own Chip Factory

_The companies that design some of the most demanding chips in the world have never made one. Now Tesla and SpaceX plan to build the largest chip factory on Earth. Why owning the factory suddenly looks worth a hundred billion dollars, and what the fine print says._

## h_question - The biggest building on Earth

Why would two companies that have never made a single chip plan to build the largest building on Earth, just to make chips?

## h_stakes - The biggest building on Earth

The building is called Terafab. Tesla and SpaceX say its first phase alone will cost sixteen point eight billion dollars. A county filing says the whole project may cost up to one hundred and nineteen billion dollars. And the man behind it, Elon Musk, gives a simple reason. We either build it, he says, or we don't have the chips.

(sources: tc_119, tx_gov, tc_168)  
> `launch` [source_located] Tom's Hardware (22 Mar 2026): Musk launched TeraFab on 21 March 2026 at the defunct Seaholm Power Plant in Austin: 'We either build the TeraFab, or we don't have the chips, and we need the chips, so we build the TeraFab'; the industry's expansion rate is 'much less than we'd like'; he put global AI compute output at roughly 20 GW a year, about 2% of his companies' eventual needs; target of a terawatt of compute a year (100-200 GW on Earth, the rest in space); Tesla, SpaceX and xAI will keep buying from TSMC, Samsung and Micron, which he would like 'to expand as quickly as they can'; he gave no timeline for when TeraFab would begin producing chips. ## m1_design - The bottleneck

Tesla already designs its own chips. They are the brains of its cars and of its humanoid robot, Optimus. But Tesla does not make them. Samsung makes the current one. TSMC in Taiwan will make the next one, first in Taiwan and then in Arizona. And in twenty twenty-five, Samsung signed a deal worth sixteen and a half billion dollars to make the one after that at its new factory in Taylor, Texas, running until the end of twenty thirty-three.

> `suppliers` [source_located] Tom's Hardware (Jul 2025): Samsung's $16.5bn deal runs through 31 Dec 2033 to make Tesla's AI6 at its Taylor, Texas fab; Musk: 'Samsung currently makes AI4. TSMC will make AI5… initially in Taiwan and then Arizona.' (sources: thw_samsung)  

## m1_rate - The bottleneck

So why build a factory at all? Because, Musk says, his suppliers cannot grow fast enough. At the launch event in Austin in March twenty twenty-six, he said the rate at which they are comfortable expanding is much less than he would like. By his own estimate, everything the world produces in AI computing today is only about two percent of what his companies will eventually need. His target is one terawatt of computing power every year.

> `launch` [source_located] Tom's Hardware (22 Mar 2026): Musk launched TeraFab on 21 March 2026 at the defunct Seaholm Power Plant in Austin: 'We either build the TeraFab, or we don't have the chips, and we need the chips, so we build the TeraFab'; the industry's expansion rate is 'much less than we'd like'; he put global AI compute output at roughly 20 GW a year, about 2% of his companies' eventual needs; target of a terawatt of compute a year (100-200 GW on Earth, the rest in space); Tesla, SpaceX and xAI will keep buying from TSMC, Samsung and Micron, which he would like 'to expand as quickly as they can'; he gave no timeline for when TeraFab would begin producing chips. (sources: thw_launch, tc_119)  

## m1_draw - The bottleneck

Let me draw the problem. A chip designer is like a writer with a great book and no printing press. The printers are very few, and every big customer is in the same queue: phone makers, AI companies, carmakers. When the queue gets long, the printer decides who goes first. Musk's answer is to buy his own printing press.

## m2_roof - Everything under one roof

Normally, a chip travels the world. It is designed in one place, printed in a factory in another, and packaged and tested somewhere else, often in another country. Terafab is meant to do all of it under one roof. Design, printing, memory, packaging and testing. Musk says that way his engineers can make a chip, test it, fix the design and print it again, without shipping wafers between sites.

> `wafers` [source_located] Electrek (22 Mar 2026): Tesla said it is targeting 2 nm process technology; Terafab is designed for an initial output of 100,000 wafer starts per month, with ambitions to scale to 1 million wafer starts per month at full capacity; it would consolidate design, lithography, fabrication, memory, advanced packaging and testing; Tesla's CFO said the full cost (est. $20-25bn) is not yet in Tesla's 2026 capex plan, which already exceeds $20bn. (sources: electrek_launch)

(sources: thw_launch, tc_119)  

## m2_scale - Everything under one roof

The targets are enormous. Tesla said it is aiming for the most advanced kind of chip, with two nanometer technology. The plan starts at one hundred thousand wafers a month, and the ambition is to reach one million wafers a month. For comparison, TSMC, the biggest chipmaker in the world, had a capacity of more than seventeen million wafers in twenty twenty-five. That is about one point four million a month. So the full target would be about seventy percent of everything TSMC can make, in one place. These are targets, not output.

> `wafers` [source_located] Electrek (22 Mar 2026): Tesla said it is targeting 2 nm process technology; Terafab is designed for an initial output of 100,000 wafer starts per month, with ambitions to scale to 1 million wafer starts per month at full capacity; it would consolidate design, lithography, fabrication, memory, advanced packaging and testing; Tesla's CFO said the full cost (est. $20-25bn) is not yet in Tesla's 2026 capex plan, which already exceeds $20bn. (sources: electrek_launch)  
> `tsmc_cap` [source_located] TSMC: annual capacity of the facilities managed by TSMC and its subsidiaries exceeded 17 million 12-inch equivalent wafers in 2025. Our calculation: ≈ 1.4 million a month, so 1 million wafer starts a month would be about 70% of TSMC's 2025 capacity. (sources: tsmc_capacity)  

## m2_two - Everything under one roof

Terafab would make two kinds of chips. Small, efficient ones that let cars drive themselves and robots move, and powerful ones for something that does not exist yet, data centers in orbit. Musk wants most of the computing power in space, where, he argues, the sun shines about five times as strongly as on the ground, and cooling in a vacuum can work. So the factory on Earth is meant to feed computers above it.

> `two_chips` [source_located] SpaceX (via TechCrunch, Aug 2026): Terafab 'will produce chips optimized for edge computing and inference for use in hardware like Tesla's Optimus robots and self-driving Cybercabs, along with high-power chips designed for operating SpaceX's space-based data centers'; it will house 'manufacturing, packaging, and testing of advanced logic and memory devices'. Electrek: Musk argued solar irradiance in space is roughly 5x greater than at Earth's surface, and that heat rejection in vacuum makes thermal scaling viable. Tom's Hardware: 100-200 GW a year of terrestrial chips, 'on the order of a terawatt of chips in space'. (sources: tc_168, electrek_launch, thw_launch)  
> `launch` [source_located] Tom's Hardware (22 Mar 2026): Musk launched TeraFab on 21 March 2026 at the defunct Seaholm Power Plant in Austin: 'We either build the TeraFab, or we don't have the chips, and we need the chips, so we build the TeraFab'; the industry's expansion rate is 'much less than we'd like'; he put global AI compute output at roughly 20 GW a year, about 2% of his companies' eventual needs; target of a terawatt of compute a year (100-200 GW on Earth, the rest in space); Tesla, SpaceX and xAI will keep buying from TSMC, Samsung and Micron, which he would like 'to expand as quickly as they can'; he gave no timeline for when TeraFab would begin producing chips. (sources: thw_launch, tc_119)  

## m2_small - Everything under one roof

The real start is much smaller. On Tesla's earnings call in April twenty twenty-six, Musk said Tesla would first build a research fab at its Texas car plant, costing about three billion dollars, and capable of maybe a few thousand wafers a month. It is meant to try out ideas. He also said Terafab plans to use a manufacturing process from Intel that, in his words, is not yet totally complete.

> `research_fab` [source_located] Tesla Q1 2026 call (22 Apr 2026), Musk: Tesla will build the research fab on its Giga Texas campus, 'probably a $3 billion-ish initiative, and capable of maybe a few thousand wafers per month. It's really intended to try out ideas'; 'We plan to use Intel's 14A process, which is state-of-the-art and in fact, not yet totally complete.' (sources: tesla_q1)

## m3_size - The price tag

The building itself would be gigantic. SpaceX plans more than one hundred million square feet of manufacturing space. The largest building in the world today, a car factory in Russia, has about sixty-five million. Musk called it the largest and most valuable building on Earth by far. The state of Texas says the first phase will create three thousand jobs, and gave SpaceX a grant of thirty million dollars.

## m3_water - The price tag

A chip factory also needs water, and a factory this size needs a lot of it. SpaceX says it will take its water from the local Gibbons Creek Reservoir, instead of pumping it from under the ground, and that most of the jobs will go to people from Grimes and the neighbouring county.

> `water` [source_located] TechCrunch (6 Aug 2026): SpaceX said it is 'committing to the use of' water from the local Gibbons Creek Reservoir instead of local groundwater; the companies say the facility will employ at least 3,000 people from Grimes and nearby Brazos County. (sources: tc_168)  

## m3_county - The price tag

The county paid its share too. In June twenty twenty-six, Grimes County commissioners voted four to one for a one hundred percent exemption from county property taxes on the new buildings. One commissioner said the project would cover three to four percent of the whole county. Hundreds of residents came to a meeting to complain about the tax breaks and a lack of transparency.

> `tax` [source_located] KBTX (3 Jun 2026): Grimes County commissioners voted 4-1 to approve a reinvestment zone and a 100% tax abatement for the Terafab project; a commissioner said the footprint would cover roughly 3% to 4% of the county. ## m4_s1 - The fine print

In May twenty twenty-six, SpaceX filed to sell its shares to the public. A company that sells shares must tell investors the risks. And the filing was careful. It said Terafab is in the very early stages. It said SpaceX and Tesla have agreed only a general framework. The money, the timelines and who owns the technology still have to be negotiated. And it said that neither Tesla nor Intel is obligated to remain part of the project.

> `s1` [source_located] SpaceX S-1 as quoted by Electrek (20 May 2026): Terafab and Macrohard 'are in the very early stages'; SpaceX and Tesla agreed 'a general framework for the future development of Terafab'; specific projects 'will be subject to separate negotiations and agreements'; 'neither Tesla nor Intel are obligated to remain a part of the project'. (sources: electrek_s1)  

## m4_tangle - The fine print

The same filing shows how tangled Musk's companies already are. In twenty twenty-five, SpaceX bought one hundred and forty-four million dollars of goods and services from Tesla, up from just four million the year before. The AI company xAI, now part of SpaceX, bought another five hundred and six million dollars from Tesla, mostly giant batteries for its data centers. Terafab would tie the companies together even more tightly.

> `tangle` [source_located] Electrek on the SpaceX S-1 (20 May 2026): in 2025 SpaceX obtained $144 million in goods and services from Tesla, up from $4 million in 2024; xAI purchased $506 million from Tesla in 2025, primarily Megapack batteries for its data centers; Tesla holds less than 1% of SpaceX, converted from its $2 billion xAI investment. (sources: electrek_s1)  

## m4_doubts - The fine print

Critics go further. The electric car news site Electrek pointed out that Tesla has no experience making chips, and compared the event to Tesla's battery day in twenty twenty, when big promises about a new battery took years longer than planned. And Musk said eighty percent of Terafab's computing power would go to AI satellites in space, an idea that still has to prove it works.

> `doubts` [source_located] Electrek (opinion, 22 Mar 2026): Tesla has zero semiconductor manufacturing experience; compares the event to Battery Day 2020 (4680 cell targets missed); Musk said 80% of Terafab's compute would go to orbital AI satellites. (sources: electrek_launch)  

## m4_draw - The fine print

Let me draw why chipmaking punishes newcomers. A factory must be built before it earns a single dollar. Then it must learn. The first wafers come out with many broken chips. Every month, the share of good chips rises, and only then does the money start to come back. The companies at the top have climbed that curve for decades. A newcomer starts at the bottom.

## m5_why - Owning the factory

For years, the smart move in chips was the opposite. Design the chip yourself and let a specialist make it. It saved billions. But that only works while the specialist has room for you. When every AI company is fighting for the same factories, being a customer means waiting in line. Owning the factory means deciding the line. That is the bet behind Terafab. Not that Tesla can make chips more cheaply than TSMC, but that it cannot afford to wait.

## payoff - The lesson

So why does Elon Musk want his own chip factory? Not because making chips is a good business for a newcomer. It is one of the hardest businesses on Earth. He wants it because in the age of AI, the scarcest thing is not money or ideas. It is a place in the factory queue. Terafab is a bet of up to one hundred and nineteen billion dollars that it is cheaper to build the queue than to wait in it. Whether it pays off, the fine print says, is still to be negotiated.
