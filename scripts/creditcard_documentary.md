## m1_split - The two-second swipe

According to the Congressional Research Service, the total fee a store pays is typically between one and three percent of the purchase. If it is three percent, the store receives ninety-seven dollars, not one hundred. The biggest slice, called the interchange fee, goes to the bank that issued your card. And here is the odd part. That fee is set not by your bank, and not by the store, but by the card network.

## m3_balances - The interest machine

Americans owe a lot on their cards. According to the New York Fed, credit card balances stood at one point two six trillion dollars in the middle of twenty twenty-six. And more than eight in ten American adults have a credit card.

## m3_apr - The interest machine

The price of that debt has jumped. The Federal Reserve tracks the average interest rate on card accounts that are actually paying interest. In twenty twenty-one, it averaged sixteen point four five percent. Its latest reading is twenty-two point three six percent.

> `apr` [source_located] Fed G.19: average interest rate on commercial bank credit card plans, accounts assessed interest, 22.36% (Q3 2026, preliminary); annual averages 16.45% (2021) and 22.89% (2024); 'the annualized ratio of total finance charges ... to the total average daily balances against which the finance charges were assessed.' (sources: g19)  

## m3_margin - The interest machine

Part of that rise followed the Federal Reserve's own rate increases. But not all of it. The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau looked at the margin, the gap between card rates and the basic prime rate. It found the margin had grown by four point three percentage points in a decade, to the highest level in recent history. By its estimate, the wider margin alone earned the big card companies about twenty-five billion dollars in extra interest in a single year.

> `margin` [source_located] CFPB (Feb 2024): APR margin, 'the difference between the average APR and the prime rate', 'is now at 14.3 percent, the highest point in recent history'; 'The average APR margin increased 4.3 percentage points from 2013 to 2023'; 'Major credit card companies earned an estimated $25 billion in additional interest revenue by raising APR margin.' (sources: cfpb_margin)  

## m3_bill - The interest machine

Put it together and the interest bill exploded. In twenty twenty-two, Americans were charged one hundred and five billion dollars in credit card interest. Two years later, it was one hundred and sixty billion dollars. That is more than fifty billion dollars extra in just two years.

> `interest` [source_located] CFPB (Dec 2025): 'In 2024, consumers were assessed $160 billion in interest charges, up from $105 billion in 2022.' (sources: cfpb_2025)

## payoff - The lesson

So what is the hidden business behind your credit card? It is not the plastic. It is the position. The card sits between you and every store, and every company in that chain takes a slice. If you pay in full, the system can work in your favor. If you carry a balance, you are the one paying for it. Either way, the card is never really free.
