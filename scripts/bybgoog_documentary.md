## m3_proxy - What's priced in

So for a company in a building spree, we use a proxy. We take operating cash and subtract depreciation, about about twenty-eight billion dollars a year, as a rough stand-in for the spending needed just to keep things running. That leaves about one hundred and fifty-two billion dollars, a yield of about four point three percent. It's a proxy, and it assumes capex eventually falls back toward that level.

> `proxy` [source_located] OUR proxy base for a capex boom (labelled proxy): operating cash flow $179.6bn minus depreciation of property and equipment of about $28.0bn TTM (SEC XBRL: FY2025 $21.1bn minus 9M 2025 $15.1bn plus 9M 2026 $22.0bn) = about $151.6bn (yield about 4.3%). It assumes capex eventually falls back toward the depreciation level. (sources: tenq, px)  

## m3_gauge - What's priced in

Now let's run it backwards. What growth in that cash, for ten years, makes today's price work, before slowing to three percent? With an eight percent discount rate, about four and a half percent a year. With ten percent, about nine percent. But run the same math on today's actual free cash flow, and the answer jumps to between about thirty-one percent and about thirty-eight percent a year.

> `rdcf` [source_located] OUR illustration (not a valuation): market cap $3,519bn = 10 years of cash growing at g plus a terminal value at 3%, discounted at r. ## m5_capex - Risks and upside

Risk one is the build-out itself. Alphabet's own filing says its investments may not result in an adequate return. It has about eight hundred billion dollars of commitments, mostly long-term supply deals, plus guarantees and repurchase promises on some hardware. If demand falls short, it could be left with capacity it can't easily use.

> `risks` [source_located] 10-Q risk factors: 'Our investments ultimately may not be commercially viable or may not result in an adequate return of capital'; large long-duration agreements 'including backstops and financial guarantees ... and contingent repurchase obligations ... we may incur additional liabilities, have excess capacity or inventory that we cannot easily redeploy'. Total commitments $802.1bn, 'mostly long-term supply agreements'; leases not yet commenced $130.5bn. New risk factor on 'the dilutive impact of current or future equity issuances'. 10-K: Class B stock 'represented approximately 52.7% of the voting power'. (sources: tenq, rel)  

## m5_legal - Risks and upside

Risk two is the courts. The search judgment restricts how Google distributes its services. In ad tech, the judge rejected a breakup but ordered other remedies, and Google plans to appeal. In Europe, Alphabet paid five point two billion dollars for the Android fine this summer. And it has about fourteen billion dollars set aside for fines and settlements.

> `legal` [source_located] 10-Q Note 10: DOJ search final judgment (Dec 2025) 'imposes restrictions on how Google distributes its services and requires Google to share certain search data with and offer syndication services to certain competitors' (both sides appealed). DOJ ad tech: the court denied 'the DOJ's request for structural divestitures ... instead ordering certain behavioral remedies' (Google plans to appeal). EC Android: 'we made a cash payment of $5.2 billion for the fine plus accrued interest' (July 2026). Accrued legal and regulatory fines and settlements $13.8bn at Sep 30, 2026. (sources: tenq)  

## m5_fines - Risks and upside

And the bills from Europe keep coming. The European ad tech fine was booked as a three and a half billion dollars charge, and the two digital markets decisions added another one billion dollars last quarter, all under appeal. A Swedish price comparison site won about about two point one billion dollars in damages, also under appeal. A Texas trial on ad tech is still ahead, and for that one Alphabet says it can't estimate a possible loss.

> `fines` [source_located] 10-Q Note 10: European Commission ad tech fine recorded as a $3.5bn charge, under appeal; EU DMA decisions (Play and Search) recorded as a $1.0bn charge in Q3 2026, under appeal; PriceRunner (Stockholm) 'approximately $2.1 billion … in principal damages plus accrued interest', under appeal; DOJ ad tech: a Texas state AG trial follows; 'We cannot estimate a possible loss.'
