# The Countries That Could Win the AI Economy Without Building AI

_Hattie, a fictional gold-country hardware-store owner, opens a package and finds Nugget, a talking AI chip with a passport full of stamps. Following the stamps from Taiwan to the Netherlands, South Korea, Japan, the Gulf, Chile and Australia, she meets the nations selling the shovels of the AI gold rush, and learns the old lesson of 1849, that shovel sellers can ride a rush down as well as up, and that the shovel that lasts is the one nobody else can make._

## co_hook - Sell the shovels

California, eighteen forty-nine. Gold! Crowds of dreamers rush into the hills with their pans. Most of them find mud. But down in town, one storekeeper is having the best year of her life. She doesn't dig. She sells the shovels. Today there's a new gold rush, and it's called AI. Everyone is watching the companies that build the chatbots. But some countries are getting rich the old way, by selling the shovels. So which countries could win the AI economy without building AI at all?

## c1_draw - Sell the shovels

Let me draw why the shovel sellers matter. At the top, someone builds a chatbot and sells answers. To do that, they rent a data center. The data center buys chips. ## c4_chile - Money and metal

Sixth stamp, the mountains of Chile. Giant trucks haul copper ore up out of the pit. Copper is the wire inside every data center, every power line and every chip package. And Chile mines almost one in every four tons of copper on Earth.

## c4_aus - Money and metal

Across the ocean, Australia digs up about a third of the world's lithium, the metal in the batteries that back up power grids, phones and electric cars.

> `aus_li` [data_derived] USGS MCS 2026: Australia 92,000 t of lithium of 290,000 t world in 2025 (est.) = 32% (OUR CALC). (sources: usgs_li26)

## c4_gap - Money and metal

Here's the twist. The International Energy Agency forecasts that the copper mines already planned could fall about a third short of demand by the middle of the next decade. A shortage sounds like a jackpot for Chile.

> `iea_cu` [source_located] IEA GCMO 2025, STEPS: implied copper supply deficit of 30% vs demand in 2035 from existing and announced mines; GCMO 2026 coverage says the copper supply outlook worsened considerably. FORECAST. (sources: iea_gcmo25, iea_gcmo26)  
> IEA Global Critical Minerals Outlook 2025 (STEPS implied copper gap ~30% by 2035); the 2026 edition says the outlook worsened. A FORECAST.  

## c4_price - Money and metal

But a mine can't charge whatever it likes. Copper is sold at one world price. When the rush runs hot, every mine wins. When it cools, the price falls for everyone at once. Nugget gulps. So metal is a shovel anyone can sell.
