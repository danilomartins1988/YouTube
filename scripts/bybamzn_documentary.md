## m3_gauge - What's priced in

Now let's run it backwards. What growth in that cash, for ten years, makes today's price work, before slowing to three percent? With an eight percent discount rate, about nine percent a year. With ten percent, about fourteen percent. On actual free cash flow, the math doesn't even start, because it's negative.

> `rdcf` [source_located] OUR illustration (not a valuation): market cap $2,929bn = 10 years of cash growing at g plus a terminal value at 3%, discounted at r. On the PROXY base $88.9bn: r = 8% gives g about 8.9% a year; r = 10% gives g about 13.9%. On TTM free cash flow (-$42.4bn) no growth rate can be solved. Steady-state lens: for the price to work as a business growing 3% a year from now, free cash flow would need to be about $142bn (8%) to $199bn (10%) a year. (sources: tenq, px)

## m5_chips - Risks and upside

And then there's supply. Amazon says it relies on a limited group of suppliers for chips, including the graphics processors behind A I, and its own guidance flags supply swings in memory chips. A patent case at the International Trade Commission is seeking to block some A W S servers. And Amazon carries about about seven point three billion dollars of uncertain tax positions.
