# Who Actually Owns America's $40 Trillion in Debt?

_The U.S. owes more than forty trillion dollars, but China holds only about one and a half percent of it. Most of the IOU is held by Americans, their funds, banks and pensions, by the Federal Reserve, and by the government's own trust funds, while the interest bill now outruns the defense budget._

## a1_split - The IOU

First, what is this debt? It's every Treasury security the government has issued and not yet paid back. It comes in two pieces. Thirty-two point eight trillion dollars is debt held by the public. Bills, notes and bonds that investors bought. The other seven point eight trillion dollars is debt the government owes to itself, to its own trust funds.

> `dtp` [source_located] Debt to the Penny, Nov 13, 2026: total public debt outstanding $40,616,678,827,092.07; debt held by the public $32,825,322,014,326.67; intragovernmental holdings $7,791,356,812,765.40. (sources: dtp)  

## a1_draw - The IOU

The smaller box is intragovernmental. Social Security's trust funds hold about a third of it, by our calculation. Hold on to that box. We'll open it later.

## a1_auction - The IOU

So where does new debt come from? An auction. In fiscal year twenty twenty-six, the Treasury held four hundred and thirty-six auctions, and sold nearly thirty-three trillion dollars of securities, by our count. Most of that was short-term bills, paid off and sold again, over and over.

> `auctions` [source_located] Treasury auction results dated Oct 1, 2025 - Sep 30, 2026: 436 auctions (328 bills, 82 notes, 26 bonds); total accepted about $32.9 trillion (OUR SUM; includes Federal Reserve rollover add-ons; mostly short-term bills that roll over). (sources: auct)  

## a1_bidders - The IOU

Take one sale. On August twelfth, the Treasury auctioned forty-two billion dollars of ten-year notes, and bids came in for more than twice that. Of what the bidders won, about three quarters went to indirect bidders, who bid through a dealer. That group includes foreign central banks. Direct bidders took about fifteen percent. Big dealer banks took the rest.

> `tenyear` [source_located] 10-year note auction of Aug 12, 2026 (CUSIP 91282CRF0): offering $42.0bn; competitive tenders $106.2bn, accepted $41.8bn; bid-to-cover 2.53; high yield 4.683%; accepted: indirect bidders $32.1bn (76.7% of competitive accepted, our calc.), direct bidders $6.1bn (14.7%), primary dealers $3.6bn (8.6%). (sources: auct)  

## a1_bills - The IOU

## payoff - The bill

So who actually owns America's debt? Not China, at least not much of it. Foreign investors hold about a quarter, and Japan holds more than China. The Fed holds about a ninth. The government owes almost a fifth to itself, mostly to future retirees. And most of the rest belongs to Americans, through money funds, banks, pensions and their own savings. The biggest lender to the United States is the United States. So every dollar of interest is two things at once. A cost for taxpayers, and income for whoever holds the IOU.
