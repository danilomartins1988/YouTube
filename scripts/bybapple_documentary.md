## m1_machine - The machine

In fiscal twenty twenty-six, Apple sold about four hundred and seventy-nine billion dollars of stuff, up fifteen percent, after just six percent the year before. ## m1_services - The machine

Then there's the pipe you can't see. Services, meaning the App Store, cloud, advertising, payments and subscriptions, brought in about one hundred and twenty-three billion dollars, about a quarter of sales. But its gross margin was seventy-six percent, against thirty-nine percent for the hardware. So Services makes forty percent of Apple's gross profit. A quarter of the revenue, two fifths of the profit.

## m3_gauge - What's priced in

Now let's run it backwards. We ask what growth in free cash flow, for the next ten years, makes today's price work, then assume growth slows to three percent forever. With an eight percent discount rate, the answer is about ten and a half percent a year. With ten percent, it's about fifteen and a half percent. Our illustration, with the assumptions on screen.

## m5_courts - Risks and upside

Risk one is the courts. Apple earns licensing money from Google for search, and says that if the antitrust ruling against Google is reversed on appeal, remedies could block those payments. Apple doesn't say how much is at stake. The App Store fight with Epic is now headed to the Supreme Court. And the Justice Department's antitrust suit is still going.

> `risks` [source_located] 10-K risk factors: Google licensing: a reversal on appeal 'could result in the imposition of certain remedies initially proposed by the DOJ, such as those prohibiting Google from offering the Company commercial terms for search distribution', which 'could materially adversely affect' that revenue (no amount disclosed). U.S. App Store: court-ordered injunction on link-out commissions; Supreme Court granted review on June 30, 2026. EU DMA: a 2025 fine under appeal; in the open investigation the Commission 'may impose fines up to 10% of the Company's annual worldwide net sales' (a legal maximum; ≈ $47.9bn on FY2026 sales, our calc.). DOJ antitrust suit filed March 2024. Tariffs: 'can have a material adverse impact'; refunds may not repeat. AI: 'Failure to successfully develop, deploy or differentiate artificial intelligence capabilities ... could result in loss of market share'. (sources: tenk)

## m5_partners - Risks and upside

And there's a quieter concentration risk. Almost all of Apple's hardware is built by outsourcing partners, and just two suppliers account for forty-five percent and twenty-seven percent of the money vendors owe Apple. On the selling side, phone carriers make up a third of what customers owe Apple, and one single customer alone is thirteen percent. Apple doesn't name any of them. But if one big partner stumbles, on either end of the chain, it shows up in Apple's numbers.

> `conc` [source_located] 10-K: 'Substantially all of the Company's hardware products are manufactured by outsourcing partners'; two vendors accounted for 45% and 27% of vendor non-trade receivables; one customer accounted for 13% of total trade receivables; cellular network carriers accounted for 33% of trade receivables. Names not disclosed. (sources: tenk)  

## m5_upside - Risks and upside

And the upside the price might not fully include. Apple says its installed base of active devices hit a new all-time high, in every major category and region, though it gives no number. New cloud services will expand access to its A I features, with no pricing announced yet. And advertising is already one of the lines driving Services. None of that is sized, so we won't size it either.
