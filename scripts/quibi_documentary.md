# The $1.75 Billion Streaming App That Lasted Six Months

_Hollywood's biggest names raised one point seven five billion dollars for a streaming app built for ten-minute shows on your phone. Six months after launch, it was gone. What the money bought, why nobody stayed, and the lesson hidden in its failure._

## h_stakes - The billion-dollar bet

The app was called Quibi. It raised one point seven five billion dollars before it had a single customer. It launched in April twenty twenty and announced it was shutting down in October. And the strangest part is that the idea behind it was not crazy at all.

## m1_idea - Quick bites

The idea came from Jeffrey Katzenberg, the former chairman of Disney's film studio, with Meg Whitman, the former head of eBay and HP, as chief executive. The name meant quick bites. Hollywood quality shows, cut into episodes of ten minutes or less, made only for your phone. Something to watch on the bus, in a queue, or between meetings.

> `raised` [source_located] Fortune (Oct 2020): Quibi raised $1.75 billion to lavish on Hollywood-caliber shows that few people watched. Variety (Aug 2018): NewTV (later Quibi) closed a $1 billion first round; backers include Disney, 21st Century Fox, NBCUniversal, Sony Pictures, Viacom, WarnerMedia, Lionsgate, MGM, Alibaba, Goldman Sachs and JPMorgan Chase; episodes of 10 minutes each. (sources: fortune_shut, variety_2018, nbc_why)  
> `pandemic` [source_located] Next TV: Katzenberg's pitch was that mobile viewers would pay to watch shows in 10-minute chunks; the pandemic made people much less mobile, and Quibi was not compatible with large-screen TV sets. Fortune: Katzenberg first claimed the pandemic wrecked the launch; the farewell notice said other businesses faced the pandemic 'and have found their way through it. We were not able to do so.' NBC: apps for TV came only in October. (sources: nexttv_shut, fortune_shut, nbc_why)  

## m1_money - Quick bites

Hollywood loved it. In twenty eighteen, the first funding round alone raised one billion dollars. The investors read like a guest list of the entertainment industry. Disney, NBCUniversal, Sony, WarnerMedia, Viacom, Lionsgate, MGM, plus Alibaba and the banks Goldman Sachs and JPMorgan. And before launch, advertisers bought its entire first year of ads, worth one hundred and fifty million dollars.

> `raised` [source_located] Fortune (Oct 2020): Quibi raised $1.75 billion to lavish on Hollywood-caliber shows that few people watched. Variety (Aug 2018): NewTV (later Quibi) closed a $1 billion first round; backers include Disney, 21st Century Fox, NBCUniversal, Sony Pictures, Viacom, WarnerMedia, Lionsgate, MGM, Alibaba, Goldman Sachs and JPMorgan Chase; episodes of 10 minutes each. (sources: fortune_shut, variety_2018, nbc_why)

## m1_warning - Quick bites

There was a warning sign. The same summer Quibi raised its first billion, the phone company Verizon shut down its own short video service for phones, called Go Ninety. It had never found an audience, and Verizon wrote off six hundred and fifty-eight million dollars, mostly because of it. Katzenberg insisted his service would be fundamentally different, built from the ground up for watching on a phone.

> `go90` [source_located] Variety (Aug 2018): Verizon shut down Go90, its ad-supported short-form video service that never hit critical mass, the month before; for Q2 2018 Verizon took a $658 million writedown mainly related to Go90's demise. (sources: variety_2018)  

## m1_draw - Quick bites

Let me draw the bet. On one side, a huge number: billions of people with phones and a few spare minutes every day. On the other side, a simple price, a few dollars a month. If even a small slice of those people paid, the business would be enormous. That was the math. The question was whether people would actually pay for minutes they were already filling for free.

## m2_launch - The worst launch date

Quibi launched on April sixth, twenty twenty, just as the pandemic was locking people inside their homes. It offered a free trial of ninety days, then cost four ninety-nine a month with ads or seven ninety-nine without. On launch day, according to the analytics firm Sensor Tower, it was downloaded about about three hundred thousand times. That sounds like a lot. But Disney's new streaming service had seen about four million installs at its own launch.

> `launch` [source_located] CNN: Quibi launched on April 6 (2020). TechCrunch (7 Apr 2020): over 300,000 downloads on launch day (Sensor Tower preliminary), about 7.5% of the ≈ 4 million installs Disney+ saw at launch; 90-day free trial, then $4.99 a month with ads or $7.99 without; no AirPlay or Chromecast at launch. (sources: cnn_may, tc_launch)  

## m2_stars - The worst launch date

The launch line-up was full of famous names, from Jennifer Lopez to Liam Hemsworth, with projects promised from directors like Steven Spielberg and Guillermo del Toro. Apple promoted the app across its store, and it climbed to number three on the App Store. For a few days, Quibi looked like the hit Hollywood had paid for.

> `stars` [source_located] TechCrunch (7 Apr 2020): shows with Sophie Turner, Liam Hemsworth, Chance the Rapper, Jennifer Lopez and Chrissy Teigen at launch; promised content from Steven Spielberg, Guillermo del Toro, Lena Waithe and Catherine Hardwicke; heavily promoted by App Store editorial; reached No. 3 on the App Store. (sources: tc_launch)  

## m2_phone - The worst launch date

And Quibi had a bigger problem. It only worked on phones. People stuck at home wanted to watch on the big television in the living room, and at first Quibi could not be sent to a TV at all. Its showpiece technology, called Turnstyle, let you rotate your phone and switch between a horizontal and a vertical version of the same scene. Clever. But it was built for a commuter who no longer existed.

> `turnstyle` [source_located] The Verge (Mar 2020): Quibi's 'Turnstyle' switches between landscape and portrait video depending on how you hold your phone; Eko sued, alleging patent infringement and misappropriation of trade secrets; Quibi said the claims 'have absolutely no merit' and that it received its own patent. (sources: verge_eko, bbc_review)  
> `pandemic` [source_located] Next TV: Katzenberg's pitch was that mobile viewers would pay to watch shows in 10-minute chunks; the pandemic made people much less mobile, and Quibi was not compatible with large-screen TV sets. Fortune: Katzenberg first claimed the pandemic wrecked the launch; the farewell notice said other businesses faced the pandemic 'and have found their way through it. We were not able to do so.' NBC: apps for TV came only in October. (sources: nexttv_shut, fortune_shut, nbc_why)  

## m2_lawsuit - The worst launch date

Even that clever feature came with a fight. Before launch, an interactive video company called Eko sued Quibi, alleging that Turnstyle infringed its patent and used its trade secrets. Quibi said the claims had absolutely no merit, and pointed out that it held its own patent. A legal battle was the last thing a new app needed.

> `turnstyle` [source_located] The Verge (Mar 2020): Quibi's 'Turnstyle' switches between landscape and portrait video depending on how you hold your phone; Eko sued, alleging patent infringement and misappropriation of trade secrets; Quibi said the claims 'have absolutely no merit' and that it received its own patent. (sources: verge_eko, bbc_review)

## m3_may - The free trial

By May, CNN reported that Quibi had about about one point three million active users, most of them still on the free trial. Disney's service had more than fifty million customers. Netflix had a hundred and eighty-three million. Next to them, Quibi was a rounding error.

> `may` [source_located] CNN (12 May 2020): Quibi has roughly 1.3 million active users, compared with more than 50 million Disney+ customers and 183 million Netflix users; most of its customers are in the 90-day free trial. (sources: cnn_may)  

## m3_convert - The free trial

Then the trials ended. Sensor Tower estimated that of about about nine hundred and ten thousand people who signed up in the first few days, only about about seventy-two thousand kept paying after the free three months. That is about eight percent. Quibi said the estimate was wrong by an order of magnitude, and that five point six million people had downloaded the app. But it never published its number of paying subscribers.

> `convert` [source_located] The Verge citing Sensor Tower (Jul 2020): of about 910,000 users who signed up in the first few days, about 72,000 stayed after the three-month free trial (≈ 8%); Quibi said the figure was 'incorrect by an order of magnitude' and that over 5.6 million people had downloaded the app. (sources: verge_subs)  

## m3_ads - The free trial

The advertisers noticed too. They had paid for short ads of six, ten or fifteen seconds, with no skip button, expecting a young audience glued to their phones. Instead, the audience was smaller than promised, and according to Broadcasting and Cable, the big brands that had bought one hundred and fifty million dollars of advertising were disappointed.

> `adfmt` [source_located] TechCrunch (Oct 2019): advertisers were offered six, 10 or 15-second pre-roll spots with no skip button. Next TV (Oct 2020): fewer downloads and less usership than expected disappointed blue-chip marketers who had bought $150 million in advertising. (sources: tc_ads, nexttv_shut)  
> `ads` [source_located] TechCrunch (Oct 2019): Quibi sold out its $150 million first-year advertising inventory before launch; ads of six, 10 or 15 seconds with no skip button. (sources: tc_ads)  

## m3_draw - The free trial

Let me draw why a free trial can hide a problem. Imagine a funnel. At the top, everyone who downloads. In the middle, those who try a few shows. At the bottom, the few who pay when the free months end. A free trial makes the top look huge. But a business lives on the bottom. Quibi had a wide top and a very narrow bottom.

## m4_spend - The money

Mostly into shows. Quibi paid Hollywood prices for stars and directors, for episodes that were only a few minutes long. According to reports in June twenty twenty, the company expected to have spent one billion dollars of its money by the autumn, and that it would need another two hundred million dollars by mid twenty twenty-one to keep going.

> `burn` [source_located] NBC News (Oct 2020): it was reported in June that Quibi expected to have spent $1 billion of the $1.75 billion by the third quarter of 2020, and projected it would need to raise an additional $200 million by mid-2021 to stay viable. Recode (Sep 2020): Quibi said it had more than $200 million on hand while looking for a buyer, pitching its cash and its Turnstyle technology. Deadline (21 Oct 2020): around $350 million still in the bank and a staff of about 200 at the shutdown announcement. (sources: nbc_why, vox_sale, deadline_shut)

## m4_rights - The money

And there was a catch in the contracts. Quibi's deals with the creators gave it exclusive rights to each show for only two years. So it was spending fortunes on shows it would not keep to itself for long. When it came time to sell, a buyer would get a short window, not a library.

> `roku` [source_located] The Verge (8 Jan 2021): Roku acquired Quibi's library of more than 75 shows for the Roku Channel; the WSJ reported it paid 'significantly less' than $100 million; the shows must be presented in their original short form; Quibi content stays exclusive for the same two-year exclusivity window originally agreed with content creators, after which Roku keeps the right to show it until 2027. (sources: verge_roku)

## m5_shut - The end

On October twenty-first, twenty twenty, Quibi announced it was shutting down. Katzenberg said its standalone business model was no longer viable. The company still had around about three hundred and fifty million dollars in the bank, and about about two hundred employees. Instead of spending the rest, the founders chose to close and return as much money as they could to the investors.

## m5_quote - The end

Katzenberg explained it simply. Keeping Quibi going, he said, would not have changed the outcome. It would only have spent a whole lot more money without any value to show for it. The founders said that in the weeks before, they had looked at every option, partners, buyers and new funding. Every one of those stones, they said, was turned.

## payoff - The lesson

So what killed Quibi? Not one thing. The pandemic, a phone-only design, and shows nobody felt they had to see. But underneath was a bigger mistake. Quibi raised the money first and found out what people wanted later. It bet that money could buy an audience. In streaming, money buys shows. Only habits buy viewers. And short videos on phones did become a huge business. Just not one that people paid a subscription for.
