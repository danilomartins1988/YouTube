# Why America's Airports Make Money Before You Fly

_America's airports earned six point nine billion dollars from parking and ground transportation in a year, more than airlines paid in landing fees. How an airport makes money before your plane leaves the ground._

## h_open - Six point nine billion dollars

Six point nine billion dollars. That is how much America's airports earned in a single year from parking and ground transportation. More than passenger airlines paid them to land their planes.

> `parking` [source_located] FAA CATS Form 127, FY2025 (432 airports reporting): 'Parking and ground transportation' $6,886,188,801; 'Passenger airline landing fees' $5,537,643,339; 'Rental cars-excludes customer facility charges' $2,481,524,183; total operating revenue $34,515,928,236. FY2024 (440 airports): parking and ground transportation $6,548,575,803. Our calc: parking ≈ 20% of operating revenue, ≈ 1.24× landing fees. (sources: cats)

## m1_card - Who owns the airport




## m1_owners - Who owns the airport

Start with a surprise. Most big American airports are not companies. According to the Government Accountability Office, many are owned by states, counties, cities or public authorities. And when they take federal grants, they promise to run the airport to be as self-sustaining as possible, and to spend the airport's money on the airport.

> `owners` [source_located] GAO-15-306: 'many U.S. airports are owned by states, counties, cities, or public authorities'. FAA Sponsor Assurance 24: a fee and rental structure 'which will make the airport as self-sustaining as possible'; Assurance 25: 'All revenues generated by the airport ... will be expended by it for the capital or operating costs of the airport'. (sources: gao, assur)  

## m1_size - Who owns the airport

That is a big business. According to the Federal Aviation Administration's financial reports, the airports that have reported so far earned at least thirty-four point five billion dollars in operating revenue in twenty twenty-five. They also carried about one hundred and forty-one billion dollars of debt, money borrowed to build terminals, runways and garages. And they counted almost a billion passenger boardings, nine hundred and ninety-six million, in a single year.

## m1_transition - Who owns the airport

The first payment starts before you even arrive. It is hidden in the price of your ticket.


## m2_card - The ticket




## m2_pfc - The ticket

It is called the passenger facility charge. Airports can add up to four dollars and fifty cents for each flight segment you take from them, up to eighteen dollars on a round trip. The airport keeps it, to pay for projects like new gates and runways. The cap was last raised in two thousand, from three dollars.

> `pfc` [source_located] FAA: 'PFCs are capped at $4.50 per flight segment with a maximum of two PFCs charged on a one-way trip or four PFCs on a round trip, for a maximum of $18 total.' AIP History: 1990 Act allowed '$1, $2, or $3'; AIR-21 (2000) raised the maximum 'from $3 to $4 or $4.50'. Key PFC statistics (Oct 31, 2026): 'Actual collections from 1992 through 2025: $79.4 billion'; 2025 collections $3,845,069,634; 'All Large category airports are now collecting at maximum $4.50 level.' ACI-NA (industry): 'The $4.50 PFC set in year 2001 should be $10.05'; calls for a '$12 per enplaned passenger' cap; 'U.S. airports will require $173.9 billion in infrastructure investments over the next five years.' (sources: pfc, acina)  

## m2_total - The ticket

## m2_taxes - The ticket

And that is only one of the extras. There is a federal ticket tax of seven point five percent, a segment fee of five dollars and thirty cents per flight, and a security fee of five dollars and sixty cents per one-way trip. By our calculation, on a three hundred dollar round trip with a connection each way, all of these add up to about seventy-two dollars and ninety cents. Even on a nonstop round trip, it is about fifty-three dollars. Only the facility charge goes to the airport.

> `taxes` [source_located] FAA excise taxes (2026): 'Domestic passenger ticket tax • 7.5 percent'; 'Domestic flight segment tax ... $5.30 per passenger per segment'. TSA: 'The fee is currently $5.60 per one-way trip ... the fee imposed per round trip shall not exceed $11.20.' Our example: $300 round trip with one connection each way = $22.50 + $21.20 + $18.00 + $11.20 = $72.90 (≈ 24% on top); nonstop round trip ≈ $53.30; only the PFC goes to the airport. (sources: excise, tsa, pfc)  

## m2_fight - The ticket

For now, the cap set in two thousand still stands.

## payoff - The lesson

So why do America's airports make money before you fly? Because an airport is not just a runway. It is a landlord, a parking operator, a shopping mall and a toll collector, all at once. And every one of those businesses gets paid long before your wheels leave the ground. So next time you land, look around. The garage, the rental counter, the coffee shop, the screens on the wall. Each one is part of how the airport pays its bills, long before anyone checks a boarding pass.
