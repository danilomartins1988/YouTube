_The Supreme Court struck down tariffs that had raised an estimated one hundred and sixty-six billion dollars. The refunds are flowing, but not to the shoppers who paid higher prices. Follow the money from the checkout to customs and back._

## m1_case - The ruling

The tariffs were imposed under an emergency powers law known as IEEPA. Importers sued. On February twentieth, twenty twenty-six, the Supreme Court decided the case, Learning Resources versus Trump, by a vote of six to three.

> `ruling` [source_located] Learning Resources, Inc. v. ## m2_importer - Who really paid

On paper, the importer pays. That is how a tariff works. A company brings a shipment of toys or shoes into the country, and before customs releases it, the company pays the duty. The government never sends you a bill.

## m2_pass - Who really paid

But companies do not have to keep that cost. They can raise their prices. Economists measure this with a number called pass-through. Researchers at the Yale Budget Lab estimate that for everyday core goods, between forty-six and eighty-six percent of the tariffs showed up in consumer prices, depending on how you measure it.

> `paid` [source_located] ESTIMATES. Yale Budget Lab: 'Implied passthrough of tariffs to imported consumer goods prices ranges from roughly 46–86% for core goods'. New York Fed (Liberty Street, Oct 2026): 'nearly 90 percent of the 2025 tariffs were passed through to U.S. import prices'; consumer prices respond more slowly. (sources: yale, nyfed)  

## m2_nyfed - Who really paid

## payoff - The lesson

So who actually gets the tariff refund? The companies that paid at the border. Not because anyone planned it that way, but because a tariff is legally a tax on the importer, even when the shopper ends up paying much of it. The money left through the port, and it is coming back through the same door. What each company does with it next is the part you can still watch. So the next time a price tag rises because of a tariff, remember where that money really travels. It leaves through your receipt, and if it ever comes back, it comes back to whoever paid at the port.
