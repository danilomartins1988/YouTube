## m2_tech - The car itself

Modern cars are also harder to fix. Cameras and radar sit in bumpers, mirrors and windshields. The Highway Loss Data Institute, which is funded by insurers, found that for otherwise identical cars, those with driver assistance features cost about ten percent more per collision claim. The sensors are expensive, and often need calibration after a repair.

> `tech` [source_located] IIHS-HLDI (Aug 2026): 'collision claim severity was about 10% higher for the equipped vehicles'; 'The sensors are expensive and often require calibration after replacement'; claim frequency 'about 10% lower for collision claims and almost 40% lower for PDL claims'. IIHS and HLDI are 'wholly supported by ... auto insurers and insurance associations.' (sources: iihs)  

## m2_safer - The car itself

But there is a twist. The same technology prevents crashes. Cars with those features had about ten percent fewer collision claims, and almost forty percent fewer claims for damage to other people's property. A more expensive repair, but fewer of them.

## m3_deaths - The crashes

In twenty twenty, roads emptied during lockdowns, but the crashes that did happen were deadlier. According to federal crash data compiled by the Insurance Institute for Highway Safety, road deaths rose from about thirty-six thousand in twenty nineteen to forty-three thousand two hundred and thirty in twenty twenty-one. The death rate per mile driven jumped from one point one one to one point three eight.

## m4_legal - The courts and the states

The insurance commissioners' data show the price of legal services rising about twenty-one percent from twenty nineteen to twenty twenty-three, more than double the rise in medical care. The insurance industry blames what it calls legal system abuse. Its own study claims that, together with inflation, it added tens of billions of dollars to personal auto losses over ten years. That is the industry's own framing, not a neutral measurement.

## m4_florida - The courts and the states

Florida, the most expensive state, changed its laws in March twenty twenty-three. It repealed one-way attorney fees and cut the time to file a negligence lawsuit from four years to two years. Three years later, the state says loss ratios for car damage fell from one hundred and twelve percent in twenty twenty-two to under fifty percent in twenty twenty-five, and its biggest insurers are signaling rate cuts of about eight percent for twenty twenty-six.

## m5_loss - The insurers' books

At first, they were losing. Insurers measure their business with the combined ratio, losses plus expenses as a share of premiums. Above one hundred means losing money on the policies. Allstate's auto combined ratio was one hundred and ten in twenty twenty-two. The company reported a net loss of one point three nine billion dollars that year.

> `insurers` [source_located] Allstate 10-K: auto combined ratio 110.1 (2022), 103.4 (2023), 95.0 (2024), 85.0 (2025); 'net loss of $1.39 billion in 2022'; 'net income applicable to common shareholders was $10.17 billion in 2025 ... primarily due to higher underwriting income and gains on dispositions'; 'Rate increases of 17.9% were taken for Allstate brand in 55 locations' (2023). ## payoff - The lesson

So why did your car insurance get so expensive? More expensive cars and repairs, deadlier crashes, rising legal and medical costs, and insurers who raised prices fast to make up for their losses. Prices are finally easing. But they remain far above where they were before the pandemic, and the next turn of the cycle is never far away.
