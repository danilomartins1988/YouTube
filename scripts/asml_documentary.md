# The Machine Only One Company on Earth Can Build

_Every advanced chip in the world is printed by a machine that only one company can build: ASML, from a small town in the Netherlands. The film explains what the machine does, how one company ended up alone, who builds it with them, how it makes money, and why governments now fight over where it can go._

## m1_shrink - What the machine does

The catch: you can't print details much smaller than the light you use. And smaller details mean more transistors on every chip, which means faster, cheaper and more efficient computers. So to keep shrinking chips, the industry needed light with a much shorter wavelength than ever before.

## m1_light - What the machine does

ASML's answer is extreme ultraviolet light, with a wavelength of just 13.5 nanometers. Nothing on Earth makes it naturally. So the machine makes its own. It fires tiny drops of molten tin, up to 50,000 of them every second. A laser flattens each drop, and a second, far more powerful pulse, hotter than the surface of the sun, turns it into a plasma that gives off the light. A special mirror collects that light and sends it into the rest of the machine. All of this happens again and again, every second, for hours, without stopping.

> `droplets` [source_located] ASML: molten tin droplets are flattened by a low-intensity laser pulse, then vaporised by a more powerful pulse (hotter than the surface of the sun) into a plasma that emits 13.5 nm light; the process repeats up to 50,000 times per second. (sources: asml_hot, ieee_euv, bits_1000w)  
> "Hotter than the surface of the sun" follows ASML's own description of the tin plasma (asml_hot).  

## m1_mirrors - What the machine does

But this light is absorbed by almost everything, even air. So the whole path runs in a vacuum, and instead of lenses, it bounces off a chain of ultra smooth mirrors before it reaches the wafer.

> `zeiss` [source_located] Carl Zeiss SMT is ASML's sole supplier of lenses, mirrors, illuminators, collectors and other critical optics (exclusive arrangement); ASML holds a 24.9% interest (agreed November 2016); total purchases from Zeiss SMT were €4,406.9m in 2025 (about $5.0bn at 1.13). (sources: asml_ar_2025, zeiss_2016)

## m2_origin - How one company ended up alone

The story starts in 1984, when the electronics giant Philips and a chip equipment maker called A S M International created a small new company near Eindhoven. A year later it moved to Veldhoven. Its early years were rough: a small, unknown newcomer in a market full of bigger, established competitors. In 1995, it became a fully independent company, listed in Amsterdam and New York, and it kept pouring money into one idea: printing with ever shorter light.

> `founded_1984` [source_located] ASML was founded in 1984 as a joint venture of Philips and ASM International, and has been based in Veldhoven, the Netherlands. (sources: asml_history)  
> `ipo_1995` [source_located] In 1995 ASML became a fully independent public company, listed on the Amsterdam and New York stock exchanges. (sources: asml_history)  
> Early struggles and Japanese competition per ASML's own history page.  

## m2_bet - How one company ended up alone

The conflict came with extreme ultraviolet light. Making it work took decades and billions, and nobody was sure it ever would. So in twenty twelve, ASML did something unusual. Its own biggest customers, Intel, TSMC and Samsung, bought 23 percent of the company and helped pay for the research.

> `ccip_2012` [source_located] In 2012 Intel, TSMC and Samsung bought minority stakes totalling 23% of ASML and committed €1.38 billion of R&D funding for EUV and 450mm lithography. (sources: ccip_2012, samsung_ccip)  

## m2_insurance - How one company ended up alone

For them, it was insurance. If the machine never arrived, their own roadmaps would stall. If it did, they would be first in line.

> `ccip_2012` [source_located] In 2012 Intel, TSMC and Samsung bought minority stakes totalling 23% of ASML and committed €1.38 billion of R&D funding for EUV and 450mm lithography. (sources: ccip_2012, samsung_ccip)

## m2_pieces - How one company ended up alone

Then ASML went after the missing pieces. It moved to buy Cymer, the American maker of its light source, in a deal worth about 2.55 billion dollars. Later it took almost 25 percent of Carl Zeiss's chip optics business, the German company that makes its mirrors. In 2020, extreme ultraviolet machines finally went into high volume production.

> `cymer_2012` [source_located] In October 2012 ASML offered to buy Cymer, the US maker of lithography light sources, for around $2.55 billion in cash and shares (AP). (sources: cymer_2012)  
> `zeiss` [source_located] Carl Zeiss SMT is ASML's sole supplier of lenses, mirrors, illuminators, collectors and other critical optics (exclusive arrangement); ASML holds a 24.9% interest (agreed November 2016); total purchases from Zeiss SMT were €4,406.9m in 2025 (about $5.0bn at 1.13). (sources: asml_ar_2025, zeiss_2016)  
> `euv_2020` [source_located] At the beginning of 2020 EUV moved into high-volume manufacturing and ASML shipped its 100th EUV system. (sources: asml_history)  

## m2_discovery - How one company ended up alone

In the end, ASML didn't win alone. It won by tying its customers and its suppliers to the same bet. Today it is the only company in the world that makes extreme ultraviolet machines, and it has become Europe's most valuable company.

> `market_90` [source_located] ASML holds a monopoly on EUV machines and has become Europe's most valuable company (Reuters, January 2026). (sources: reuters_explainer)

## m3_map - Who builds it

Now follow the parts on a map. The mirrors come from Zeiss, in southern Germany. ASML bought about 5 billion dollars of parts from Zeiss in twenty twenty-five alone, and Zeiss is its only supplier for them. In fact, Zeiss's sites in Oberkochen and Wetzlar are the only places where these optics can be built, so their capacity limits how many machines ASML can ship. The light source is developed by Cymer, in San Diego. In total, ASML works with about 5,100 suppliers, and employs more than 44,000 people. Everything comes together in Veldhoven.

> `zeiss` [source_located] Carl Zeiss SMT is ASML's sole supplier of lenses, mirrors, illuminators, collectors and other critical optics (exclusive arrangement); ASML holds a 24.9% interest (agreed November 2016); total purchases from Zeiss SMT were €4,406.9m in 2025 (about $5.0bn at 1.13). (sources: asml_ar_2025, zeiss_2016)  
> `zeiss_sites` [source_located] Zeiss's Oberkochen and Wetzlar sites are the only places where the optical columns for ASML's EUV machines are built, which caps how many scanners ASML can produce; Zeiss is expanding Oberkochen. (sources: asml_ar_2025)  
> `cymer_2012` [source_located] In October 2012 ASML offered to buy Cymer, the US maker of lithography light sources, for around $2.55 billion in cash and shares (AP). (sources: cymer_2012)

## m4_sales - How it makes money

Let me draw the numbers. In twenty twenty-five, ASML sold about 37 billion dollars worth of machines and services, up 16 percent in a year. It kept almost 11 billion dollars as profit. Extreme ultraviolet was the star: 48 machines, bringing in about 13 billion dollars.

> `sales_2025` [source_located] ASML's 2025 total net sales were €32.7 billion (about $37.0bn at 1.13), up from €28.3 billion in 2024 (+16%); net income €9.6bn (about $10.8bn). (sources: asml_q4_2025, asml_q4_2024, ecb_2025_avg)  
> `euv_sales_2025` [source_located] In 2025 ASML recognised revenue on 48 EUV systems; EUV system sales were €11.6 billion (about $13.1bn at 1.13). (sources: asml_q4_2025_deck, ecb_2025_avg)

## m4_customers - How it makes money

And who buys them? In twenty twenty-five, customers in China made up 29 percent of ASML's sales, none of it the newest machines, because China cannot buy them. A year earlier it was 36 percent. Then come Taiwan, South Korea and the United States, home to the chipmakers we met in the last episode.

> `regions` [source_located] ASML 2025 total net sales by customer location (20-F): China €9,519.7m (29.1%), Taiwan €8,337.9m (25.5%), South Korea €8,159.6m (25.0%), United States €4,089.1m (12.5%), Japan €1,420.9m (4.3%) of €32,667.3m; China was 36.1% in 2024. (sources: asml_ar_2025)  
> `export_euv` [source_located] ASML's export licence for an EUV machine for a Chinese customer expired unused in June 2019 and was not renewed; ASML has never shipped an EUV machine to China (CNBC, January 2024). (sources: reuters_2019_licence, cnbc_export)  

## m4_flywheel - How it makes money

The money flows in a loop. Chipmakers pay for machines. ASML spends about 5.3 billion dollars a year on research. That research makes the next machine, which chipmakers need to stay ahead, so they order again. At the end of twenty twenty-five, ASML already had almost 44 billion dollars of orders waiting.

> `rd_2025` [source_located] ASML spent €4.7 billion on R&D in 2025 (about $5.3bn at 1.13). (sources: asml_ar_2025, ecb_2025_avg)  
> `backlog` [source_located] ASML's order backlog at the end of 2025 was €38.8 billion (about $43.8bn at 1.13). (sources: asml_q4_2025, ecb_2025_avg)

## m5_map - Where the machine may go

Extreme ultraviolet machines flow to Taiwan, South Korea and the United States. But not to China. The Dutch government has not let ASML send them there since 2019, and it has never shipped one. In 2023, the Dutch government also began restricting some of the older machines, and extended the rules a year later.

> `export_euv` [source_located] ASML's export licence for an EUV machine for a Chinese customer expired unused in June 2019 and was not renewed; ASML has never shipped an EUV machine to China (CNBC, January 2024). (sources: reuters_2019_licence, cnbc_export)  
> `export_duv` [source_located] Dutch export controls on the most advanced DUV machines took effect in September 2023 and were extended to two more DUV immersion systems from 7 September 2024. (sources: ioplus_2024, dutch_2024)  

## m5_gate - Where the machine may go

Let me draw the gate. On one side, the supply network in Europe and the United States. On the other, the buyers. Each export license is a gate. Open it, and a country gets the tools for the most advanced chips. Close it, and it has to try to build its own, which could take many years.

## m5_cost - Where the machine may go

The gate has a price for ASML too. The company expects China to fall to about 20 percent of its sales in twenty twenty-six. And China is now racing to build its own lithography machines, while the next generation of ASML's machines is estimated to cost 300 to 400 million dollars. The first of those went to Intel, at the end of twenty twenty-three. So the gap between those who have the machine and those who don't may keep growing.

> `china_2026` [source_located] ASML expects revenue from China to fall to about 20% of total sales in 2026 (Caixin, January 2026). (sources: caixin_china)  
> `highna_price` [source_located] High-NA EUV scanners are estimated to cost about $300-400 million each; ASML began shipping the first one to Intel in December 2023. (sources: highna_2023, highna_reuters)  
> `highna_intel` [source_located] ASML began shipping the first High-NA EUV system to Intel in December 2023 (pilot EXE:5000). (sources: highna_reuters)  
> `regions` [source_located] ASML 2025 total net sales by customer location (20-F): China €9,519.7m (29.1%), Taiwan €8,337.9m (25.5%), South Korea €8,159.6m (25.0%), United States €4,089.1m (12.5%), Japan €1,420.9m (4.3%) of €32,667.3m; China was 36.1% in 2024. (sources: asml_ar_2025)

## payoff - The lesson

So, the machine only one company can build is not a single invention. It is forty years of bets, a network of suppliers no one else can copy, and customers who paid to make it real. That is why a small town in the Netherlands holds one of the most important keys to the future of computing. And the smallest details on the most powerful chips in the world all begin as a drop of tin, hit by a laser, inside one Dutch machine.
