## m1_scale - The network

Start with the job. The Postal Service delivers to more than one hundred and seventy million addresses, six and often seven days a week. In twenty twenty-five alone, about one point eight million new addresses were added to its routes. Every new house, every new apartment, is one more stop.

## m1_tax - The network

And here is the part many people get wrong. The Postal Service generally receives no tax dollars for its operating expenses. It is supposed to pay its own way, by selling stamps, delivering packages and charging for mail.

## m1_law - The network

## payoff - The lesson

So why does the Post Office keep losing billions? Because it is asked to run like a business and serve like a public utility at the same time. It must reach every address, six days a week, while its most profitable product disappears and old promises to retirees come due. Until Congress decides who pays for that promise, the losses are the bill. Every stamp, every package and every new address on a route is part of that same calculation. And for now, the bill keeps arriving, six days a week.
