# How Costco Makes Money Selling Things Cheap

_Costco collected five point nine billion dollars in membership fees in a year, an amount equal to about half of its operating profit. How a store that sells almost at cost became one of the strongest businesses in America._

## m1_sales - The thin margin

Start with the size of it. In its financial year that ended in August twenty twenty-six, Costco sold two hundred and ninety-seven billion dollars worth of goods, up ten point one percent from two hundred and sixty-nine point nine billion the year before. It runs nine hundred and thirty-nine warehouses around the world, six hundred and forty-seven of them in the United States and Puerto Rico. By sales, it is one of the biggest retailers on the planet.

## m1_margin - The thin margin

But look at what it keeps. After paying for the goods themselves, Costco's gross margin was about eleven point one percent of sales. Put another way, by our calculation, the average item was sold for only about twelve and a half percent more than it cost Costco to buy. Costco itself says it operates at significantly lower margins than most other retailers.

## m2_members - The membership machine

To shop at Costco, you need a membership. At the end of its last financial year, Costco had about eighty-four million paid members, and about one hundred and fifty million cardholders once family and business cards are counted.

## m2_price - The membership machine

In September twenty twenty-four, Costco raised its basic fee in the United States and Canada from sixty to sixty-five dollars a year. The Executive membership went from one hundred and twenty to one hundred and thirty. The increase touched around fifty-two million memberships. Costco says the higher fee accounted for about thirty percent of the growth in its membership income in twenty twenty-six. The rest came from new members signing up and from members upgrading to the more expensive card.

## m2_renew - The membership machine

Look at the balance sheet. At the end of August twenty twenty-six, Costco held about nineteen point three billion dollars of goods in its warehouses. At the same moment, it owed its suppliers twenty-two point six billion dollars. It owed more than the value of everything on its shelves.

## m4_turns - Selling before paying

How is that possible? Speed. By our calculation, Costco's entire inventory sells through about fourteen times a year. A typical item sits in the warehouse for less than a month. If the supplier gives Costco about a month to pay, the cash from the customer often arrives first. A slower store, where goods sit on the shelf for months, would have to find that money somewhere else.

## m5_exec - The loyalty loop

Executive members pay double the basic fee. In return, they get back about two percent of what they spend, up to an annual limit. Costco had forty-two point three million of them last year. And they accounted for about seventy-five percent of Costco's sales worldwide. Two years earlier, there were thirty-five point four million. Every year, more members choose to pay the higher fee.

> `exec` [source_located] Costco 10-K FY2026: 'Executive members represented 42,300, 38,700, and 35,400 of total paid members in 2026, 2025, and 2024. 'These members earn an Executive reward, generally 2% on qualified purchases, subject to an annual maximum value'. (sources: tenk, fee)

## payoff - The lesson

So how does Costco make money selling things cheap? It does not really try to profit on the goods. It sells access. The cheap prices are the product that keeps millions of people paying, every single year, for the privilege of shopping there. The store is the bait. The membership is the business. And for members, the deal works only as long as the savings are bigger than the fee. That is the promise Costco has to keep, every single year, to stay on top.
