## m1_launch - The fee

It began in February two thousand and five. Amazon introduced its first ever membership program. For a flat fee of seventy-nine dollars a year, members got unlimited two-day shipping for free, with no minimum purchase. Jeff Bezos called it all-you-can-eat express shipping, and admitted it was expensive for the company in the short term.

> `price` [source_located] Amazon (Feb 2, 2005): 'For a flat membership fee of $79 per year, members get unlimited, express two-day shipping for free'; Bezos: 'Though expensive for the Company in the short-term'. Amazon (Feb 2015): 'annual membership program for $99 a year.' Amazon (Feb 3, 2022): 'the annual membership from $119 to $139' and monthly '$12.99 to $14.99'; 'the first time Amazon has raised the price of Prime since 2018'. Amazon (Mar 2026): 'Prime membership is $14.99 a month/$139 a year.' (sources: launch, prices)  

## m1_prices - The fee

The price crept up. By twenty fifteen, Amazon described Prime as a ninety-nine dollar annual program. In twenty eighteen, it went to one hundred and nineteen. In February twenty twenty-two, Amazon raised it again, to one hundred and thirty-nine dollars a year, or fourteen ninety-nine a month. That is still the price today.

## m1_members - The fee

## m1_revenue - The fee

That line includes Prime, but also music, audiobooks, e-books and video. Amazon does not report Prime fees on their own. In the third quarter of twenty twenty-six alone, subscriptions brought in about fourteen billion dollars, up about eleven percent on a year earlier, by our calculation.

## payoff - The lesson

So what is the hidden economics of Amazon Prime? Prime is not a product that has to make money on its own. It is the glue. It keeps hundreds of millions of people shopping, watching and clicking in one place. Amazon may spend more on delivery than Prime fees bring in. But what it buys with that money is something far more valuable. Your habit.
