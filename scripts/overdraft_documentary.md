# Why Banks Still Make Billions From Overdrafts

_A twenty-four dollar card purchase can cost a thirty-five dollar fee. Banks cut overdraft fees in half, a federal cap was written and then erased, and the fees still bring in billions. Follow the money behind the overdraft._

## h_open - Thirty-five dollars

Thirty-five dollars. That is the typical overdraft fee at America's largest banks, according to the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau. You can pay it for spending just a few dollars more than you have.

> `fee` [source_located] CFPB (Dec 2023): median overdraft fee $35 among the 119 institutions over $10bn that charge one; median NSF fee $32. CFPB 2014 Data Point: median debit card transaction that led to an overdraft $24. Wells Fargo: $35 per item, up to 3 a day. (sources: cfpbfee, banks)  

## h_twist - Thirty-five dollars

## m3_optin - The retreat

The first crack came years earlier. In twenty ten, a Federal Reserve rule took effect. Banks could no longer charge overdraft fees on ATM withdrawals or one-time debit card purchases unless the customer had opted in. Customers had to say yes first. It was the first time the default had changed.

## m4_rule - The rule that vanished

In December twenty twenty-four, the bureau issued a final rule for banks and credit unions with more than ten billion dollars in assets. They would have three choices. Charge an overdraft fee of no more than five dollars. Charge a fee that only covered their own costs and losses. Or treat the overdraft as a loan, under the federal lending law, with the disclosures that come with credit.

> `rule` [source_located] CFPB final rule (Dec 12, 2024): institutions over $10bn; options: $5 fee, a fee that covers costs and losses, or treat overdraft as a loan under TILA; 'up to $5 billion in annual overdraft fee savings', $225 per overdrafting household; effective Oct 1, 2025. Wells Fargo 2024 Annual Report: 'we would expect a significant reduction to our fees for overdraft services'. (sources: rule)  

## m4_save - The rule that vanished

The bureau projected that the rule could save consumers up to five billion dollars a year, or about two hundred and twenty-five dollars for each household that pays overdraft fees. It was due to take effect in October twenty twenty-five. Wells Fargo warned its investors that it would expect a significant reduction in its overdraft fees.

> `rule` [source_located] CFPB final rule (Dec 12, 2024): institutions over $10bn; options: $5 fee, a fee that covers costs and losses, or treat overdraft as a loan under TILA; 'up to $5 billion in annual overdraft fee savings', $225 per overdrafting household; effective Oct 1, 2025. Wells Fargo 2024 Annual Report: 'we would expect a significant reduction to our fees for overdraft services'. (sources: rule)  

## m4_repeal - The rule that vanished

It never took effect. In twenty twenty-five, Congress used a law called the Congressional Review Act to cancel it. The Senate voted in March. The House voted two hundred and seventeen to two hundred and eleven in April.

> `repeal` [source_located] Public Law 119-10 (S.J.Res. 18), approved May 9, 2025: the rule (89 FR 106768) 'shall have no force or effect'. House passed 217-211 on Apr 9, 2025 (Roll Call 96). Senate passed it in March 2025 (CRS). (sources: repeal)  

## m4_signed - The rule that vanished

## m5_why - Still billions

Why does the fee survive? Look at how it is built. It is a flat fee. At a bank like Wells Fargo, it is the same thirty-five dollars whether you overdraw by two dollars or by two hundred. On a small purchase, covered for a few days until the next deposit, that is a very wide margin. And without a federal cap, each bank still decides for itself what to charge.

## payoff - The lesson

So why do banks still make billions from overdrafts? Because the fee is small for most people and huge for a few. It is charged on tiny purchases, paid by customers who live close to zero, and repeated month after month. Banks cut it in half when the pressure was high. The cap that would have cut it again was erased. If you want to keep your money out of that machine, the simplest move is to check whether you opted in.
