# Could AI Bring Europe Back to Power?

_Theo, an engineer in an aging European factory town, keeps hearing that Europe is falling behind. Following him and his sister Nora, the film tests a hypothesis: if AI and robots make labor matter less, owning the technology matters more, and Europe owns a lot of it._

## co_hook - A continent in a squeeze

Meet Theo. He's an engineer in an old factory town in Europe, and everyone keeps telling him the same thing. Europe is falling behind. America has the tech giants. China has the factories. Europe has aging towns, pricey energy and a lot of nostalgia. But Theo's town still makes parts for machines the whole world needs, and his sister Nora works on medicines sold around the world. So here's the question Theo is about to chase. What if the technology that seems to threaten Europe could bring it back?

## a1_gap - A continent in a squeeze

Slipping how? A report for the European Commission, led by the former central banker Mario Draghi, found the gap between Europe and America has been widening for years. And most of it comes down to a single thing. European workers simply produce less in the same amount of time.

> `draghi_gap` [source_located] Draghi report (Sep 2024): EU-US gap in the level of GDP at 2015 prices widened from slightly more than 15% in 2002 to 30% in 2023; around 70% of the gap in per-capita GDP at PPP is explained by lower productivity in the EU. (sources: draghi_a)  

## a1_age - A continent in a squeeze

Theo sees it on his own shop floor. The veterans are retiring, and fewer young people walk in. Draghi's report warns that Europe will soon have just two workers for every retiree.

> `workforce` [source_located] Draghi report (Sep 2024): by 2040 the EU's workforce is projected to shrink by close to 2 million workers each year; ratio of working to retired people expected to fall from around 3:1 to 2:1. (sources: draghi_a)  

## a1_squeeze - A continent in a squeeze

Then there's the power bill. European companies pay two to three times what American companies pay for electricity. And when Europe grows a promising tech startup, it often packs up and moves to America to raise money. Theo's boss has a word for all of this. Squeezed.

> `energy` [source_located] Draghi report (Sep 2024): EU companies face electricity prices 2-3 times those in the US and natural gas prices 4-5 times higher. (sources: draghi_a)

## a5_asml - The machines behind the machines

> `asml` [source_located] ASML (Veldhoven, NL), 2025: total net sales EUR 32.7bn ≈ $37.0bn; net income EUR 9.6bn ≈ $10.8bn; R&D EUR 4.7bn ≈ $5.3bn (our conversion); about 5,100 suppliers; the only maker of EUV lithography systems. (sources: asml_fy25, asml_ar25, ecb_2025_avg)  

## a5_web - The machines behind the machines

## a6_energy - Owning vs. operating

Then Theo's boss throws in a twist. If robots make labor matter less, other costs matter more. Energy. Money. Logistics. Stability. And the taxes on producing. Brazil, for example, gets most of its electricity from renewables. Yet an industry study found that almost half of what its power companies bill goes to taxes and charges, and the country is now overhauling its sales taxes. So a country with cheap power would still need competitive costs to produce. And even if Company E's partner plant ends up in Brazil, the profit from the technology could still come home to Europe.

> `br_power` [source_located] EPE Brazilian Energy Balance 2026: renewables were 86.8% of Brazil's electricity matrix in 2025 (88.2% in 2024). (sources: epe_ben26)  
> `br_charges` [source_located] PwC / Instituto Acende Brasil: in 2024 taxes (30%) and sector charges (14.8%) were 44.8% of the gross operating revenue of a sample of 44 Brazilian power companies (about two-thirds of the market). (sources: pwc_acende25)  
> `br_vat` [source_located] Brazil's Constitutional Amendment 132/2023 creates a dual VAT (CBS and IBS) replacing five consumption taxes, tested from 2026 with the transition running to 2033 (Agencia Senado). (sources: br_reform)
