# How Car Dealerships Really Make Money

_One of America's biggest dealer groups made more gross profit per car in its finance office than on the new car itself. Where a car dealership really makes its money._

## h_open - Two thousand five hundred dollars

Two thousand five hundred and sixty-four dollars. That is the average gross profit AutoNation, one of America's largest dealership groups, made on each new car it sold in twenty twenty-five. On a car that sold for about fifty-two thousand dollars.

## m2_card - The finance office




## m2_how - The finance office

Most people do not pay cash for a car. They take a loan, often arranged right there at the dealership. The dealer does not lend the money itself. It places the loan with a bank or finance company, and earns a commission for it. It also sells extended warranties, service contracts and other protection products, again on commission.

> `fi` [source_located] CFPB Bulletin 2013-02: 'the lender may pay the dealer what is typically referred to as "reserve" (or "participation"), compensation based upon the difference in interest revenues between the buy rate and the actual note rate charged to the consumer' (bulletin disapproved by Congress in May 2018). Ask CFPB: 'interest rates through dealers are generally higher because their rates often add additional interest to compensate them for handling your financing. Regardless of the source, interest rates are negotiable.' GAP: 'one of several optional add-on products ... The cost of the product will be rolled into the loan amount.' AutoNation 10-K: F&I revenue 'represents commissions earned by us for the placement of ... loans and leases ... vehicle service contracts ... and other vehicle protection products'. (sources: cfpb13, cfpbask, an10k)  

## m2_markup - The finance office

Here is one way the commission can work. The lender sets a rate, called the buy rate. The dealer can offer you a higher rate, and the lender shares the difference with the dealer. That was described in a twenty thirteen bulletin from the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau, which Congress later overturned. The Bureau's own advice is simple. Rates through dealers are often higher, and interest rates are negotiable.

## m2_cars - The finance office

Regulators tried to step in. In December twenty twenty-three, the Federal Trade Commission adopted the CARS Rule, banning charges for add-ons that give the buyer no benefit, and requiring dealers to get the buyer's express, informed consent for every charge. It expected to save consumers more than three point four billion dollars a year. The dealers' association sued. In January twenty twenty-five, a federal appeals court vacated the rule.

> `cars` [source_located] FTC (Dec 12, 2023): 'The rule prohibits dealers from charging for any add-on that does not provide a benefit to consumers.' 'expected to save consumers nationwide more than $3.4 billion'; 'The rule requires dealers to get consumers' express, informed consent for any charges that they pay'. Fifth Circuit (Jan 27, 2025): 'we GRANT the petition for review and VACATE the CARS Rule.' (sources: ftc, ca5)

## m4_card - The dealer's shield




## m4_law - The dealer's shield

In many states, franchise laws stand between the carmaker and the buyer. Michigan's law, for example, says a manufacturer may not sell a new car directly to a retail customer other than through franchised dealers.

> `law` [source_located] Michigan MCL 445.1574(1)(i): a manufacturer shall not 'Sell any new motor vehicle directly to a retail customer other than through franchised dealers' (amended 2014). FTC staff (2015): 'Blanket prohibitions on direct manufacturer sales to consumers are an anomaly within the larger economy.' AutoNation 10-K: franchise laws make it 'unlawful for a manufacturer to terminate or not renew a franchise unless "good cause" exists'; dealers are 'restricted by various state franchise laws from relocating stores or establishing new stores ... within any area that is served by another dealer of the same vehicle brand'; 'these franchise laws, which were designed to protect dealers'. (sources: mich, ftc15, an10k)  

## m4_protect - The dealer's shield

These laws do more. A carmaker cannot simply end a dealer's franchise unless there is good cause. And a new dealer of the same brand often cannot open nearby. As AutoNation's annual report puts it, these franchise laws were designed to protect dealers.

## payoff - The lesson

So how do car dealerships really make money? Not mainly on the car you drive away in. On the loan you sign for it, the warranty you add to it, and the service bay you return to. Knowing that changes how you buy. Negotiate the price, but also the rate, the add-ons and the term. That is where the dealer's money really is.

> `mix` [source_located] AutoNation 10-K FY2025: 'new vehicle sales accounted for 49% of our total revenue and 13% of our total gross profit. Used vehicle sales accounted for 28% of our total revenue and 9% of our total gross profit. Our parts and service operations, while comprising 17% of our total revenue, contributed 48% of our total gross profit. Our finance and insurance sales, while comprising 5% of our total revenue, contributed 30% of our total gross profit.' Gross profit ($M): new 664.8; used 462.6; parts & service 2,355.1; F&I 1,464.4. Per vehicle retailed: new $2,564 (2024 $3,045; 2023 $4,342); used $1,555; F&I $2,769; average new-vehicle revenue $52,075. Q3 2026 10-Q: new $2,176, F&I $2,698 per vehicle; parts & service 51.1% of gross profit. (sources: an10k, an10q)  
> `fi` [source_located] CFPB Bulletin 2013-02: 'the lender may pay the dealer what is typically referred to as "reserve" (or "participation"), compensation based upon the difference in interest revenues between the buy rate and the actual note rate charged to the consumer' (bulletin disapproved by Congress in May 2018). Ask CFPB: 'interest rates through dealers are generally higher because their rates often add additional interest to compensate them for handling your financing. Regardless of the source, interest rates are negotiable.' GAP: 'one of several optional add-on products ... The cost of the product will be rolled into the loan amount.' AutoNation 10-K: F&I revenue 'represents commissions earned by us for the placement of ... loans and leases ... vehicle service contracts ... and other vehicle protection products'. (sources: cfpb13, cfpbask, an10k)
